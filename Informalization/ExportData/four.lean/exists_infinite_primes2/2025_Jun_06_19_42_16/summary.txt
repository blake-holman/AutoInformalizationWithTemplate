We will show that if \( p \) is the smallest prime factor of \( n! + 1 \), then \( p \geq n \). First, observe that \( n! + 1 \neq 1 \) since \( n! \) is always positive and thus \( n! + 1 > 1 \). Next, by definition, the smallest factor \( p \) of \( n! + 1 \) is prime because the smallest factor greater than 1 of any integer is prime. Suppose for the sake of contradiction that \( p < n \). Since \( p \) divides \( n! + 1 \) and \( p \leq n \), it follows that \( p \) divides \( n! \) as well, because \( n! \) contains all factors from 1 up to \( n \). Hence, \( p \) divides both \( n! \) and \( n! + 1 \), which implies that \( p \) divides their difference, 1. But no prime divides 1, yielding a contradiction. Therefore, it must be that \( p \geq n \). This completes the proof.
