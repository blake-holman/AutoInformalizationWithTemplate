[answer]
Assume that the conjunction of the propositions \( B \), \( C \), \( D \), \( A \), and \( E \) holds. From this assumption, we will prove that the conjunction of \( A \), \( B \), \( C \), \( D \), and \( E \) also holds.
[appliedTactic]
rw [and_comm,and_assoc,and_assoc,and_assoc ] 
[answer]
By repeatedly applying the commutativity and associativity of conjunction, the goal statement is rearranged from asserting \( A \) and \( B \) and \( C \) and \( D \) and \( E \) in that order to asserting \( B \) and \( C \) and \( D \) and \( E \) and \( A \), effectively reordering the components of the conjunction without changing their truth value.
[appliedTactic]
rw [← and_assoc,← and_assoc, ← and_assoc,and_right_comm,and_assoc,and_assoc,and_assoc ] 
[answer]
By applying the associative and commutative properties of conjunction repeatedly, the goal of proving that the conjunction of \(B\), \(C\), \(D\), \(E\), and \(A\) holds is transformed into proving the conjunction of \(B\), \(C\), \(D\), \(A\), and \(E\), which matches the given hypothesis.
[appliedTactic]
exact  k 
[answer]
The proof is completed by directly using the assumption that all five propositions \(B\), \(C\), \(D\), \(A\), and \(E\) hold simultaneously.
