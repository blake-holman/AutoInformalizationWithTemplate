We will show that if the conjunction of the propositions \(B\), \(C\), \(D\), \(A\), and \(E\) holds, then the conjunction of \(A\), \(B\), \(C\), \(D\), and \(E\) also holds. To do this, we begin by observing that conjunction is both commutative and associative, which allows us to reorder and regroup the components without altering the truth value. By applying these properties repeatedly, we rearrange the goal statement from \(A \wedge B \wedge C \wedge D \wedge E\) to the equivalent form \(B \wedge C \wedge D \wedge A \wedge E\). Since this matches the given assumption, the conclusion follows directly, thereby completing the proof.
