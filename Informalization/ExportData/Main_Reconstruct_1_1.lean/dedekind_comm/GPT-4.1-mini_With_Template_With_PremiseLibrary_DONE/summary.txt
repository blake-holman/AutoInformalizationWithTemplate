We will show that if the propositions \( B \), \( C \), \( D \), \( A \), and \( E \) all hold in that order, then the propositions \( A \), \( B \), \( C \), \( D \), and \( E \) also hold in that order. Starting from the assumption that \( B \), \( C \), \( D \), \( A \), and \( E \) all hold, we observe that by the commutativity and associativity of conjunction, proving \( A \wedge B \wedge C \wedge D \wedge E \) is equivalent to proving \( B \wedge C \wedge D \wedge E \wedge A \). Furthermore, applying these properties again allows us to rearrange this conjunction to \( B \wedge C \wedge D \wedge A \wedge E \). Since by assumption \( B \), \( C \), \( D \), \( A \), and \( E \) hold, the proof is complete.
