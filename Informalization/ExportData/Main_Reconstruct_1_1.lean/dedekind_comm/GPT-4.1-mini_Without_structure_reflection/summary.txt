We will show that \( A \), \( B \), \( C \), \( D \), and \( E \) all hold. Starting with the assumption that \( B \), \( C \), \( D \), \( A \), and \( E \) hold, we use the commutativity and associativity of conjunction to reorder the conjunction \( A \wedge B \wedge C \wedge D \wedge E \) into \( B \wedge C \wedge D \wedge E \wedge A \), and subsequently into \( B \wedge C \wedge D \wedge A \wedge E \). Since conjunction is associative and commutative, these rearrangements preserve logical equivalence. Therefore, the goal reduces to proving \( B \wedge C \wedge D \wedge A \wedge E \), which follows directly from the original assumptions, thus completing the proof.
