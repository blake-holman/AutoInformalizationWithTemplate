We will show that if the conjunction \( B \wedge C \wedge D \wedge A \wedge E \) holds, then the conjunction \( A \wedge B \wedge C \wedge D \wedge E \) also holds. Starting from the assumption \( B \wedge C \wedge D \wedge A \wedge E \), we use the commutativity and associativity of conjunction to reorder the terms in the goal. First, we rearrange \( A \wedge B \wedge C \wedge D \wedge E \) to \( B \wedge C \wedge D \wedge E \wedge A \), and then further reorder it to \( B \wedge C \wedge D \wedge A \wedge E \), matching exactly the order of the assumption. Since the goal now coincides with the given hypothesis, the proof is completed directly by the assumption that all five propositions hold simultaneously.
