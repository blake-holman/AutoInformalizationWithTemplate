Assume that the conjunction of the propositions \( B \), \( C \), \( D \), \( A \), and \( E \) holds. From this assumption, we will prove that the conjunction of \( A \), \( B \), \( C \), \( D \), and \( E \) also holds.
[appliedTactic]
rw [and_comm,and_assoc,and_assoc,and_assoc ] 
[answer]
By repeatedly applying the commutativity and associativity of conjunction, the goal is rearranged from asserting that \( A \), \( B \), \( C \), \( D \), and \( E \) all hold in the order \( A \wedge B \wedge C \wedge D \wedge E \) to an equivalent statement where the order is \( B \wedge C \wedge D \wedge E \wedge A \). This reordering aligns the goal’s structure more closely with the given hypothesis.

[answer]
The goal was to prove a conjunction of five propositions arranged in the order \( B \), \( C \), \( D \), \( E \), and \( A \). By repeatedly applying the associative and commutative properties of conjunction, the goal was transformed to match the exact order of the existing assumption, which is \( B \), \( C \), \( D \), \( A \), and \( E \). This rearrangement aligned the goal with the given hypothesis, simplifying the proof by making the goal identical to the known conjunction.
