We will show the equivalence involving the infimum of a set \( A \) and a particular element \( a_0 \). First, assume that the infimum of \( A \) is \( a_0 \). By definition, \( a_0 \) is the greatest lower bound of \( A \), so it is a lower bound of \( A \) itself. To prove that for every positive number \( \varepsilon \), there exists an element of \( A \) less than \( a_0 + \varepsilon \), suppose the contrary: that there is some \( \varepsilon > 0 \) such that every \( a \in A \) satisfies \( a \geq a_0 + \varepsilon \). This would imply \( a_0 + \varepsilon \) is a lower bound greater than \( a_0 \), contradicting the maximality of \( a_0 \). Thus, for each positive \( \varepsilon \), such an element must exist. 

Conversely, assume that \( a_0 \) is a lower bound of \( A \) and that for every \( \varepsilon > 0 \), there is an element \( a \in A \) with \( a < a_0 + \varepsilon \). We confirm \( a_0 \leq a \) for all \( a \in A \), ensuring \( a_0 \) is indeed a lower bound. Then, to establish \( a_0 \) as the greatest lower bound, suppose there exists another lower bound \( x \) with \( x > a_0 \). Using \( \varepsilon = x - a_0 > 0 \), the assumption guarantees an element \( a \in A \) such that \( a < a_0 + \varepsilon = x \). However, since \( x \) is a lower bound, all elements of \( A \) must satisfy \( a \geq x \), producing a contradiction. Hence, no lower bound can be greater than \( a_0 \), and so \( a_0 \) is the infimum of \( A \).
