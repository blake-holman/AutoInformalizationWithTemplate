We will show that the infimum of a set \( A \) equals \( a_0 \) if and only if \( a_0 \) is a lower bound of \( A \) and for every positive number \( \varepsilon \), there exists an element of \( A \) less than \( a_0 + \varepsilon \). First, assume that \( a_0 \) is the greatest lower bound (infimum) of \( A \). By definition, \( a_0 \) is a lower bound of \( A \), and any other lower bound is less than or equal to \( a_0 \). To establish the approximation property, suppose for contradiction that there exists a positive \(\varepsilon\) such that every element of \( A \) is at least \( a_0 + \varepsilon \). Then \( a_0 + \varepsilon \) would be a lower bound greater than \( a_0 \), contradicting the minimality of \( a_0 \). Hence, for every positive \(\varepsilon\), there must be an element of \( A \) less than \( a_0 + \varepsilon \). Conversely, assume that \( a_0 \) is a lower bound of \( A \) and that for every positive \(\varepsilon\), there exists an element \( a \in A \) with \( a < a_0 + \varepsilon \). Since \( A \) is nonempty and bounded below, to show \( a_0 \) is the infimum, it suffices to verify that any lower bound \( y \) of \( A \) satisfies \( y \leq a_0 \). Suppose, for contradiction, that there exists a lower bound \( x \) with \( x > a_0 \). Setting \(\varepsilon = x - a_0 > 0\), by assumption, there exists \( a \in A \) with \( a < a_0 + \varepsilon = x \), contradicting that \( x \) is a lower bound. Therefore, no such \( x \) exists, so \( a_0 \) is indeed the greatest lower bound of \( A \). This completes the proof.
