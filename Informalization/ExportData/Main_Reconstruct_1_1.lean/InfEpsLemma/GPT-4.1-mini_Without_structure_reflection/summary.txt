We will prove that the infimum of a set \( A \) equals a real number \( a_0 \) if and only if \( a_0 \) is a lower bound of \( A \) and for every positive number \( \varepsilon \), there exists an element in \( A \) less than \( a_0 + \varepsilon \). To establish this equivalence, it suffices to prove both directions separately.

First, assume that the infimum of \( A \) is \( a_0 \). By definition, the infimum of a nonempty set \( A \) bounded below is its greatest lower bound. Thus, \( a_0 \) is a lower bound of \( A \) and any other lower bound is less than or equal to \( a_0 \). It follows immediately that \( a_0 \) is a lower bound of \( A \). Next, suppose for contradiction that there exists some positive \( \varepsilon \) such that no element of \( A \) is less than \( a_0 + \varepsilon \). Then every element of \( A \) is at least \( a_0 + \varepsilon \), which implies \( a_0 + \varepsilon \) is also a lower bound of \( A \). But since \( \varepsilon > 0 \), this contradicts the maximality of \( a_0 \) as the greatest lower bound. Therefore, for every positive \( \varepsilon \), there must exist an element in \( A \) less than \( a_0 + \varepsilon \).

For the converse, assume that \( a_0 \) is a lower bound of \( A \) and that for every positive \( \varepsilon \), there exists an element \( a \in A \) with \( a < a_0 + \varepsilon \). We need to show that \( a_0 \) is the greatest lower bound of \( A \). Since \( a_0 \) is a lower bound, this is half of the requirement. Now, let \( y \) be any lower bound of \( A \). Suppose, for the sake of contradiction, that \( y > a_0 \). Then defining \( \varepsilon = y - a_0 > 0 \), by assumption there exists an element \( a \in A \) such that \( a < a_0 + \varepsilon = y \). But since \( y \) is a lower bound, every element of \( A \) satisfies \( a \geq y \), which contradicts \( a < y \). Hence, no such \( y \) exists, and every lower bound \( y \) satisfies \( y \leq a_0 \). Therefore, \( a_0 \) is the greatest lower bound of \( A \), and so \( a_0 \) is the infimum of \( A \).

This completes the proof of the equivalence.
