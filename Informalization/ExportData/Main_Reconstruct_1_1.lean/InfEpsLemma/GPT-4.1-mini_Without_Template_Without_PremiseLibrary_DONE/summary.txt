We will show the equivalence characterizing the infimum of a nonempty set \( A \) bounded below. First, assume that \( a_0 \) is the infimum of \( A \). By definition, \( a_0 \) is a lower bound of \( A \), and any lower bound of \( A \) is less than or equal to \( a_0 \). To prove the key property of the infimum, suppose for contradiction that there exists a positive number \(\varepsilon\) such that no element of \( A \) is less than \( a_0 + \varepsilon \). Then every element of \( A \) would be at least \( a_0 + \varepsilon \), making \( a_0 + \varepsilon \) a lower bound greater than \( a_0 \), contradicting the maximality of \( a_0 \). Thus, for every positive \(\varepsilon\), there must exist an element of \( A \) less than \( a_0 + \varepsilon \).

Conversely, suppose \( a_0 \) is a lower bound of \( A \) and for every positive \(\varepsilon\), there exists an \( a \in A \) with \( a < a_0 + \varepsilon \). We must show that \( a_0 \) is the greatest lower bound. Since \( a_0 \) is a lower bound, no elements of \( A \) lie below it. To see that \( a_0 \) is the greatest, assume for contradiction that there is another lower bound \( x > a_0 \). Then \(\varepsilon = x - a_0 > 0\), so by assumption there exists an \( a \in A \) with \( a < a_0 + \varepsilon = x \). However, since \( x \) is a lower bound, we have \( a \geq x \), a contradiction. Hence, no lower bound exceeds \( a_0 \), and \( a_0 \) is the infimum of \( A \).

This completes the proof of the equivalence characterizing the infimum.
