We will show that a real number \( a_0 \) is the infimum of a nonempty set \( A \) bounded below if and only if \( a_0 \) is a lower bound of \( A \) and every neighborhood above \( a_0 \) contains an element of \( A \). First, assume that \( a_0 = \inf A \). Since the infimum coincides with the greatest lower bound, it follows that \( a_0 \) is a lower bound of \( A \). To show that for every \( \varepsilon > 0 \), there exists \( a \in A \) with \( a < a_0 + \varepsilon \), suppose on the contrary that no such element exists for some \( \varepsilon > 0 \). Then \( a_0 + \varepsilon \) would be a lower bound of \( A \) strictly greater than \( a_0 \), contradicting the minimality of \( a_0 \). Hence, such elements must exist. Conversely, suppose that \( a_0 \) is a lower bound of \( A \) and for every \( \varepsilon > 0 \), there is an element \( a \in A \) with \( a < a_0 + \varepsilon \). To prove \( a_0 = \inf A \), we verify that \( a_0 \) is the greatest lower bound of \( A \). Indeed, \( a_0 \) is a lower bound by assumption. If there existed a lower bound \( x \) with \( x > a_0 \), then setting \( \varepsilon = x - a_0 > 0 \) would imply the existence of an \( a \in A \) with \( a < a_0 + \varepsilon = x \), contradicting the assumption that \( x \) is a lower bound. Therefore, no such \( x \) exists, and \( a_0 \) is the greatest lower bound, concluding that \( \inf A = a_0 \).
