We will show that any element \( s \) of a set \( S \) is greater than or equal to the greatest lower bound (infimum) of \( S \). First, we note that the infimum of \( S \) is itself a lower bound of \( S \) by the definition of the infimum for a nonempty set bounded below; specifically, the infimum is the greatest of all lower bounds of \( S \), and thus must be a lower bound. Since \( s \) belongs to \( S \), it follows from this and the fact that the infimum is a lower bound that \( s \) is at least as large as the infimum. This completes the proof.
