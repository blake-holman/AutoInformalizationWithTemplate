We will show that for any element \( s \) in the set \( S \), \( s \) is greater than or equal to the greatest lower bound (infimum) of \( S \). To do this, we first note that by the definition of the infimum, the greatest lower bound of a nonempty set \( S \) that is bounded below is itself a lower bound of \( S \). Since \( s \) is an element of \( S \), it must be greater than or equal to every lower bound of \( S \), and in particular to the greatest lower bound. Thus, we conclude that \( s \geq \inf S \) for all \( s \in S \), completing the proof.
