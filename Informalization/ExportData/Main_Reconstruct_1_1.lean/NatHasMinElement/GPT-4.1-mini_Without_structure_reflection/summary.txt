We will show that if the set \( S \) is nonempty, then there exists an element \( x \in S \) such that for every element \( y \in S \), \( x \leq y \). Assume, for the sake of contradiction, that for every element \( x \in S \), there exists a smaller element \( y \in S \). We proceed to prove by strong induction that no natural number belongs to \( S \). Let \( n \) be an arbitrary natural number, and suppose \( n \in S \). By the assumption, there exists an element \( y \in S \) with \( y < n \). Since \( y < n \), the induction hypothesis implies \( y \notin S \), contradicting the fact that \( y \in S \). Thus, \( n \notin S \). By strong induction, this holds for every natural number \( n \). Since \( S \) is nonempty, there must exist an element \( x \in S \), but by the previous result, \( x \notin S \), yielding a contradiction. Therefore, our initial assumption is false, and there exists an element \( x \in S \) such that for all \( y \in S \), \( x \leq y \), completing the proof.
