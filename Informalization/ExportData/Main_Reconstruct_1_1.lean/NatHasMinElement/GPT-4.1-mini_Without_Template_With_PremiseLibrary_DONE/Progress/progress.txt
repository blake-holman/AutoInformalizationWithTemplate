[answer]
We will show that no natural number belongs to the set \( S \) by using strong induction. Assume an arbitrary natural number \( n \). As the induction hypothesis, suppose that for every natural number less than \( n \), that number does not belong to \( S \). Now, for the sake of contradiction, assume that \( n \) is in \( S \). By the given property of the set \( S \), there must exist some element \( y \in S \) such that \( y < n \). However, applying the induction hypothesis to \( y \), since \( y < n \), implies that \( y \notin S \), which contradicts the fact that \( y \in S \). This contradiction shows that our assumption that \( n \in S \) is false, and therefore \( n \notin S \). Since \( n \) was arbitrary, it follows that no natural number belongs to \( S \).
