We will show that if the set \( S \) is nonempty, then there exists an element \( x \in S \) such that \( x \) is less than or equal to every element \( y \in S \). Suppose, toward a contradiction, that for every element \( x \in S \), there exists another element \( y \in S \) strictly smaller than \( x \). To derive a contradiction, we proceed by strong induction on the natural numbers to show that no natural number belongs to \( S \). Assume as the induction hypothesis that for every natural number less than \( n \), that number is not in \( S \). Now, suppose \( n \in S \). By the stated property, there exists \( y \in S \) with \( y < n \). However, by the induction hypothesis, \( y \notin S \), contradicting the assumption. Therefore, \( n \notin S \) for every natural number \( n \). Returning to our original assumption that \( S \) is nonempty, we select an element \( x \in S \). Applying the above result, we conclude that \( x \notin S \), contradicting its selection from \( S \). This contradiction shows that our initial supposition is false, and thus there exists an element \( x \in S \) which is minimal in the sense that no element in \( S \) is strictly smaller. This completes the proof.
