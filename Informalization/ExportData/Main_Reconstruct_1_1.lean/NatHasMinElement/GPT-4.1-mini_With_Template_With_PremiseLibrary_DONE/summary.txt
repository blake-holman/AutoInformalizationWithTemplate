We will show that every nonempty set \( S \) of natural numbers contains a smallest element, that is, an element \( x \in S \) such that \( x \leq y \) for every \( y \in S \). Assume, for the sake of contradiction, that this is not the case; in other words, for every element \( x \in S \), there exists some element \( y \in S \) with \( y < x \). Using strong induction on the natural numbers, suppose there exists \( n \in S \). By the assumption, there must be \( y \in S \) with \( y < n \). Since \( y < n \), the induction hypothesis implies \( y \notin S \), contradicting that \( y \in S \). Hence, no natural number can belong to \( S \), contradicting the initial assumption that \( S \) is nonempty. Therefore, the original assumption leads to a contradiction, and so there must exist an element \( x \in S \) such that \( x \leq y \) for all \( y \in S \), completing the proof.
