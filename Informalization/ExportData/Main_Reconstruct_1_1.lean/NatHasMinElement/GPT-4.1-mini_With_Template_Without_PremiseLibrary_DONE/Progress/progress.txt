[answer]
We will show that no natural number belongs to the set \( S \). To prove this, we proceed by strong induction on the natural number \( n \). Assume, for the sake of contradiction, that \( n \in S \). By the given property of \( S \), there exists an element \( y \in S \) such that \( y < n \). By the induction hypothesis, all elements less than \( n \) are not in \( S \), so in particular, \( y \notin S \). This contradicts the earlier statement that \( y \in S \). Hence, our assumption that \( n \in S \) must be false, and it follows that no natural number belongs to \( S \). This completes the proof.
