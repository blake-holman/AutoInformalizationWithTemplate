We will show that for any positive real number \(\varepsilon\), there exists a natural number \(n\) such that the reciprocal of \(n + 1\) is less than \(\varepsilon\). By the Archimedean property, since \(\varepsilon > 0\) and \(1 > 0\), there exists \(n \in \mathbb{N}\) satisfying \((n + 1)\varepsilon > 1\). Fix such an \(n\). Noting that \(n + 1 > 0\) as the sum of natural numbers and positive real numbers, and using the fact that dividing an inequality by a positive number preserves the inequality direction, the inequality \(1 < (n + 1)\varepsilon\) is equivalent to \(\frac{1}{n + 1} < \varepsilon\). By commutativity of multiplication, this establishes the desired inequality, completing the proof.
