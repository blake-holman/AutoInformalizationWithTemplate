We will show that for every positive real number \(\varepsilon\), there exists a natural number \(n\) such that the reciprocal of \(n + 1\) is less than \(\varepsilon\). By the Archimedean property of the real numbers, since \(\varepsilon > 0\), there exists a natural number \(n\) such that \((n + 1) \varepsilon > 1\). To confirm this implies \(\frac{1}{n + 1} < \varepsilon\), we first note that \(n + 1 > 0\) as the sum of a natural number and 1, relying on the fundamental fact that \(1 > 0\) in the real numbers. Next, applying the property that dividing one number by a positive number is less than another number if and only if the numerator is less than the product of the denominator and that other number, we see that proving \(\frac{1}{n+1} < \varepsilon\) reduces to showing \(1 < (n + 1) \varepsilon\). By the commutativity of multiplication, this is equivalent to the inequality already established by the Archimedean property. Therefore, such an \(n\) exists, completing the proof.
