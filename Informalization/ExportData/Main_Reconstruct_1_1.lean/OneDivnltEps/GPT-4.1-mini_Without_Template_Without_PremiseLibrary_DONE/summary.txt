We will show that for any positive real number \( \varepsilon \), there exists a natural number \( n \) such that \(\frac{1}{n+1} < \varepsilon\). Suppose \(\varepsilon > 0\) is given. By the Archimedean property of the real numbers, since both \(\varepsilon\) and 1 are positive, there exists a natural number \( n \) satisfying the inequality \((n+1)\varepsilon > 1\). Selecting such an \( n \), we note that \( n+1 \) is a positive real number because either \( n > 0 \) or, if not, at least \(1 > 0\), ensuring \( n+1 > 0 \). Since the divisor \( n+1 \) is positive, we can rewrite the inequality \( 1 < (n+1)\varepsilon \) equivalently as \(\frac{1}{n+1} < \varepsilon\) by dividing both sides by \( n+1 \). Thus, we have found a natural number \( n \) such that the reciprocal \(\frac{1}{n+1}\) is less than the given \(\varepsilon\), completing the proof.
