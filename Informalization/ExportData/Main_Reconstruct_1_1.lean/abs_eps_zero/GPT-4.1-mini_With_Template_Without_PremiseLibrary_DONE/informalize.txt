Show that if for every positive number ε, the absolute value of \( a \) is less than \( 0 + \varepsilon \), then the absolute value of \( a \) is less than or equal to 0. This follows from the principle that if a quantity is less than every positive number added to zero, it must be less than or equal to zero.

[answer]
By simplifying the expression, the assumption that for every positive real number \( \varepsilon \), the absolute value of \( a \) is less than \( 0 + \varepsilon \) becomes the assumption that for every positive real number \( \varepsilon \), the absolute value of \( a \) is less than \( \varepsilon \).

[answer]
The proof is completed from the assumption that for every positive real number ε, the absolute value of \(a\) is less than ε.
