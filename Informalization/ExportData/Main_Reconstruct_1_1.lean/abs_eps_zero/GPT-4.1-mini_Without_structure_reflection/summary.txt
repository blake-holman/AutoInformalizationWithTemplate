We will show that if a number \( a \) satisfies the condition that for every positive real number \( \varepsilon \), the absolute value \( |a| \) is less than \( \varepsilon \), then \( a = 0 \). To begin, we note that since \( |a| < \varepsilon \) for all positive \( \varepsilon \), it follows that \( |a| \) must be less than or equal to zero, because if \( |a| \) were greater than zero, there would exist some positive \( \varepsilon \) smaller than \( |a| \), contradicting the assumption. By the definition of absolute value, \( |a| \leq 0 \) implies \( |a| = 0 \), which in turn means \( a = 0 \). Therefore, from the initial assumption that \( |a| < \varepsilon \) for every positive \( \varepsilon \), we conclude that \( a = 0 \), completing the proof.
