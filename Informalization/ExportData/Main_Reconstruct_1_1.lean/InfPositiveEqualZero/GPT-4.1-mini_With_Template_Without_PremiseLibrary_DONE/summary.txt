We will show that the set \( P \) of all positive real numbers is nonempty and that its greatest lower bound (infimum) is zero. First, note that since \(1 > 0\), the number 1 belongs to \( P \), proving that \( P \) is indeed nonempty. Next, by definition every element \( a \in P \) satisfies \( a > 0 \), and thus \( a \geq 0 \), so zero is a lower bound of \( P \). Consequently, \( P \) is bounded below by zero. Because \( P \) is nonempty and bounded below, the infimum \(\inf P\) exists and satisfies \(\inf P \geq 0\). To show that \(\inf P = 0\), assume for contradiction that \(\inf P > 0\). Then \(\frac{\inf P}{2} > 0\) and belongs to \( P \), but since \(\inf P\) is the greatest lower bound, every element of \( P \) must satisfy \( a \geq \inf P\), implying \(\frac{\inf P}{2} \geq \inf P\). This inequality contradicts the fact that \(\frac{\inf P}{2} < \inf P\). Hence, the assumption \(\inf P > 0\) is false, and we conclude that \(\inf P \leq 0\). Combining this with \(\inf P \geq 0\) yields \(\inf P = 0\), completing the proof.
