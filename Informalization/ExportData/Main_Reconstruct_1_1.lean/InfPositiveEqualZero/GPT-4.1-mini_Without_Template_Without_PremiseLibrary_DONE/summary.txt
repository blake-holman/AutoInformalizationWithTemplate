We will show that the set \( P \) of all real numbers greater than zero is nonempty, bounded below, and has greatest lower bound (infimum) equal to zero. First, note that \( P \) is nonempty because \( 1 \in P \) since \( 1 > 0 \). Next, we establish that zero is a lower bound of \( P \) by observing that for any \( a \in P \), \( a > 0 \) implies \( a \geq 0 \); hence, zero is a lower bound. Since \( P \) is nonempty and bounded below, its greatest lower bound exists and satisfies \(\inf P \geq 0\). To show that \(\inf P\) cannot be strictly greater than zero, suppose for contradiction that \(\inf P > 0\). Then, \(\frac{1}{2} \inf P > 0\) and thus \(\frac{1}{2} \inf P \in P\). However, by definition of infimum, \(\frac{1}{2} \inf P \geq \inf P\), which implies \(\inf P \leq \frac{1}{2} \inf P\), a contradiction because it forces \(\inf P \leq 0\). Combining these inequalities, it follows that \(\inf P \leq 0\) and \(\inf P \geq 0\), so \(\inf P = 0\). Therefore, the greatest lower bound of the set of positive real numbers is exactly zero.
