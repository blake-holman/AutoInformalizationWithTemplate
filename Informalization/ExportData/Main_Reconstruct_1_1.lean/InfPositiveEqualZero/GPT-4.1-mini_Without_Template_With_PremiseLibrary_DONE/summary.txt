We will show that the set \( P \) of all real numbers greater than zero is nonempty, bounded below by zero, and that its greatest lower bound is precisely zero. First, note that \( P \) is nonempty since the number 1 belongs to \( P \) because \(1 > 0\). Next, consider any element \(a \in P\); by definition, \(a > 0\), which implies \(a \geq 0\). Thus, zero is a lower bound of \( P \). Since \( P \) is nonempty and bounded below, the greatest lower bound \(\inf P\) exists and satisfies \(\inf P \geq 0\). To show \(\inf P = 0\), suppose for contradiction that \(\inf P > 0\). Then, consider \(\frac{\inf P}{2} > 0\), which lies in \( P \). By the definition of the infimum, every element of \( P \) must be greater than or equal to \(\inf P\), so \(\frac{\inf P}{2} \geq \inf P\). Multiplying both sides by 2 yields \(\inf P \geq 2\inf P\), which implies \(\inf P \leq 0\), contradicting \(\inf P > 0\). Therefore, \(\inf P\) cannot be strictly greater than zero. Combining this with the fact that \(\inf P \geq 0\), we conclude that \(\inf P = 0\). This completes the proof.
