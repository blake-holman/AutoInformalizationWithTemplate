We will show that if \( C \) is a nonempty set of real numbers bounded above, then there exists a real number \( a \) that serves as the least upper bound of \( C \). First, define the set \( B \) as the collection of all upper bounds of \( C \), which is nonempty by the boundedness assumption. Let \( A \) be the complement of \( B \) in the real numbers, consisting of all real numbers that are not upper bounds of \( C \). We show that the pair \((A, B)\) forms a Dedekind cut by verifying that \( A \) and \( B \) are disjoint, their union covers the entire real line, and both \( A \) and \( B \) are nonempty. Specifically, \( A \cap B = \emptyset \) by definition of complements, and \( A \cup B = \mathbb{R} \) follows similarly. To establish \( A \neq \emptyset \), choose \( c \in C \) and observe that \( c - 1 < c \) implies there exists an element \( z \in A \), since \( z \) cannot be an upper bound. Moreover, every element of \( A \) is strictly less than every element of \( B \); for otherwise, an element of \( A \) would belong to \( B \), contradicting the disjointness. Having established that \((A, B)\) is a Dedekind cut, it follows that either \( A \) has a greatest element or \( B \) has a least element. In the case where \( B \) has a minimum element, this element is the least upper bound of \( C \). Alternatively, if \( A \) has a maximum element \( x \), then \( x \notin B \), so it is not an upper bound for \( C \), guaranteeing the existence of an element \( c \in C \) with \( c > x \). Considering numbers strictly between \( x \) and \( c \), such as their midpoint, shows there are elements of \( A \) exceeding \( x \), contradicting the maximality of \( x \). Hence, \( A \) cannot have a greatest element without contradicting its definition, implying that \( B \) must have a least element. Thus, the supremum of \( C \) exists as this minimal element of \( B \), completing the proof.
