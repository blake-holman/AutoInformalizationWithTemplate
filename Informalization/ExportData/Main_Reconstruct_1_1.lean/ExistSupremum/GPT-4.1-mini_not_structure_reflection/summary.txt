We will show that if \( C \) is a nonempty subset of the real numbers bounded above, then there exists a real number \( a \) that is the supremum of \( C \). First, let \( B \) be the set of all upper bounds of \( C \). Since \( C \) is bounded above, \( B \) is nonempty. Define \( A \) as the set of real numbers not in \( B \). We verify that \( A \) and \( B \) form a Dedekind cut by showing both are nonempty, disjoint, and together cover all real numbers, with every element of \( A \) strictly less than every element of \( B \).

To see that \( A \) is nonempty, pick any element \( c \in C \) and note that numbers less than \( c \), such as \( c - 1 \), belong to \( A \) since they are not upper bounds of \( C \). We prove that for any \( a \in A \) and \( b \in B \), \( a < b \) holds by contradiction: assuming \( a \geq b \) implies \( a \in B \), contradicting \( a \in A \), so the intersection \( A \cap B \) is empty.

Having established that \( A \) and \( B \) form a Dedekind cut, by the completeness axiom there exists either a maximum element in \( A \) or a minimum element in \( B \). If \( B \) has a minimum element \( x \), then \( x \) is the supremum of \( C \) by definition. Alternatively, if \( A \) has a maximum element \( x \), we show \( x \) is also the supremum of \( C \) by demonstrating \( x \notin B \), that is, \( x \) is not an upper bound of \( C \), but no element greater than \( x \) belongs to \( A \), thereby establishing \( x \) as the least upper bound. This is achieved by considering elements between \( x \) and some \( c \in C \) greater than \( x \), which belong to \( A \), and showing the midpoint is also in \( A \), contradicting any assumption that all elements of \( A \) are less than or equal to \( x \) unless \( x \) is maximal.

Thus, in either case, there exists a real number \( a \) that serves as the supremum of the set \( C \), completing the proof.
