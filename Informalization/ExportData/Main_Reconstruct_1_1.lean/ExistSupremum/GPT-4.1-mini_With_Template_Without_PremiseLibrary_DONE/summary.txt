We will show that if the set \( C \) is nonempty and bounded above, then there exists a supremum of \( C \). To this end, let \( B \) be the set of all upper bounds of \( C \), which is nonempty by assumption since \( C \) is bounded above. Define \( A \) as the complement of \( B \) in the real numbers. We verify that the pair \( (A, B) \) forms a Dedekind cut by confirming that both sets are disjoint, their union covers the entire set of real numbers, \( A \) is nonempty, and every element of \( A \) is strictly less than every element of \( B \).

In particular, \( A \) is nonempty because for some \( c \in C \), the number \( c - 1 \) lies in \( A \) since it is not an upper bound of \( C \). Moreover, assuming for contradiction that there exist \( a \in A \) and \( b \in B \) with \( a \geq b \), we derive \( a \in B \), contradicting the disjointness of \( A \) and \( B \). Thus, \( a < b \) for all \( a \in A \) and \( b \in B \).

By the completeness property associated with Dedekind cuts, either \( A \) has a maximum element or \( B \) has a minimum element. If \( B \) has a minimum element \( x \), then \( x \) serves as the least upper bound (supremum) of \( C \). If \( A \) has a maximum element \( x \), we show that such an \( x \) is the supremum of \( C \) by demonstrating that \( x \notin B \) and that there exists some \( c \in C \) with \( x < c \). Furthermore, for every real number \( z \) between \( x \) and \( c \), it holds that \( z \in A \), ensuring the nonemptiness of the interval \( (x, c) \). This establishes the existence of elements in \( A \) greater than \( x \), contradicting maximality unless \( x \) itself is the supremum.

Consequently, in either case, the set \( C \) admits a supremum in the real numbers, completing the proof.
