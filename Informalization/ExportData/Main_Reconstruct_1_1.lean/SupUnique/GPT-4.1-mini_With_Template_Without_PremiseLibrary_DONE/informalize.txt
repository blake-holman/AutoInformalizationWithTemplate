[appliedTactic]
rw [Supremum] at  * 
[answer]
By using the definition of supremum, the assumptions that \( a \) and \( b \) are suprema of the set \( S \) become the statements that \( a \) and \( b \) are upper bounds of \( S \) and that any upper bound of \( S \) is greater than or equal to \( a \) and \( b \), respectively, while the goal of proving \( a = b \) remains unchanged.
[appliedTactic]
have aleb: a ≤ b := aSup.right b  bSup.left 
[answer]
Show that \( a \) is less than or equal to \( b \). This can be proved by using the fact that \( a \) is an upper bound of the set and \( b \) is also an upper bound of the set, which implies that \( a \) cannot exceed \( b \).
[appliedTactic]
have blea: b ≤ a := bSup.right a  aSup.left 
[answer]
Show that \( b \leq a \). This can be proved by noting that \( b \) is an upper bound of the set \( S \) and \( a \) is the least upper bound of \( S \), so since \( a \) is less than or equal to any upper bound, it follows that \( b \leq a \).
[appliedTactic]
apply le_antisymm aleb  blea 
[answer]
From the property that two elements are equal if each is less than or equal to the other, and the assumptions that \( a \leq b \) and \( b \leq a \), in order to establish that \( a = b \), it suffices to observe that these inequalities hold.
