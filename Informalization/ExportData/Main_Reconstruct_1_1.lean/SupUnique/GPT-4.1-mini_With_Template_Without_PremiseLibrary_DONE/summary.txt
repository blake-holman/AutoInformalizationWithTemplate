We will show that if both \( a \) and \( b \) are supremums of the set \( S \), then \( a = b \). By the definition of supremum, \( a \) and \( b \) are upper bounds of \( S \), and moreover, any upper bound of \( S \) is greater than or equal to \( a \) and \( b \), respectively. To prove the equality, first note that since \( a \) is an upper bound and \( b \) is also an upper bound, it follows that \( a \leq b \). Similarly, because \( a \) is the least upper bound of \( S \) and \( b \) is an upper bound, we have \( b \leq a \). Combining these inequalities, we conclude that \( a = b \), as two elements are equal precisely when each is less than or equal to the other.
