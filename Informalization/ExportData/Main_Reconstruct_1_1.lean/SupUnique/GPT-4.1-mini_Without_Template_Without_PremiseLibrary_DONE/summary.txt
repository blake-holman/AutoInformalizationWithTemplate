We will show that if \(a\) and \(b\) are both suprema of the set \(S\), then \(a = b\). By the definition of the supremum, both \(a\) and \(b\) are upper bounds of \(S\), and any other upper bound of \(S\) is greater than or equal to them. Since \(b\) is an upper bound of \(S\), it follows from the minimality of \(a\) that \(a \leq b\). Similarly, since \(a\) is an upper bound, the minimality of \(b\) implies \(b \leq a\). Combining these two inequalities and applying the antisymmetry property of the ordering relation, we conclude that \(a = b\). This establishes the uniqueness of the supremum.
