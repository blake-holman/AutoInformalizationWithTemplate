intro aSup  bSup 
[answer]
Assume that \( a \) is a supremum of the set \( S \) and that \( b \) is also a supremum of the set \( S \). From now on, we will show that \( a \) and \( b \) are equal.
[appliedTactic]
rw [Supremum] at  * 
[answer]
By unfolding the definition of the supremum, the assumptions that \( a \) and \( b \) are the least upper bounds of the set \( S \) are restated explicitly as \( a \) and \( b \) being upper bounds of \( S \) such that any other upper bound of \( S \) is greater than or equal to them. This clarifies the properties of \( a \) and \( b \) in the goal to prove their equality.
[appliedTactic]
have aleb: a ≤ b := aSup.right b  bSup.left 
[answer]
Establish that \( a \) is less than or equal to \( b \) by using the fact that both \( a \) and \( b \) are upper bounds of the set \( S \), and that \( a \) is less than or equal to any upper bound of \( S \). Since \( b \) is an upper bound, it follows that \( a \leq b \).
[appliedTactic]
have blea: b ≤ a := bSup.right a  aSup.left 
[answer]
Establish that \( b \) is less than or equal to \( a \) by using the fact that \( b \) is an upper bound of the set and that \( a \) is also an upper bound. Since \( b \) is an upper bound, it must be less than or equal to any other upper bound, including \( a \). This introduces the inequality \( b \leq a \) alongside the existing \( a \leq b \).
[appliedTactic]
apply le_antisymm aleb  blea 
[answer]
Since \( a \) is less than or equal to \( b \) and \( b \) is less than or equal to \( a \), it follows by the antisymmetry property of the order relation that \( a \) and \( b \) are equal. This completes the proof.
