We will show that if \( a \) and \( b \) are both supremums of the set \( S \), then \( a = b \). By the definition of a supremum, both \( a \) and \( b \) are upper bounds of \( S \), and each is less than or equal to any other upper bound of \( S \). Since \( b \) is an upper bound, the fact that \( a \) is the least upper bound implies \( a \leq b \). Similarly, because \( a \) is an upper bound, the minimality of \( b \) ensures \( b \leq a \). Combining these inequalities, by the antisymmetry property of the order relation, it follows that \( a = b \), completing the proof.
