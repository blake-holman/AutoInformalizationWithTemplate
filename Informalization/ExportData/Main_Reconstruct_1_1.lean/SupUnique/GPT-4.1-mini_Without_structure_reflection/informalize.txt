[appliedTactic]
intro aSup  bSup 
[answer]
Assume that \( a \) and \( b \) are both supremums of the set \( S \). From now on, we will show that \( a = b \).
[appliedTactic]
rw [Supremum] at  * 
[answer]
By using the definition that a supremum of a set is an upper bound that is less than or equal to every other upper bound, the assumptions that \( a \) and \( b \) are suprema of the set \( S \) become the assumptions that \( a \) and \( b \) are upper bounds of \( S \) and for any upper bound \( y \) of \( S \), \( a \leq y \) and \( b \leq y \), respectively, while the goal remains to prove that \( a = b \).
[appliedTactic]
have aleb: a ≤ b := aSup.right b  bSup.left 
[answer]
Show that \( a \leq b \). This can be proved by using that \( a \) is an upper bound of the set and that \( b \) is also an upper bound of the set, which implies \( a \leq b \) by comparing these upper bounds.
[appliedTactic]
have blea: b ≤ a := bSup.right a  aSup.left 
[answer]
Show that \( b \) is less than or equal to \( a \). This can be proved by using the fact that \( b \) is an upper bound of the set and that \( a \) is also an upper bound of the set, which implies \( b \leq a \) by the definition of the supremum and the ordering of upper bounds.
[appliedTactic]
apply le_antisymm aleb  blea 
[answer]
From the theorem that if one element is less than or equal to another and vice versa, then the two elements are equal, and the assumptions that \( a \leq b \) and \( b \leq a \), in order to establish that \( a = b \), it suffices to prove no further goals.
