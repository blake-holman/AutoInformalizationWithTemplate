intro aSup  bSup 
[answer]
Assume that \( a \) is a supremum of the set \( S \) and that \( b \) is also a supremum of the set \( S \). From now on, we will show that \( a \) and \( b \) are equal.
[appliedTactic]
rw [Supremum] at  * 
[answer]
By expanding the definition of the supremum, the assumptions that \( a \) and \( b \) are supremums of the set \( S \) are restated as \( a \) and \( b \) being upper bounds of \( S \) such that any other upper bound of \( S \) is greater than or equal to \( a \) and \( b \), respectively. This reformulates the goal of proving \( a = b \) in terms of these properties of least upper bounds.
[appliedTactic]
have aleb: a ≤ b := aSup.right b  bSup.left 
[answer]
Establish that \( a \) is less than or equal to \( b \) by using the properties of upper bounds for the set \( S \). Specifically, since \( a \) is an upper bound of \( S \) and \( b \) is also an upper bound, the minimality condition on \( a \) implies that \( a \) cannot exceed \( b \). This is concluded by extracting the relevant parts of the assumptions about \( a \) and \( b \) being upper bounds and applying the ordering condition that any upper bound must be at least \( a \).
[appliedTactic]
have blea: b ≤ a := bSup.right a  aSup.left 
[answer]
Establish that \( b \) is less than or equal to \( a \) by using the fact that \( b \) is an upper bound of the set and that \( a \) is also an upper bound. Since \( a \) satisfies the property of being an upper bound, it follows that \( b \) cannot exceed \( a \). This introduces the inequality \( b \leq a \) as a new assumption in the proof.
[appliedTactic]
apply le_antisymm aleb  blea 
[answer]
Since \( a \) is less than or equal to \( b \) and \( b \) is less than or equal to \( a \), it follows from the antisymmetry property of the order relation that \( a \) and \( b \) are equal. This completes the proof.
