We will show that if \( a \) and \( b \) are both suprema of a set \( S \), then \( a = b \). By the definition of supremum, \( a \) and \( b \) are upper bounds of \( S \) with the property that any other upper bound of \( S \) is greater than or equal to them. In particular, since \( b \) is an upper bound of \( S \), the minimality of \( a \) implies \( a \leq b \). Similarly, since \( a \) is an upper bound of \( S \), the minimality of \( b \) implies \( b \leq a \). Combining these inequalities and applying the antisymmetry of the order relation, we conclude that \( a = b \), establishing the uniqueness of the supremum.
