We will show that if a set \( S \) of real numbers has two supremums, say \( a \) and \( b \), then \( a = b \). By the definition of supremum, both \( a \) and \( b \) are upper bounds of \( S \) and are the least such upper bounds, meaning any upper bound of \( S \) is greater than or equal to both \( a \) and \( b \). Since \( b \) is an upper bound and \( a \) is the least upper bound, it follows that \( a \leq b \). Similarly, because \( a \) is an upper bound and \( b \) is the least upper bound, we have \( b \leq a \). From these inequalities, by the antisymmetry property of the real numbers, we conclude that \( a = b \).
