intro aSup  bSup 
[answer]
Assume that \( a \) is a supremum of \( S \) and \( b \) is a supremum of \( S \). From now on, we will show that \( a = b \).
[appliedTactic]
rw [Supremum] at  * 
[answer]
By using the definition that a supremum of a set is an upper bound that is less than or equal to every other upper bound, the assumptions that \( a \) and \( b \) are suprema of the set \( S \) become the assumptions that \( a \) and \( b \) are upper bounds of \( S \) and that any upper bound of \( S \) is greater than or equal to \( a \) and \( b \) respectively, while the goal of proving \( a = b \) remains unchanged.
[appliedTactic]
have aleb: a ≤ b := aSup.right b  bSup.left 
[answer]
Show that \( a \leq b \). This can be proved by using the fact that \( a \) is the least upper bound of the set, so it is less than or equal to any other upper bound, and since \( b \) is an upper bound of the set, it follows that \( a \leq b \).
[appliedTactic]
have blea: b ≤ a := bSup.right a  aSup.left 
[answer]
Show that \( b \) is less than or equal to \( a \). This can be proved by using the fact that \( b \) is an upper bound of the set \( S \) and \( a \) is also an upper bound of \( S \), and since \( a \) is an upper bound, \( b \) must be less than or equal to \( a \).
[appliedTactic]
apply le_antisymm aleb  blea 
[answer]
From the theorem that if one element is less than or equal to another and vice versa, then the two elements are equal, and the assumptions that \( a \leq b \) and \( b \leq a \), in order to establish that \( a = b \), it suffices to prove no further goals.
