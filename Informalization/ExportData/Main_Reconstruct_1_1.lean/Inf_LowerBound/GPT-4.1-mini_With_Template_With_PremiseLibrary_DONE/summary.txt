We will show that if \( s \) is a lower bound of a nonempty set \( S \) that is bounded below, then \( s \) is less than or equal to the greatest lower bound (infimum) of \( S \). By definition, the infimum of \( S \) is the greatest element among all lower bounds of \( S \). This means that the infimum is itself a lower bound of \( S \), and any other lower bound of \( S \) must be less than or equal to the infimum. Since \( s \) is assumed to be a lower bound of \( S \), it follows directly from this characterization that \( s \leq \inf S \). Hence, every lower bound of \( S \) is less than or equal to its greatest lower bound, completing the proof.
