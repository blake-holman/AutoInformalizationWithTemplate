Assume that \( s \) is a real number that is a lower bound of the set \( S \). From now on, we will show that \( s \) is less than or equal to the infimum of \( S \).
[appliedTactic]
have := Inf_def S SnotEmpty  SBB 
[answer]
Show that the infimum of the set \( S \) is indeed its greatest lower bound. This can be proved by using the fact that for a nonempty set of real numbers that is bounded below, the infimum is defined as the greatest element among all lower bounds of the set.
[appliedTactic]
rw [Infimum] at  this 
[answer]
By using the definition that a real number is the infimum of a set if it is the greatest lower bound that is less than or equal to every element in the set, the statement that the infimum of the set is equal to a certain value becomes the statement that this value is a lower bound of the set and that any other lower bound of the set is less than or equal to this value.
[appliedTactic]
apply this.right s  LB 
[answer]
From the fact that the infimum of the set \( S \) is a lower bound of \( S \) and that any lower bound of \( S \) is less than or equal to the infimum, together with the assumption that \( s \) is a lower bound of \( S \), in order to establish that \( s \) is less than or equal to the infimum of \( S \), it suffices to directly apply the property that the infimum is the greatest lower bound of \( S \).
