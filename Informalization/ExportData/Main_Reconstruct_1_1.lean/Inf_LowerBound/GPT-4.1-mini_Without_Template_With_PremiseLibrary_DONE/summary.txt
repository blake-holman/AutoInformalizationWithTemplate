We will show that any real number \( s \) that serves as a lower bound for a nonempty set \( S \) is less than or equal to the greatest lower bound (infimum) of \( S \). Since \( S \) is nonempty and bounded below, the infimum of \( S \) exists by definition. By the characterization of the infimum, it is itself a lower bound of \( S \), and moreover, it is greater than or equal to any other lower bound of the set. Given that \( s \) is a lower bound, it follows directly that \( s \leq \inf S \), which completes the proof.
