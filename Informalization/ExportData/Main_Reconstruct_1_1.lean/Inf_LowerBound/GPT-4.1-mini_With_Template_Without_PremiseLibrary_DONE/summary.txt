We will show that if \( s \) is a lower bound of a set \( S \), then \( s \) is less than or equal to the greatest lower bound (infimum) of \( S \). First, recall that the greatest lower bound of \( S \) coincides with the infimum of \( S \), as established by the definition of the infimum: for a nonempty set \( S \) that is bounded below, the infimum is the greatest element among all lower bounds of \( S \). By this definition, the infimum \( sInf(S) \) is a lower bound of \( S \), and moreover, any lower bound of \( S \) cannot exceed \( sInf(S) \). Given the assumption that \( s \) is a lower bound of \( S \), it follows directly that \( s \leq sInf(S) \), confirming that \( s \) is less than or equal to the greatest lower bound of \( S \), as required.
