We will show that any real number \( s \) that serves as a lower bound for the set \( S \) is less than or equal to the greatest lower bound of \( S \). Since \( S \) is nonempty and bounded below, it follows that \( S \) has an infimum, which by definition coincides with the greatest lower bound of \( S \). By unpacking the definition of the greatest lower bound, we know it satisfies two key properties: it is itself a lower bound of \( S \), and any other lower bound of \( S \) is less than or equal to it. Therefore, applying this property to our chosen lower bound \( s \), we conclude that \( s \leq \inf S \), completing the proof.
