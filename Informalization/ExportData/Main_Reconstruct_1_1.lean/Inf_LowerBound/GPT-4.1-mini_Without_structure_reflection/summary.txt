We will show that any real number \( s \) that is a lower bound of a set \( S \) satisfies \( s \leq \inf S \), where \(\inf S\) denotes the infimum of \( S \). By definition, the infimum of a nonempty set \( S \) that is bounded below is the greatest lower bound of \( S \). This means that \(\inf S\) is itself a lower bound of \( S \), and moreover, any other lower bound \( s \) of \( S \) must be less than or equal to \(\inf S\). Since \( s \) is assumed to be a lower bound of \( S \), it follows from this characterization that \( s \leq \inf S \). Thus, the proposition is established by directly applying the definition and properties of the infimum as the greatest lower bound.
