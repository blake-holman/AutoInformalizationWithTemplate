We will show that if for every positive number \( \varepsilon \), the number \( a \) is less than \( b + \varepsilon \), then \( a \leq b \). To begin, define the set \( P \) as the collection of all positive real numbers and observe that this set is nonempty since 1 is clearly greater than zero and hence belongs to \( P \). Next, we show that zero is a lower bound of \( P \) because every positive real number is greater than zero. Since \( P \) is nonempty and bounded below by zero, it follows that it has a greatest lower bound (infimum), which is known to be zero. We then show that the difference \( a - b \) is a lower bound for \( P \) by noting that for any \( p \in P \), the assumption \( a < b + p \) implies \( a - b < p \), so \( a - b \leq p \) holds for all \( p \in P \). Hence, \( a - b \) is a lower bound of \( P \) and must be less than or equal to the infimum of \( P \), i.e., \( a - b \leq 0 \). Rearranging this inequality yields \( a \leq b \), completing the proof.
