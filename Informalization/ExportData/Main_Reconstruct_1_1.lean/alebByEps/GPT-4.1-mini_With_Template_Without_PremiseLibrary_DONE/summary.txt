We will show that if for every positive number \( \varepsilon \), \( a < b + \varepsilon \), then it follows that \( a \leq b \). To begin, consider the set \( P \) of all positive real numbers. First, we note that this set is nonempty since it contains the element 1, as \( 1 > 0 \). Next, we establish that zero is a lower bound of \( P \) because every positive real number is greater than zero, hence each element of \( P \) satisfies \( p \geq 0 \). It follows that \( P \) is bounded below by zero. We then show that \( a - b \) serves as a lower bound for the set \( P \). Indeed, since \( a < b + p \) for every \( p \in P \), no positive \( p \) can be less than \( a - b \), implying \( a - b \leq p \) for all \( p \in P \). By the property that any lower bound of a nonempty set is less than or equal to its greatest lower bound (infimum), we have \( a - b \leq \inf P \). Since the infimum of the positive real numbers is zero, we deduce \( a - b \leq 0 \), which implies \( a \leq b \). This completes the proof.
