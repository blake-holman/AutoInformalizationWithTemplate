We will show that if for every positive number \( \varepsilon \), the inequality \( a < b + \varepsilon \) holds, then it follows that \( a \leq b \). To this end, consider the set \( P \) of all positive real numbers. First, note that \( P \) is nonempty since \( 1 \in P \) because \( 1 > 0 \). Next, observe that zero is a lower bound for \( P \) because every element of \( P \) is strictly greater than zero, implying that zero is less than or equal to each element in \( P \). Hence, \( P \) is bounded below with zero as a lower bound. Now, based on the assumption \( a < b + p \) for arbitrary \( p \in P \), we can rewrite this as \( a - b < p \), which means \( a - b \) is less than every positive real number \( p \); equivalently, \( a - b \) is a lower bound for \( P \). Since \( a - b \) is a lower bound of \( P \) and zero is the greatest lower bound (infimum) of \( P \), it follows that \( a - b \leq 0 \). Adding \( b \) to both sides yields \( a \leq b \), which completes the proof.
