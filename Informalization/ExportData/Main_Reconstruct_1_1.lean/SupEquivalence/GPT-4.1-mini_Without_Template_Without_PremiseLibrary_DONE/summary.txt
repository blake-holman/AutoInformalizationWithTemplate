We will show the equivalence between \( a_0 \) being the supremum (least upper bound) of the set \( A \) and the two key properties: that \( a_0 \) is an upper bound of \( A \), and for every positive number \( \varepsilon \), there is an element of \( A \) greater than \( a_0 - \varepsilon \). First, assuming \( a_0 \) is the supremum of \( A \), it follows directly from the definition that \( a_0 \) bounds all elements of \( A \) from above. To verify the second property, suppose for contradiction that there exists some \( \varepsilon > 0 \) such that no element of \( A \) exceeds \( a_0 - \varepsilon \). This would imply \( a_0 - \varepsilon \) is an upper bound of \( A \), which contradicts the minimality of \( a_0 \) as the least upper bound since \( a_0 - \varepsilon < a_0 \). Hence, for every positive \( \varepsilon \), there must be an element in \( A \) larger than \( a_0 - \varepsilon \). Conversely, suppose \( a_0 \) is an upper bound of \( A \) with the property that for every \( \varepsilon > 0 \) there exists some \( a \in A \) with \( a > a_0 - \varepsilon \). To show that \( a_0 \) is the least upper bound, consider any upper bound \( x \) of \( A \). If we suppose, aiming for contradiction, that \( x < a_0 \), then setting \( \varepsilon = a_0 - x > 0 \) yields an element \( a \in A \) with \( a > a_0 - \varepsilon = x \), contradicting the assumption that \( x \) is an upper bound. Therefore, \( a_0 \leq x \) for every upper bound \( x \), confirming that \( a_0 \) is the supremum of \( A \). Combining these implications establishes the desired equivalence and completes the proof.
