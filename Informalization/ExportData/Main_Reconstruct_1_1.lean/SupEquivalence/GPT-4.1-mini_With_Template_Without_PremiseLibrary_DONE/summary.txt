We will show that \( a_0 \) is the supremum of the set \( A \) if and only if \( a_0 \) is an upper bound of \( A \) and for every positive number \( \varepsilon \), there exists an element \( a \in A \) such that \( a > a_0 - \varepsilon \). First, assume that \( a_0 \) is the supremum of \( A \). By definition, \( a_0 \) is an upper bound of \( A \). To prove the approximation property, suppose for contradiction that there is some \( \varepsilon > 0 \) such that no element of \( A \) exceeds \( a_0 - \varepsilon \). Then \( a_0 - \varepsilon \) would be an upper bound smaller than \( a_0 \), contradicting the minimality of \( a_0 \) as the least upper bound. Hence, for every positive \( \varepsilon \), there must be an element in \( A \) greater than \( a_0 - \varepsilon \). Conversely, suppose \( a_0 \) is an upper bound of \( A \) and that for every \( \varepsilon > 0 \), there exists an \( a \in A \) with \( a > a_0 - \varepsilon \). Let \( x \) be any upper bound of \( A \), and assume toward a contradiction that \( x < a_0 \). Then \( \varepsilon = a_0 - x > 0 \) and by the approximation property, there exists some \( a \in A \) with \( a > a_0 - \varepsilon = x \), contradicting that \( x \) is an upper bound. Therefore, \( a_0 \leq x \) for every upper bound \( x \), showing that \( a_0 \) is the least upper bound, i.e., the supremum of \( A \). Combining both directions completes the proof.
