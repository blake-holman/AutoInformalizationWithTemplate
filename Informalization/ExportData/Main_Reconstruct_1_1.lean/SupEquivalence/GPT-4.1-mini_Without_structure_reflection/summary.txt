We will show that if a real number \( a_0 \) is the supremum of a nonempty set \( A \) bounded above, then \( a_0 \) is an upper bound of \( A \) and for every positive number \( \varepsilon \), there exists an element in \( A \) greater than \( a_0 - \varepsilon \); conversely, if \( a_0 \) is an upper bound of \( A \) with this property, then \( a_0 \) is the supremum of \( A \).

First, assume that \( a_0 = \sup A \). By definition of the supremum as the least upper bound, \( a_0 \) is an upper bound for \( A \), meaning every element \( a \in A \) satisfies \( a \leq a_0 \). To show that for every \( \varepsilon > 0 \), there exists an element \( a \) in \( A \) with \( a > a_0 - \varepsilon \), suppose on the contrary that there exists some positive \( \varepsilon \) such that no element of \( A \) exceeds \( a_0 - \varepsilon \). Then \( a_0 - \varepsilon \) would be an upper bound of \( A \) that is strictly less than \( a_0 \), contradicting the minimality of \( a_0 \) as supremum. Hence, such an element must exist for every positive \( \varepsilon \).

Conversely, assume that \( a_0 \) is an upper bound of \( A \) and for every positive \( \varepsilon \), there exists \( a \in A \) with \( a > a_0 - \varepsilon \). To show that \( a_0 = \sup A \), consider any upper bound \( x \) of \( A \). If \( a_0 \not\leq x \), then \( x < a_0 \). Setting \( \varepsilon = a_0 - x > 0 \), there exists \( a \in A \) with \( a > a_0 - \varepsilon = x \). But this contradicts \( x \) being an upper bound of \( A \), which requires \( a \leq x \). Therefore, \( a_0 \leq x \) for every upper bound \( x \), establishing \( a_0 \) as the least upper bound, i.e., the supremum of \( A \).

Thus, we have shown the equivalence: \( a_0 \) is the supremum of \( A \) if and only if \( a_0 \) is an upper bound of \( A \) and for every positive \( \varepsilon \), there exists an element of \( A \) greater than \( a_0 - \varepsilon \).
