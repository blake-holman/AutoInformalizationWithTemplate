We will show that the supremum of the set \( A \) equals \( a_0 \) if and only if \( a_0 \) is an upper bound of \( A \) and for every positive number \( \varepsilon \), there exists an element of \( A \) exceeding \( a_0 - \varepsilon \). First, by the definition of supremum, if \( a_0 = \sup A \), then \( a_0 \) is clearly an upper bound of \( A \). To prove that elements of \( A \) approach \( a_0 \) from below, suppose, for the sake of contradiction, that there exists some positive \( \varepsilon \) such that no element of \( A \) is greater than \( a_0 - \varepsilon \). This would imply that every element of \( A \) is less than or equal to \( a_0 - \varepsilon \), making \( a_0 - \varepsilon \) an upper bound of \( A \). This contradicts the minimality of \( a_0 \) as the least upper bound, since it would mean \( a_0 \leq a_0 - \varepsilon \), an impossibility for positive \( \varepsilon \). Hence, for every \( \varepsilon > 0 \), there exists an element of \( A \) greater than \( a_0 - \varepsilon \). Conversely, assume that \( a_0 \) is an upper bound of \( A \) and that for every positive \( \varepsilon \), there is an element \( a \in A \) with \( a > a_0 - \varepsilon \). To show \( a_0 = \sup A \), consider any upper bound \( x \) of \( A \) and suppose, seeking contradiction, that \( x < a_0 \). Then \( \varepsilon = a_0 - x > 0 \), and by assumption there exists \( a \in A \) such that \( a > a_0 - \varepsilon = x \). This contradicts the fact that \( x \) is an upper bound of \( A \). Therefore, no upper bound of \( A \) can be less than \( a_0 \), and since \( a_0 \) is itself an upper bound, it follows that \( a_0 = \sup A \). This completes the proof of the equivalence.
