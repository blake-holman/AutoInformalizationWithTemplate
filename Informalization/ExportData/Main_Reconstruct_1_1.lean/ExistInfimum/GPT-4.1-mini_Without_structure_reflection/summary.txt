We will show that the sets \( B \), consisting of all real numbers that are lower bounds of \( C \), and its complement \( A \), together form a Dedekind cut of the real numbers. To this end, we first prove that both \( B \) and \( A \) are nonempty: since \( C \) is bounded below, \( B \) is nonempty by assumption, and to prove \( A \) is nonempty, we construct an element \( z \) greater than some \( c \in C \) but not a lower bound of \( C \), showing \( z \in A \). Next, we establish that the intersection \( B \cap A \) is empty because \( A \) is the complement of \( B \). We then show their union covers all real numbers due to the definitions of \( A \) and \( B \). Finally, we prove that every element of \( B \) is less than every element of \( A \) by contradiction: if some \( a \in A \) were less than or equal to some \( b \in B \), then \( a \) would also be a lower bound, contradicting that \( a \in A \).

Invoking the continuity axiom applied to this Dedekind cut, we deduce that either \( B \) has a greatest element or \( A \) has a least element. In the first case, let \( x \in B \) be such a greatest lower bound; by definition, \( x \) is a lower bound of \( C \) and any other lower bound is less than or equal to \( x \), so \( x \) is the infimum of \( C \). In the second case, let \( x \in A \) be the least element; we show \( x \notin B \), so \( x \) is not a lower bound of \( C \), meaning some \( c \in C \) satisfies \( c < x \). Defining \( z = \frac{x + c}{2} \), which lies strictly between \( c \) and \( x \), we verify \( z \in A \) as it is not a lower bound, but then \( z < x \), contradicting the minimality of \( x \) in \( A \). This contradiction settles the existence of the greatest lower bound, completing the proof that the set \( C \) indeed has an infimum derived from the Dedekind cut formed by \( B \) and \( A \).
