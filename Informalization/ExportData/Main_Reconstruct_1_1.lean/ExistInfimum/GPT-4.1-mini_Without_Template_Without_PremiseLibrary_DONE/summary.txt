We will show that the sets \( B \) and \( A \), defined respectively as the collection of all lower bounds of a nonempty set \( C \subseteq \mathbb{R} \) and its complement within the real numbers, form a Dedekind cut and consequently establish the existence of the greatest lower bound (infimum) of \( C \). First, we confirm that \( B \) is nonempty because \( C \) is bounded below, guaranteeing at least one lower bound. Next, we prove that \( A \) is also nonempty by considering \( c \in C \) and selecting any real number greater than \( c \), which by definition is not a lower bound and thus belongs to \( A \). We then verify that \( A \) and \( B \) are disjoint—since \( A \) is the complement of \( B \) in the reals—and that their union covers the entire real line, establishing a partition. To show that every element of \( B \) is strictly less than every element of \( A \), we assume the contrary and derive a contradiction by demonstrating that such an element in \( A \) would also have to be in \( B \), violating the disjointness of the sets. Having established that \( (B, A) \) constitute a Dedekind cut, the continuity property of the real numbers implies that either \( B \) has a greatest element or \( A \) has a least element. If \( B \) has a maximum element \( x \), then \( x \) is the greatest lower bound of \( C \), as no lower bound can exceed \( x \). Alternatively, assuming \( A \) has a minimum element \( x \), analysis leads to a contradiction showing that \( x \) cannot be in \( A \), implying that such a minimal element does not exist and reinforcing the existence of the infimum in \( B \). Thus, the set \( C \) admits an infimum, completing the proof.
