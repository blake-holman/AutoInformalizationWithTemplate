We will show that every nonempty set \( C \subseteq \mathbb{R} \) that is bounded below has an infimum. Let \( c \in C \) and define the set \( B \) to be the collection of all lower bounds of \( C \). Consider the set \( A \), the complement of \( B \) in the real numbers. Our goal is to demonstrate that \( (B, A) \) forms a Dedekind cut.

First, we verify the defining properties of a Dedekind cut: both \( B \) and \( A \) are nonempty, their union covers all real numbers, their intersection is empty, and every element of \( B \) is strictly less than every element of \( A \). To show \( A \neq \emptyset \), observe that since \( c \in C \), the number \( c + 1 \) is greater than \( c \) and cannot be a lower bound of \( C \), hence it lies in \( A \). The intersection \( B \cap A \) is empty because \( A \) is defined as the complement of \( B \). Their union equals the entire set of real numbers by the definition of complement. Lastly, suppose for contradiction there exist \( a \in A \) and \( b \in B \) such that \( b \leq a \); then \( a \) would also be a lower bound of \( C \), contradicting \( a \in A \). Thus, every element of \( B \) is less than every element of \( A \).

By the continuity axiom for Dedekind cuts, either \( B \) has a maximum element or \( A \) has a minimum element. In the first case, if \( x \in B \) is the greatest lower bound of \( C \), then \( x \) is the infimum, as it is a lower bound and no other lower bound exceeds it. In the second case, suppose \( x \in A \) is the minimum element of \( A \); then \( x \notin B \), implying there is some \( c \in C \) with \( c < x \). Defining \( z = \frac{x + c}{2} \), we find \( z \in A \) with \( z < x \), contradicting the minimality of \( x \). Hence, this case cannot occur.

Therefore, the set of lower bounds \( B \) has a greatest element \( x \), which serves as the infimum of \( C \). This completes the proof that every nonempty set bounded below has an infimum.
