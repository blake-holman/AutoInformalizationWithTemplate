We will show that the sets \( B \) and \( A \), defined as the set of all lower bounds of a nonempty set \( C \subseteq \mathbb{R} \) and its complement respectively, form a Dedekind cut, which provides the framework to prove the existence of the greatest lower bound (infimum) of \( C \). Since \( C \) is nonempty, we select an element \( c \in C \), which serves as a lower bound, ensuring \( B \) is nonempty. To show \( A \) is nonempty, we consider any real number greater than \( c \), such as \( c + 1 \), and note that it cannot be a lower bound of \( C \) because it is greater than some element of \( C \); hence, \( A \) is also nonempty. By construction, \( A \) and \( B \) are disjoint sets whose union covers the entire real line. Assuming for contradiction that there exist \( a \in A \) and \( b \in B \) with \( a \leq b \), it follows that \( a \) must be a lower bound of \( C \) (since \( b \) is), contradicting the definition of \( A \). Therefore, every element of \( B \) is strictly less than every element of \( A \), confirming that \((B, A)\) satisfies the properties of a Dedekind cut. Applying the Dedekind completeness property, this cut admits either a greatest element \( x \in B \) or a least element \( x \in A \). In the first case, \( x \) is the maximum of all lower bounds of \( C \), and hence the infimum. In the second case, assuming \( x \) is the minimal element of \( A \), a contradiction argument involving the midpoint between \( x \) and an element of \( C \) shows that \( x \) must be a lower bound of \( C \), and thus is the greatest lower bound. Therefore, the set \( C \) has an infimum, completing the proof.
