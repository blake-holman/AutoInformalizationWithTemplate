We will show that the pair of sets \(B\) and \(A\), defined respectively as the set of all lower bounds of a set \(C\) and its complement in the real numbers, forms a Dedekind cut. First, we establish that \(B\) is nonempty by assuming that \(C\) is bounded below, and we show \(A\) is nonempty by exhibiting an element \(z\) greater than some \(c \in C\) that fails to be a lower bound, hence belongs to \(A\). Next, we note that by construction, the intersection \(B \cap A\) is empty, as \(A\) complements \(B\). Moreover, their union equals the entire real line, since every real number either is a lower bound of \(C\) or is not. To complete the Dedekind cut verification, we prove the ordering property: for any \(b \in B\) and \(a \in A\), it holds that \(b < a\). This is shown by contradiction—if \(a \leq b\), then \(a\) would also be a lower bound and thus belong to \(B\), contradicting \(a \in A\). Having established the Dedekind cut properties, we invoke the continuity axiom to assert that either \(B\) has a maximum element or \(A\) has a minimum element. Using these, a greatest lower bound of \(C\) exists. Specifically, if \(B\) has a maximum \(x\), then \(x\) is the greatest lower bound. Alternatively, if \(A\) has a minimum \(x\), we argue by contradiction using the midpoint \(z = \frac{c + x}{2}\) (for some \(c \in C\) with \(c < x\)) that this minimality fails, ensuring the existence of the greatest lower bound. Thus, the set \(C\) admits a greatest lower bound, completing the proof.
