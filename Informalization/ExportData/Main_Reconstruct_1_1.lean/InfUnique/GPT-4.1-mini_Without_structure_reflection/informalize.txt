[appliedTactic]
intro aInf  bInf 
[answer]
Assume that \( a \) and \( b \) are both infimums of the set \( S \). From now on, we will show that \( a = b \).
[appliedTactic]
rw [Infimum] at  * 
[answer]
By using the definition that a real number is the infimum of a set if it is the greatest lower bound less than or equal to every element in the set, the assumptions that \(a\) and \(b\) are infima of the set become the statements that \(a\) and \(b\) are lower bounds of the set and that any other lower bound is less than or equal to them, while the goal of proving \(a = b\) remains unchanged.
[appliedTactic]
apply le_antisymm (bInf.right a aInf.left) (aInf.right b bInf.left ) 
[answer]
From the theorem that two elements are equal if each is less than or equal to the other, and from the assumptions that \( a \) and \( b \) are lower bounds of the set \( S \) with \( a \) being the greatest lower bound less than or equal to any other lower bound and similarly for \( b \), in order to establish that \( a = b \), it suffices to prove no further goals as the equality follows directly.
