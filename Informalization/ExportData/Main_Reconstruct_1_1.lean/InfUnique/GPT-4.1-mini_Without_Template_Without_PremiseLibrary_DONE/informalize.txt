[answer]
Assume that \( a \) is a greatest lower bound of the set \( S \) and that \( b \) is also a greatest lower bound of the set \( S \). We will now show that \( a \) and \( b \) are equal.
[appliedTactic]
rw [Infimum] at  * 
[answer]
By unfolding the definition of the greatest lower bound, the assumptions that \(a\) and \(b\) are the greatest lower bounds of the set \(S\) are expressed as \(a\) and \(b\) each being lower bounds of \(S\) such that any other lower bound of \(S\) is less than or equal to them, respectively. This reformulates the original assumptions into explicit conditions about lower bounds and their minimality.
[appliedTactic]
apply le_antisymm (bInf.right a aInf.left) (aInf.right b bInf.left ) 
[answer]
To prove that \( a \) equals \( b \), it suffices to show that \( a \) is less than or equal to \( b \) and that \( b \) is less than or equal to \( a \). Using the fact that both \( a \) and \( b \) are greatest lower bounds of the set \( S \), we establish these inequalities by applying the respective properties that characterize \( a \) and \( b \) as lower bounds and their minimality. This completes the proof that \( a = b \).
