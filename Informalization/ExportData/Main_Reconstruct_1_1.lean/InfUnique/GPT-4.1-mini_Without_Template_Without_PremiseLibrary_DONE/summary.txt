We will show that if a set \( S \) has two greatest lower bounds \( a \) and \( b \), then these must be equal. By the definition of greatest lower bound, both \( a \) and \( b \) are lower bounds of \( S \) such that any other lower bound of \( S \) is less than or equal to them. To prove that \( a = b \), it suffices to demonstrate the inequalities \( a \leq b \) and \( b \leq a \). Since \( a \) is a greatest lower bound, it must be less than or equal to every other lower bound, including \( b \). Similarly, because \( b \) is a greatest lower bound, it must satisfy \( b \leq a \). Combining these inequalities yields \( a = b \), completing the proof.
