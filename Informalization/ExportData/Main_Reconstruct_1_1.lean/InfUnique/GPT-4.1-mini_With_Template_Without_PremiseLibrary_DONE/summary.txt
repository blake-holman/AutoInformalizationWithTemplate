We will show that if \( a \) and \( b \) are both infimums of the set \( S \), then \( a = b \). By definition, since \( a \) is the infimum of \( S \), it is a lower bound of \( S \) and any other lower bound of \( S \) is less than or equal to \( a \). Similarly, \( b \) is also a lower bound with the analogous property. To conclude that \( a = b \), we use the antisymmetry property of the order relation, which states that if \( a \leq b \) and \( b \leq a \), then \( a = b \). Since both \( a \) and \( b \) are lower bounds that are minimal with respect to other lower bounds, it follows that \( a \leq b \) and \( b \leq a \), and hence \( a = b \), establishing the uniqueness of the infimum.
