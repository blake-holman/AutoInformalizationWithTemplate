[appliedTactic]
intro aInf  bInf 
[answer]
Assume that \( a \) and \( b \) are both infimums of the set \( S \). From now on, we will show that \( a = b \).
[appliedTactic]
rw [Infimum] at  * 
[answer]
By using the definition that a real number is the infimum of a set if and only if it is a lower bound of the set and any other lower bound is less than or equal to it, the assumptions that \(a\) and \(b\) are infima of the set \(S\) become the assumptions that \(a\) and \(b\) are lower bounds of \(S\) and that any lower bound of \(S\) is less than or equal to \(a\) and \(b\), respectively, while the goal of proving \(a = b\) remains unchanged.
[appliedTactic]
apply le_antisymm (bInf.right a aInf.left) (aInf.right b bInf.left ) 
[answer]
From the fact that in a partial order, two elements are equal if each is less than or equal to the other, and given that \( a \) and \( b \) are both lower bounds of the set \( S \) with the property that any lower bound is less than or equal to them respectively, in order to establish that \( a = b \), it suffices to prove the inequalities showing \( a \leq b \) and \( b \leq a \) using these properties.
