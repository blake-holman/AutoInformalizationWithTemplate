We will show that if two real numbers \( a \) and \( b \) both serve as the infimum of the same set \( S \), then \( a = b \). By the definition of infimum, since \( a \) and \( b \) are each the infimum of \( S \), they are both lower bounds of \( S \), and any other lower bound of \( S \) must be less than or equal to \( a \) and to \( b \), respectively. To prove that \( a = b \), it suffices to show that \( a \leq b \) and \( b \leq a \), since equality in a partial order follows from mutual bounding. Using the properties of the infimum, we observe that \( a \), being a lower bound, must be less than or equal to any other lower bound, including \( b \), giving \( a \leq b \). Similarly, \( b \) must be less than or equal to any other lower bound, including \( a \), yielding \( b \leq a \). Combining these two inequalities, we conclude that \( a = b \), completing the proof.
