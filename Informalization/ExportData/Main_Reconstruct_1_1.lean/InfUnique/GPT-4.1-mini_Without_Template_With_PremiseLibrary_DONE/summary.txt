We will show that if \( a \) and \( b \) are both greatest lower bounds (infimums) of a set \( S \), then \( a = b \). By the definition of the infimum, \( a \) and \( b \) are lower bounds of \( S \), and any other lower bound of \( S \) is less than or equal to both \( a \) and \( b \). To prove equality, it suffices to show that \( a \leq b \) and \( b \leq a \). Since \( a \) is a greatest lower bound, it must be less than or equal to any other lower bound, including \( b \), so \( a \leq b \). Similarly, \( b \leq a \) by the same reasoning. By antisymmetry of the order relation, these inequalities imply that \( a = b \), completing the proof.
