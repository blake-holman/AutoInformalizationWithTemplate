[appliedTactic]
intro aInf  bInf 
[answer]
Assume that \( a \) is a greatest lower bound of the set \( S \) and that \( b \) is also a greatest lower bound of the set \( S \). Under these assumptions, we will prove that \( a \) and \( b \) are equal.
[appliedTactic]
rw [Infimum] at  * 
[answer]
By unfolding the definition of the infimum, each assumption that a and b are infimums of the set S is replaced by the statement that a and b are lower bounds of S and that any other lower bound of S is less than or equal to a and b respectively. This reformulates the assumptions in terms of lower bounds and their maximality, while the goal remains to prove that a equals b.
[appliedTactic]
apply le_antisymm (bInf.right a aInf.left) (aInf.right b bInf.left ) 
[answer]
To prove that \( a \) equals \( b \), it suffices to show that \( a \) is less than or equal to \( b \) and that \( b \) is less than or equal to \( a \). Using the properties that \( a \) and \( b \) are both greatest lower bounds of the set \( S \), we establish these inequalities by applying the corresponding bounds from each element’s defining conditions. This confirms the equality of \( a \) and \( b \) by the antisymmetry property of the order relation.
