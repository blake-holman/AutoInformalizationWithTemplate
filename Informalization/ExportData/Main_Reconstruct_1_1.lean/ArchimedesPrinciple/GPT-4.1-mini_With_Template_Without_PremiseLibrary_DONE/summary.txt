We will show that for positive real numbers \( a \) and \( b \), there exists a natural number \( n \) such that \((n + 1) \cdot a > b\). Suppose, for contradiction, that for every natural number \( n \), the inequality \((n + 1) \cdot a \leq b\) holds. Consider the set \( C \) of all real numbers \( s \) for which there exists a natural number \( k \) such that \( s = (k + 1) \cdot a \). We observe that \( C \) is nonempty since \( a = (0 + 1) \cdot a \in C \). Moreover, by our assumption, every element \( s \in C \) satisfies \( s \leq b \), establishing that \( b \) is an upper bound of \( C \). Since \( C \) is nonempty and bounded above, it admits a least upper bound, say \( x = \sup C \).

We next show that for any natural number \( n \), \((n + 2) \cdot a\) belongs to \( C \), since \((n + 2) \cdot a = ((n + 1) + 1) \cdot a\). By the definition of the supremum, it follows that \((n + 2) \cdot a \leq x\) for all \( n \). From this, we rewrite the inequality as \((n + 1) \cdot a + a \leq x\), which implies \((n + 1) \cdot a \leq x - a\).

We then demonstrate that \( x - a \) is an upper bound of \( C \). For any element \( l = (k + 1) \cdot a \in C \), this inequality shows \( l \leq x - a \). By the supremum property, any upper bound \( y \) of \( C \) satisfies \( x \leq y \), so in particular \( x \leq x - a \).

However, since \( a > 0 \), the inequality \( x \leq x - a \) is impossible, yielding a contradiction. Therefore, our initial assumption must be false, and there exists a natural number \( n \) such that \((n + 1) \cdot a > b\), completing the proof.
