We will show that for any real number \( x \) and any positive real number \( \varepsilon \), there exists a rational number \( q \) such that the distance between \( x \) and \( q \) is less than \( \varepsilon \). First, consider the case when \( x > 0 \). By the Archimedean property, there exists a natural number \( n \) such that the reciprocal \( \frac{1}{n+1} \) is less than \( \varepsilon \). We then define the set \( M \) of natural numbers \( m \) such that the rational number \( \frac{m+1}{n+1} \) exceeds \( x \). This set is non-empty since, by the Archimedean property, such an \( m \) exists. Let \( m \) be the smallest element of \( M \), and define \( q = \frac{m+1}{n+1} \). It follows that \( q > x \) and we aim to show that \( |x - q| < \varepsilon \).

We observe that \( x - q < 0 \), so \( |x - q| = q - x \). Since \( \varepsilon > \frac{1}{n+1} \), it suffices to show that \( q - x \leq \frac{1}{n+1} \). By rewriting, this inequality is equivalent to showing \( \frac{m}{n+1} \leq x \). Assuming the contrary leads to a contradiction by analyzing the minimality of \( m \). If \( m \geq 1 \), then \( m - 1 \in M \), contradicting the minimality of \( m \). If \( m = 0 \), then \( x < 0 \), contradicting the assumption that \( x > 0 \). Thus, \( \frac{m}{n+1} \leq x \) must hold, ensuring \( |x - q| < \varepsilon \).

For \( x = 0 \), choosing \( q = 0 \) obviously satisfies \( |x - q| = 0 < \varepsilon \). For \( x < 0 \), consider the positive number \( -x \) and apply the previously established argument to find a rational number \( q \) with \( |(-x) - q| < \varepsilon \). Then \( -q \) approximates \( x \) within \( \varepsilon \). Hence, in all cases, there exists a rational number \( q \) such that the distance between \( x \) and \( q \) is less than \( \varepsilon \), completing the proof.
