We will show that a sequence constantly equal to a real number \( a \) converges to \( a \). Let \( \varepsilon > 0 \) be given. We aim to find a natural number \( n_0 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_0 \), the absolute difference between the sequence term at \( n \) and \( a \) is less than \( \varepsilon \). Since the sequence is constant, choosing \( n_0 = 1 \) suffices. For any \( n \geq 1 \), the sequence term equals \( a \), so the absolute difference is \( |a - a| = 0 \). Because \( 0 < \varepsilon \), this inequality holds for all such \( n \). Hence, by the definition of convergence, the constant sequence converges to \( a \).
