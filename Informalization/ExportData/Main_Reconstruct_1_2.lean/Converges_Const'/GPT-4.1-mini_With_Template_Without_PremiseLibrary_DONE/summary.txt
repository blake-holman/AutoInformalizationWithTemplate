We will show that for any positive real number \(\varepsilon\), there exists a natural number \(n_0\) such that for all \(n \geq n_0\), the absolute difference between the constant sequence and \(a\) is less than \(\varepsilon\). To prove this, it suffices to demonstrate that for all \(n \geq 1\), the absolute difference \(|f(n) - a|\) is less than \(\varepsilon\). Since the sequence is constant and equals \(a\) for every \(n\), the difference simplifies to \(|a - a| = 0\). Because the absolute value of zero is zero, it follows that \(|f(n) - a| = 0 < \varepsilon\) for any \(\varepsilon > 0\). Thus, choosing \(n_0 = 1\) completes the proof.
