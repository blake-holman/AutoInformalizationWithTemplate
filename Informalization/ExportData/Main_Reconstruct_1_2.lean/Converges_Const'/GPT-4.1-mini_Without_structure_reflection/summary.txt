We will show that for any positive real number \( \varepsilon \), there exists a natural number \( n_0 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_0 \), the absolute difference between the function value at \( n \) and \( a \) is less than \( \varepsilon \). To establish this, it suffices to prove that for all natural numbers \( n \geq 1 \), the absolute difference is less than \( \varepsilon \). Assuming such an \( n \), we observe that the absolute value of the difference between the function evaluated at \( n \) and \( a \) is in fact zero, since subtracting a number from itself yields zero, and the absolute value of zero is zero. Consequently, showing this absolute difference is less than \( \varepsilon \) reduces to demonstrating that zero is less than \( \varepsilon \), which follows directly from the assumption that \( \varepsilon \) is positive. Thus, the proof is complete.
