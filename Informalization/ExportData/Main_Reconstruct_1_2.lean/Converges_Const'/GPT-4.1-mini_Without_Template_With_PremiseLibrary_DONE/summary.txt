We will show that for any positive real number \( \varepsilon \), there exists a natural number \( n_0 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_0 \), the absolute difference between the value of the constant function at \( n \) and \( a \) is less than \( \varepsilon \). Choosing \( n_0 = 1 \), it suffices to consider all \( n \geq 1 \) and show that this inequality holds. For an arbitrary such \( n \), the difference between the constant function evaluated at \( n \) and \( a \) is zero since the constant function equals \( a \) for all \( n \), implying the absolute value of their difference is zero. Therefore, the desired inequality reduces to showing that \( 0 < \varepsilon \), which is true by the positivity of \( \varepsilon \). Hence, the definition is satisfied, completing the proof.
