We will show that any real sequence \( (x_n) \) converging to a limit \( a \) is bounded. By the definition of convergence, choosing \(\varepsilon = 1\) ensures the existence of an index \( n_0 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_0 \), the terms satisfy \(|x_n - a| < 1\). From this inequality and the reverse triangle inequality, it follows that \(|\,|x_n| - |a|\,| \leq |x_n - a| < 1\), which implies \(|x_n| < |a| + 1\) for all \( n \geq n_0 \).

Next, consider the finite set \( Z \) composed of the absolute values of the sequence terms with indices less than \( n_0 \), together with the number \( |a| + 1 \). By construction, this set is nonempty, as it contains \( |a| + 1 \). Let \( M \) denote the maximum element of this set \( Z \).

To establish boundedness, we verify that for every natural number \( n \), the absolute value \( |x_n| \) is less than or equal to \( M \). If \( n \geq n_0 \), then \(|x_n| < |a| + 1 \leq M\). If \( n < n_0 \), then \(|x_n|\) is an element of \( Z \), so \(|x_n| \leq M\) by the maximality of \( M \). Together, these cases show that \( |x_n| \leq M \) for all \( n \).

Finally, interpreting the boundedness in terms of arbitrary elements \( a = |x_n| \) of the set of absolute values of the sequence, the previous argument confirms that each such \( a \) satisfies \( a \leq M \). Therefore, the sequence \( (x_n) \) is bounded.
