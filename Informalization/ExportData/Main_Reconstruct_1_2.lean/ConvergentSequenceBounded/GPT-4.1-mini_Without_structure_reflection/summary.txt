We will show that if a sequence \( (x_n) \) of real numbers converges to a real number \( a \), then \( (x_n) \) is bounded. By the definition of convergence, there exists a natural number \( n_0 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_0 \), the distance between \( x_n \) and \( a \) is less than 1. Using the triangle inequality and the fact that the absolute value difference satisfies \( |\,|x_n| - |a|\,| \leq |x_n - a| \), it follows that for all \( n \geq n_0 \), \( |x_n| < |a| + 1 \).

Define the finite set \( Z \) consisting of the absolute values \( |x_n| \) for all \( n < n_0 \) together with the number \( |a| + 1 \). This set is nonempty since \( |a| + 1 \in Z \). Let \( M \) be the maximum element of \( Z \). We now show that for every natural number \( n \), \( |x_n| \leq M \). 

If \( n \geq n_0 \), then \( |x_n| < |a| + 1 \leq M \) by definition of \( M \) as the maximum element of \( Z \). For \( n < n_0 \), since \( |x_n| \in Z \), it follows that \( |x_n| \leq M \) by the maximality of \( M \). Therefore, \( M \) serves as a uniform upper bound for the sequence \( (|x_n|) \), proving that \( (x_n) \) is bounded.
