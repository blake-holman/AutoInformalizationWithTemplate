We will show that every convergent sequence of real numbers is bounded. Let \( (x_n) \) be a sequence converging to some real number \( a \). By the definition of convergence, for \(\epsilon = 1\), there exists a natural number \( n_0 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_0 \), we have \( |x_n - a| < 1 \). Using the triangle inequality, it follows that for all such \( n \),
\[
|x_n| \leq |a| + |x_n - a| < |a| + 1.
\]
Now, consider the finite set 
\[
Z = \{ |x_k| : k < n_0 \} \cup \{ |a| + 1 \}.
\]
This set is non-empty since it contains \( |a| + 1 \), and thus admits a maximum element \( M = \max(Z) \). For \( n \geq n_0 \), we have \( |x_n| < |a| + 1 \leq M \), and for \( n < n_0 \), by definition of \( Z \), \( |x_n| \leq M \). Therefore, for every natural number \( n \), \( |x_n| \leq M \). Hence, the sequence \( (x_n) \) is bounded by \( M \). This completes the proof.
