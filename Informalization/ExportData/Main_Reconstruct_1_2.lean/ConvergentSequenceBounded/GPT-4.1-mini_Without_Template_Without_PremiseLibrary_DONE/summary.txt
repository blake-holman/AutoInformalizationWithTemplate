We will show that any sequence \( x = (x_n) \) of real numbers that converges to a real number \( a \) is bounded. Since \( x \) converges to \( a \), by definition of convergence, for the positive number 1 there exists a natural number \( n_0 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_0 \), the distance between \( x_n \) and \( a \) satisfies \(|x_n - a| < 1\). Consequently, for every \( n \geq n_0 \), applying the reverse triangle inequality gives 
\[
\big|\,|x_n| - |a|\,\big| \leq |x_n - a| < 1,
\]
which implies
\[
|x_n| < |a| + 1.
\]

Next, define the finite set 
\[
Z = \{ |x_0|, |x_1|, \dots, |x_{n_0 - 1}| \} \cup \{ |a| + 1 \}
\]
to collect the absolute values of the first \( n_0 \) terms along with the bound \( |a| + 1 \). This set is nonempty since it contains at least \( |a| + 1 \). Let \( M = \max Z \) be the maximum element of \( Z \).

We now show that \( M \) bounds the entire sequence. If \( n \geq n_0 \), then by construction we have \( |x_n| < |a| + 1 \leq M \). If \( n < n_0 \), then \( |x_n| \in Z \), so \( |x_n| \leq M \). Combining these cases, for every natural number \( n \), \( |x_n| \leq M \). 

Thus, \( M \) serves as a uniform bound for the absolute values of all terms of the sequence, proving that the convergent sequence \( x \) is bounded.
