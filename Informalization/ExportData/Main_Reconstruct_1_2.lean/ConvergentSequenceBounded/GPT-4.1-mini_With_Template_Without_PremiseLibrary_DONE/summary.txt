We will show that every convergent sequence \( (x_n) \) of real numbers with limit \( a \) is bounded. Since \( x_n \to a \), for \(\varepsilon = 1\) there exists a natural number \( n_0 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_0 \), we have \(|x_n - a| < 1\). By the reverse triangle inequality, this implies that \(|\,|x_n| - |a|\,| \leq |x_n - a| < 1\), and hence \(|x_n| < |a| + 1\) for all \( n \geq n_0 \).

Next, consider the finite set 
\[
Z = \{ |x_k| : k < n_0 \} \cup \{ |a| + 1 \},
\]
which is nonempty since it explicitly contains the element \( |a| + 1 \). Let \( M = \max(Z) \).

For any \( n \), if \( n \geq n_0 \), then \( |x_n| < |a| + 1 \leq M \), and if \( n < n_0 \), then \( |x_n| \in Z \), so \( |x_n| \leq M \) by the definition of maximum. Therefore, for all \( n \), \( |x_n| \leq M \), showing that the sequence \( (x_n) \) is bounded by \( M \).

This completes the proof.
