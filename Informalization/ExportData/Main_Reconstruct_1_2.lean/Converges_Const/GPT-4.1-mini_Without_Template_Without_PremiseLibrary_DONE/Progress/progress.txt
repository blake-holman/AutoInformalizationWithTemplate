We will show that for every natural number \( n \), the absolute value of the difference between \( x(n) \) and \( a \) is zero. To do this, fix an arbitrary natural number \( n \). Since \( x(n) = a \), it follows that the difference \( x(n) - a \) is equal to zero. Taking the absolute value of this difference yields \( |x(n) - a| = |0| = 0 \). Therefore, for every natural number \( n \), the absolute value \( |x(n) - a| \) is zero, completing the proof.

    intro ε εpos n

[answer]
We will show that for every positive number \( \varepsilon \) and every natural number \( n \), the absolute difference between the sequence term \( x_n \) and the limit \( a \) is less than \( \varepsilon \). Fix an arbitrary positive \( \varepsilon \) and an arbitrary natural number \( n \). Since the sequence is constant and equal to \( a \) at every term, it follows that \( x_n = a \), and thus the difference \( x_n - a \) is zero. Taking the absolute value, we have \( |x_n - a| = 0 \). Therefore, for all \( n \), the distance between \( x_n \) and \( a \) is zero. Substituting this into the inequality, we see that \( 0 < \varepsilon \) holds by the positivity of \( \varepsilon \). This completes the proof that for every \( n \), \( |x_n - a| < \varepsilon \).
