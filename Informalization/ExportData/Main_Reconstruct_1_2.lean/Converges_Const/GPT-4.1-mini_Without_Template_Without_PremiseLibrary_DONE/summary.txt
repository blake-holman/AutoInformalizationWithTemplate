We will show that if the sequence \( x \) is constant and equal to \( a \) for every natural number \( n \), then \( x \) converges to \( a \). To do this, let \(\varepsilon > 0\) be arbitrary and fix any natural number \( n \). Since the sequence is constant, \( x_n = a \) for all \( n \), so \(|x_n - a| = 0 < \varepsilon\). Next, given any positive \(\varepsilon\), we choose \( n_0 = 1 \) and observe that for all \( n \geq n_0 \), the inequality \(|x_n - a| < \varepsilon\) holds by the same reasoning. Thus, by the definition of convergence, the constant sequence \( x \) converges to \( a \), completing the proof.
