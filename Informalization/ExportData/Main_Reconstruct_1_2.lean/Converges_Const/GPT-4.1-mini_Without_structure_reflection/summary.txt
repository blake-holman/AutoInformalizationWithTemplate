We will show that the constant sequence \( (x_n) \), defined by \( x_n = a \) for every natural number \( n \), converges to \( a \). To this end, let \( \varepsilon > 0 \) be arbitrary. Since the sequence is constant, for every \( n \in \mathbb{N} \), we have \(|x_n - a| = |a - a| = 0\). Consequently, \(|x_n - a| < \varepsilon\) holds for all \( n \). This shows that for any positive \( \varepsilon \), the terms of the sequence are within distance \( \varepsilon \) of \( a \), and thus the sequence converges to \( a \). Formally, choosing \( n_0 = 1 \) guarantees that for all \( n \geq n_0 \), \(|x_n - a| < \varepsilon\), completing the proof.
