We will show that if a sequence \( (x_n) \) is constant and equal to \( a \) for every natural number \( n \), then \( (x_n) \) converges to \( a \). To do this, let \( \varepsilon > 0 \) be arbitrary and let \( n \) be any natural number. Since \( x_n = a \) for all \( n \), it follows that \( |x_n - a| = |a - a| = 0 \), which is clearly less than any positive \( \varepsilon \). Therefore, for every \( \varepsilon > 0 \), there exists a natural number \( n_0 \), specifically \( n_0 = 1 \), such that for all \( n \geq n_0 \), we have \( |x_n - a| < \varepsilon \). This shows that the sequence \( (x_n) \) converges to \( a \), completing the proof.
