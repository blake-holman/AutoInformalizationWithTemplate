We will show that a constant sequence \( (x_n) \), which takes the constant value \( a \) for every natural number \( n \), converges to \( a \). Let \( \varepsilon > 0 \) be arbitrary. Since each term of the sequence satisfies \( x_n = a \), it follows that \( |x_n - a| = |a - a| = 0 \), which is less than any positive number \( \varepsilon \). To establish convergence, we need to find a natural number \( n_0 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_0 \), the inequality \( |x_n - a| < \varepsilon \) holds. Choosing \( n_0 = 1 \) suffices, because for every \( n \geq 1 \), \( |x_n - a| = 0 < \varepsilon \). Hence, by the definition of convergence, the sequence \( (x_n) \) converges to \( a \).
