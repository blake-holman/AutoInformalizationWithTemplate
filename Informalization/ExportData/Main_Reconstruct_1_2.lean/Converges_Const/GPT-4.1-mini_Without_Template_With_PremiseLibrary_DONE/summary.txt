We will show that if a sequence \( x \) is constant and equal to some value \( a \) for every natural number \( n \), then \( x \) converges to \( a \). To prove this, let \(\varepsilon > 0\) be given arbitrarily. Since \( x(n) = a \) for all \( n \), it follows that \(|x(n) - a| = |a - a| = 0 < \varepsilon\) for every natural number \( n \). Hence, the sequence remains exactly at \( a \), so it is certainly within \(\varepsilon\) of \( a \) for all \( n \). Consequently, we can choose \( n_0 = 1 \), and for all \( n \geq n_0 \), the inequality \(|x(n) - a| < \varepsilon\) holds, demonstrating that \( x \) converges to \( a \). This completes the proof.
