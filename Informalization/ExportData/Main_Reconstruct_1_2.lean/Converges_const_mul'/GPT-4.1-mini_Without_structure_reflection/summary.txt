We will show that if the sequence \( (x_n) \) converges to \( a \), then the sequence \( (\alpha x_n) \) converges to \( \alpha a \). Let \(\varepsilon > 0\) be given. Our goal is to find a natural number \( n_0 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_0 \), the inequality \( |\alpha x_n - \alpha a| < \varepsilon \) holds. Since \( x_n \to a \), there exists \( n_0 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_0 \), we have \( |x_n - a| < \frac{\varepsilon}{|\alpha| + 1} \).

Now, consider any \( n \geq n_0 \). Using the properties of absolute values, we write 
\[
|\alpha x_n - \alpha a| = |\alpha| \cdot |x_n - a| \leq |\alpha| \cdot \frac{\varepsilon}{|\alpha| + 1}.
\]
We claim this quantity is less than \(\varepsilon\). To verify this, it suffices to show that 
\[
|\alpha| \cdot \frac{\varepsilon}{|\alpha| + 1} < \varepsilon,
\]
which, after rearrangement, requires that 
\[
|\alpha| < |\alpha| + 1,
\]
an inequality that is clearly true. 

If \( |\alpha| > 0 \), then multiplying both sides of \( |x_n - a| < \frac{\varepsilon}{|\alpha| + 1} \) by the positive number \( |\alpha| \) preserves the inequality, leading to the desired conclusion.

In the special case where \( |\alpha| = 0 \), i.e., \( \alpha = 0 \), the sequence \( (\alpha x_n) \) is identically zero and trivially converges to \( \alpha a = 0 \).

Therefore, in every case, we conclude that for all \( n \geq n_0 \), \( |\alpha x_n - \alpha a| < \varepsilon \), showing that \( \alpha x_n \to \alpha a \) as \( n \to \infty \), completing the proof.
