We will show that if a sequence \( (x_n) \) converges to a limit \( a \), then the sequence obtained by multiplying each term by a fixed scalar \(\alpha\) converges to \(\alpha a\). Let \(\varepsilon > 0\) be arbitrary. Since \( (x_n) \to a \), there exists a natural number \( n_0 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_0 \), the inequality \( |x_n - a| < \frac{\varepsilon}{|\alpha| + 1} \) holds. This choice is valid because \( |\alpha| + 1 > 0 \). Taking any \( n \geq n_0 \), we consider the difference \( |\alpha x_n - \alpha a| = |\alpha| \cdot |x_n - a| \). By the triangle inequality and the given bound, we have  
\[
|\alpha x_n - \alpha a| \leq |\alpha| \cdot \frac{\varepsilon}{|\alpha| + 1}.
\]  
We analyze two cases: if \( |\alpha| > 0 \), then dividing both sides by \( |\alpha| \) confirms \( |x_n - a| < \frac{\varepsilon}{|\alpha| + 1} \), ensuring the desired bound. If \( |\alpha| = 0 \), then \(\alpha = 0\) and the difference is zero, trivially less than \(\varepsilon\). Finally, the expression \( |\alpha| \cdot \frac{\varepsilon}{|\alpha| + 1} < \varepsilon \) holds since it reduces to showing \( |\alpha| < |\alpha| + 1 \), which is true. Hence, for all \( n \geq n_0 \), \( |\alpha x_n - \alpha a| < \varepsilon \), proving that \( (\alpha x_n) \) converges to \(\alpha a\).
