We will show that if a sequence \( x \) satisfies \( x(n) \geq a \) for every natural number \( n \), then the limit of \( x \) is at least \( a \). To this end, define the constant sequence \( y \) by \( y(n) = a \) for all \( n \). Clearly, for each \( n \), we have \( y(n) \leq x(n) \) by the given assumption. Since \( y \) is constant, it converges to \( a \), establishing that \( y \) is convergent with limit \( a \). Because both \( x \) and \( y \) are convergent sequences and \( y(n) \leq x(n) \) for all \( n \), it follows by comparison that the limit of \( y \) is less than or equal to the limit of \( x \). Therefore, the limit of \( x \) is at least \( a \), completing the proof.
