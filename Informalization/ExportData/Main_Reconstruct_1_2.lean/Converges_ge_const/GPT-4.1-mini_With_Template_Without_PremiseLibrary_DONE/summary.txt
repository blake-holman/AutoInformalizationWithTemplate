We will show that if a sequence \( x \) satisfies \( x(n) \geq a \) for every natural number \( n \), then its limit is at least \( a \). To this end, consider the constant sequence \( y \) defined by \( y(n) = a \) for all \( n \). By the assumption on \( x \), we have \( y(n) \leq x(n) \) for every \( n \). Since \( y \) is constant, it trivially converges to \( a \). Moreover, because \( y(n) \leq x(n) \) for all \( n \), the limit superior of \( y \) is less than or equal to the limit superior of \( x \). Given that \( y \) converges to \( a \), this implies that the limit of \( x \) is at least \( a \), completing the proof.
