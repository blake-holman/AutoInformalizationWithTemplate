We will show that if a sequence \( x \) satisfies \( x(n) \geq a \) for every natural number \( n \), then its limit is also at least \( a \). To this end, define a constant sequence \( y \) by setting \( y(n) = a \) for all \( n \). By the hypothesis, for each \( n \), \( y(n) = a \leq x(n) \), establishing that \( y \) is termwise less than or equal to \( x \). Since \( y \) is constant, it trivially converges to \( a \), confirming that \( y \) is convergent with limit \( a \). Given that both sequences converge and \( y(n) \leq x(n) \) for all \( n \), it follows that the limit of \( y \) is less than or equal to the limit of \( x \). Therefore, the limit of \( x \) must be at least \( a \), completing the proof.
