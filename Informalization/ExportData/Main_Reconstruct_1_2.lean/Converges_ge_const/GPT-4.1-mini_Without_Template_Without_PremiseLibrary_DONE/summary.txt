We will show that if a sequence \( x \) satisfies \( x(n) \geq a \) for every natural number \( n \), then the limit of \( x \) is also at least \( a \). To this end, define a constant sequence \( y \) by \( y(n) = a \) for all \( n \). By the initial assumption, it follows that \( y(n) \leq x(n) \) for every \( n \). Since \( y \) is constant, it trivially converges to \( a \). Because both sequences \( x \) and \( y \) converge and \( y(n) \leq x(n) \) for all \( n \), it follows that the limit of \( y \) is less than or equal to the limit of \( x \). Using the equivalence between the limit of a sequence and its sequential limit, this inequality establishes that the limit of \( x \) is at least \( a \), completing the proof.
