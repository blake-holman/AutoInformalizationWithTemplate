We will show that if a convergent real-valued sequence \( x \) is bounded below by a constant \( a \), then its limit is at least \( a \). To this end, define the constant sequence \( y \) such that \( y(n) = a \) for every natural number \( n \). By the assumption that \( x(n) \geq a \) for all \( n \), it follows directly that \( y(n) \leq x(n) \) for every \( n \). Since \( y \) is constant, it trivially converges to \( a \). Moreover, since both \( x \) and \( y \) are convergent sequences and \( y(n) \leq x(n) \) termwise, it follows from the order-preserving property of limits that the limit of \( y \), which is \( a \), is less than or equal to the limit of \( x \). Hence, the limit of \( x \) is at least \( a \), completing the proof.
