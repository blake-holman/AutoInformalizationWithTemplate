We will show that the sequence \((x_n + y_n)\), formed by adding two convergent sequences \((x_n)\) and \((y_n)\) with limits \(a\) and \(b\) respectively, converges to the sum \(a + b\). Let \(\varepsilon > 0\) be given. Since \(\varepsilon\) is positive, so is \(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\). By the convergence of \((x_n)\) to \(a\), there exists an index \(n_1\) such that for all \(n \geq n_1\), \(|x_n - a| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2}\). Similarly, by the convergence of \((y_n)\) to \(b\), there exists \(n_2\) such that for all \(n \geq n_2\), \(|y_n - b| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2}\). Letting \(n_0 = \max(n_1, n_2)\), it follows that for all \(n \geq n_0\), both inequalities hold simultaneously. Applying the triangle inequality, we have
\[
|x_n + y_n - (a + b)| = |(x_n - a) + (y_n - b)| \leq |x_n - a| + |y_n - b| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2} + \frac{\varepsilon}{2} = \varepsilon.
\]
Therefore, for all \(n \geq n_0\), the terms \(x_n + y_n\) lie within \(\varepsilon\) of \(a + b\), which shows that the sequence \((x_n + y_n)\) converges to \(a + b\). This completes the proof.
