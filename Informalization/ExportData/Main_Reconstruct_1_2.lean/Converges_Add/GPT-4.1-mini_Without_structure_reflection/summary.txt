We will show that if two sequences \( (x_n) \) and \( (y_n) \) converge to limits \( a \) and \( b \), respectively, then the sequence formed by adding their terms, \( (x_n + y_n) \), converges to the sum \( a + b \). Assume \( \varepsilon > 0 \) is given. Since \( x_n \to a \), there exists \( n_1 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_1 \), \( |x_n - a| < \varepsilon/2 \). Similarly, since \( y_n \to b \), there exists \( n_2 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_2 \), \( |y_n - b| < \varepsilon/2 \), noting that \( \varepsilon/2 > 0 \) as it is half of a positive number. Let \( n_0 = \max(n_1, n_2) \); this ensures that for all \( n \geq n_0 \), both inequalities hold simultaneously. For such \( n \), we have  
\[
|x_n + y_n - (a + b)| = |(x_n - a) + (y_n - b)| \leq |x_n - a| + |y_n - b| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2} + \frac{\varepsilon}{2} = \varepsilon,
\]  
where the inequality follows from the triangle inequality. Thus, by definition of the limit, the sequence \( (x_n + y_n) \) converges to \( a + b \). This completes the proof.
