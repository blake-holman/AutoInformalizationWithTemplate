We will show that if two sequences \((x_n)\) and \((y_n)\) converge to limits \(a\) and \(b\) respectively, then the sequence formed by their termwise sum \((x_n + y_n)\) converges to \(a + b\). Assume that \((x_n)\) converges to \(a\) and \((y_n)\) converges to \(b\). Given an arbitrary \(\varepsilon > 0\), by the definition of convergence, there exist natural numbers \(n_1\) and \(n_2\) such that for all \(n \geq n_1\), \(|x_n - a| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2}\), and for all \(n \geq n_2\), \(|y_n - b| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2}\). Letting \(n_0 = \max(n_1, n_2)\), it follows that for all \(n \geq n_0\), both inequalities hold simultaneously. Applying the triangle inequality, we have
\[
|x_n + y_n - (a + b)| = |(x_n - a) + (y_n - b)| \leq |x_n - a| + |y_n - b| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2} + \frac{\varepsilon}{2} = \varepsilon.
\]
This shows that \((x_n + y_n)\) converges to \(a + b\), which completes the proof.
