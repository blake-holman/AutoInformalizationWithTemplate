We will show that if two sequences \( (x_n) \) and \( (y_n) \) converge to limits \( a \) and \( b \) respectively, then the sequence formed by their termwise sum \( (x_n + y_n) \) converges to \( a + b \). Let \( \varepsilon > 0 \) be arbitrary. By the definition of convergence, there exists a natural number \( n_1 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_1 \), we have \( |x_n - a| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2} \), and similarly, there exists a natural number \( n_2 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_2 \), \( |y_n - b| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2} \). Defining \( n_0 = \max(n_1, n_2) \), it follows that for all \( n \geq n_0 \), both inequalities hold simultaneously. Using the triangle inequality, we then have
\[
|x_n + y_n - (a + b)| = |(x_n - a) + (y_n - b)| \leq |x_n - a| + |y_n - b| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2} + \frac{\varepsilon}{2} = \varepsilon.
\]
Therefore, the sequence \( (x_n + y_n) \) converges to \( a + b \), completing the proof.
