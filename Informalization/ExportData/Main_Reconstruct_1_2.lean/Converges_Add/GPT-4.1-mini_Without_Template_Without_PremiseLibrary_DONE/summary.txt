We will show that if the sequences \((x_n)\) and \((y_n)\) converge to limits \(a\) and \(b\) respectively, then the sequence \((x_n + y_n)\) converges to \(a + b\). Assume that \((x_n) \to a\) and \((y_n) \to b\). Let \(\varepsilon > 0\) be arbitrary. By the definition of convergence, there exists \(n_1 \in \mathbb{N}\) such that for all \(n \geq n_1\), \(|x_n - a| < \varepsilon/2\). Similarly, there exists \(n_2 \in \mathbb{N}\) such that for all \(n \geq n_2\), \(|y_n - b| < \varepsilon/2\). Setting \(n_0 = \max(n_1, n_2)\), for all \(n \geq n_0\), the triangle inequality gives
\[
|x_n + y_n - (a + b)| = |(x_n - a) + (y_n - b)| \leq |x_n - a| + |y_n - b| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2} + \frac{\varepsilon}{2} = \varepsilon.
\]
Hence, \((x_n + y_n) \to a + b\). This completes the proof that the limit of the sum of two convergent sequences equals the sum of their limits.
