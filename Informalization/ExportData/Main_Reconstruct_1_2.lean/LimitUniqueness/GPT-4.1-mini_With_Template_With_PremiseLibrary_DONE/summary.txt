We will show that if a sequence \((x_n)\) of real numbers converges to two limits \(a\) and \(b\), then these limits must be equal. Assume \(\varepsilon > 0\) is arbitrary. By the definition of convergence to \(a\), there exists a natural number \(n_0\) such that for all \(n \geq n_0\), \(|x_n - a| < \varepsilon/2\). Similarly, since \((x_n)\) also converges to \(b\), there exists \(n_2\) such that for all \(n \geq n_2\), \(|x_n - b| < \varepsilon/2\). Letting \(n = \max(n_0, n_2)\), both inequalities hold simultaneously for this \(n\). Using the triangle inequality, we obtain
\[
|a - b| = |a - x_n + x_n - b| \leq |a - x_n| + |x_n - b| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2} + \frac{\varepsilon}{2} = \varepsilon.
\]
Since \(\varepsilon > 0\) was arbitrary, it follows that \(|a - b|\) is less than every positive number, and thus must be zero. Hence, by the property that equality follows from zero difference, we conclude that \(a = b\), completing the proof.
