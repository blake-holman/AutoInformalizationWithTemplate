Let \(\varepsilon > 0\) be an arbitrary positive number. Since the sequence \((x_n)\) converges to \(a\), by the definition of convergence there exists a natural number \(n_1\) such that for all \(n \geq n_1\), the distance between \(x_n\) and \(a\) is less than \(\varepsilon/2\); that is, \(|x_n - a| < \varepsilon/2\). Similarly, because \((x_n)\) also converges to \(b\), there exists a natural number \(n_2\) such that for all \(n \geq n_2\), we have \(|x_n - b| < \varepsilon/2\). Letting \(n = \max(n_1, n_2)\), it follows from the properties of the maximum that \(n \geq n_1\) and \(n \geq n_2\), so both inequalities hold for \(x_n\). Applying the triangle inequality, we write
\[
|a - b| = |a - x_n + x_n - b| \leq |a - x_n| + |x_n - b| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2} + \frac{\varepsilon}{2} = \varepsilon.
\]
Since \(\varepsilon > 0\) was arbitrary, this shows that the distance between \(a\) and \(b\) is less than any positive number \(\varepsilon\), and hence \(a = b\).
