We will show that if a sequence \((x_n)\) converges to both \(a\) and \(b\), then \(a = b\). Let \(\varepsilon > 0\) be arbitrary. Since \((x_n)\) converges to \(a\), there exists \(n_1 \in \mathbb{N}\) such that for all \(n \geq n_1\), \(|x_n - a| < \varepsilon/2\). Similarly, because \((x_n)\) converges to \(b\), there exists \(n_2 \in \mathbb{N}\) such that for all \(n \geq n_2\), \(|x_n - b| < \varepsilon/2\). Taking \(n = \max(n_1, n_2)\), both inequalities hold for \(x_n\). By the triangle inequality, we have
\[
|a - b| = |a - x_n + x_n - b| \leq |a - x_n| + |x_n - b| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2} + \frac{\varepsilon}{2} = \varepsilon.
\]
Because \(\varepsilon > 0\) was arbitrary, this implies \(|a - b| = 0\) and hence \(a = b\), completing the proof.
