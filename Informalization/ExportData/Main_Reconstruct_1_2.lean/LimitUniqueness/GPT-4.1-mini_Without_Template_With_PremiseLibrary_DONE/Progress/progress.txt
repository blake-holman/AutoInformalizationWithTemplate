We will show that if a sequence converges to both \(a\) and \(b\), then the distance between \(a\) and \(b\) must be zero. Let \(\varepsilon > 0\) be arbitrary. Since the sequence \( (x_n) \) converges to \(a\), by the definition of convergence there exists a natural number \(n_1\) such that for all \( n \geq n_1 \), the distance between \(x_n\) and \(a\) is less than \(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\). \]
Since \(\varepsilon\) was arbitrary, it follows that the distance between \(a\) and \(b\) is less than every positive \(\varepsilon\), and thus \(a = b\).
