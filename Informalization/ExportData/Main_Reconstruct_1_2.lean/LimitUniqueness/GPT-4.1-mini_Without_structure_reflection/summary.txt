We will show that if a sequence \( (x_n) \) converges to both \( a \) and \( b \), then \( a = b \). To this end, let \( \varepsilon > 0 \) be arbitrary. Since \( (x_n) \) converges to \( a \), there exists a natural number \( n_1 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_1 \), the distance \( |x_n - a| \) is less than \( \frac{\varepsilon}{2} \). Similarly, since \( (x_n) \) converges to \( b \), there exists \( n_2 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_2 \), \( |x_n - b| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2} \). Let \( n = \max(n_1, n_2) \), so that for all \( n \geq n \), both inequalities hold. Then by the triangle inequality, we have
\[
|a - b| = |(a - x_n) + (x_n - b)| \leq |a - x_n| + |x_n - b| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2} + \frac{\varepsilon}{2} = \varepsilon.
\]
Since this holds for every positive \( \varepsilon \), it follows that \( |a - b| = 0 \), hence \( a = b \), completing the proof.
