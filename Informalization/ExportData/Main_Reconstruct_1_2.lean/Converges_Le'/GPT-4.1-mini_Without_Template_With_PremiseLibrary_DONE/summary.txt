We will show that if for every natural number \( n \), the inequality \( x_n \leq y_n \) holds, then the limits satisfy \( a \leq b \), where \( a = \lim_{n \to \infty} x_n \) and \( b = \lim_{n \to \infty} y_n \). To prove this, let \(\varepsilon > 0\) be arbitrary. Since \((x_n)\) converges to \(a\), there exists \( n_1 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_1 \), \( |x_n - a| < \varepsilon/2 \). Similarly, since \((y_n)\) converges to \(b\), there exists \( n_2 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_2 \), \( |y_n - b| < \varepsilon/2 \). Define \( n_0 = \max\{n_1, n_2\} \). Then, for all \( n \geq n_0 \), the inequalities
\[
a - \frac{\varepsilon}{2} < x_n \leq y_n < b + \frac{\varepsilon}{2}
\]
hold. We establish this by first noting from the convergence of \((x_n)\) that \( x_n > a - \frac{\varepsilon}{2} \), and from the given assumption, \( x_n \leq y_n \). Moreover, the convergence of \((y_n)\) ensures \( y_n < b + \frac{\varepsilon}{2} \). Applying this at \( n = n_0 \), we have
\[
a - \frac{\varepsilon}{2} < x_{n_0} \leq y_{n_0} < b + \frac{\varepsilon}{2}.
\]
By transitivity of inequalities, it follows that
\[
a - \frac{\varepsilon}{2} < b + \frac{\varepsilon}{2}.
\]
Adding \(\varepsilon/2\) to both sides yields
\[
a < b + \varepsilon.
\]
Since \(\varepsilon > 0\) was arbitrary, by the standard order properties of real numbers this implies \( a \leq b \), completing the proof.
