We will show that if for every natural number \( n \), the inequality \( x_n \leq y_n \) holds, then the limit \( a \) of the sequence \( (x_n) \) is less than or equal to the limit \( b \) of the sequence \( (y_n) \). To do this, fix an arbitrary positive number \(\varepsilon > 0\). By the definition of limits, there exist indices \( n_1 \) and \( n_2 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_1 \), \( |x_n - a| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2} \) and for all \( n \geq n_2 \), \( |y_n - b| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2} \). Define \( n_0 = \max(n_1, n_2) \). Then for any \( n \geq n_0 \), the inequalities \( a - \frac{\varepsilon}{2} < x_n < a + \frac{\varepsilon}{2} \) and \( b - \frac{\varepsilon}{2} < y_n < b + \frac{\varepsilon}{2} \) hold. Using the assumption \( x_n \leq y_n \), it follows that
\[
a - \frac{\varepsilon}{2} < x_n \leq y_n < b + \frac{\varepsilon}{2}.
\]
By focusing on the particular index \( n_0 \), these inequalities hold simultaneously at \( n_0 \), and applying the transitivity of inequalities gives
\[
a - \frac{\varepsilon}{2} < b + \frac{\varepsilon}{2}.
\]
Rearranging this inequality by adding \(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\) to both sides and using the associativity and commutativity of addition, we derive that
\[
a < b + \varepsilon.
\]
Since this holds for every \(\varepsilon > 0\), it follows by the definition of order that \( a \leq b \), completing the proof.
