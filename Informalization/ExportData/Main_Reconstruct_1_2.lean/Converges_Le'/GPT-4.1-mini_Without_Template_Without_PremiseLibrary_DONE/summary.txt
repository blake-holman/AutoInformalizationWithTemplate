We will show that if for every natural number \( n \), the sequence terms satisfy \( x_n \leq y_n \), then the limits \( a = \lim x_n \) and \( b = \lim y_n \) satisfy \( a \leq b \). To this end, let \(\varepsilon > 0\) be arbitrary. Since \((x_n)\) converges to \(a\), there exists \( n_1 \in \mathbb{N} \) such that for all \( n \geq n_1 \), \( |x_n - a| < \varepsilon/2 \). Similarly, since \((y_n)\) converges to \(b\), there exists \( n_2 \in \mathbb{N} \) such that for all \( n \geq n_2 \), \( |y_n - b| < \varepsilon/2 \). Setting \( n_0 = \max\{ n_1, n_2 \} \), we ensure these inequalities hold simultaneously for all \( n \geq n_0 \).

For such \( n \), the inequality \( |x_n - a| < \varepsilon/2 \) implies \( a - \varepsilon/2 < x_n < a + \varepsilon/2 \), and similarly \( |y_n - b| < \varepsilon/2 \) implies \( b - \varepsilon/2 < y_n < b + \varepsilon/2 \). By the given assumption, \( x_n \leq y_n \) for all natural numbers \( n \), so in particular \( x_{n_0} \leq y_{n_0} \). Combining these inequalities, we obtain
\[
a - \frac{\varepsilon}{2} < x_{n_0} \leq y_{n_0} < b + \frac{\varepsilon}{2}.
\]
Applying the transitivity of inequalities yields
\[
a - \frac{\varepsilon}{2} < b + \frac{\varepsilon}{2},
\]
which can be rearranged as
\[
a < b + \varepsilon.
\]

Since this holds for every positive \(\varepsilon\), it follows by the definition of the order on real numbers that \( a \leq b \). Thus, the limit of the sequence \( (x_n) \) is less than or equal to the limit of the sequence \( (y_n) \), as desired.
