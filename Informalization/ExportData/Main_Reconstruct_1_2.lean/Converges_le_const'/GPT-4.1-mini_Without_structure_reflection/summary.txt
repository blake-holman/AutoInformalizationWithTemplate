We will show that if a sequence \( x \) converges to \( a \) and for every natural number \( n \), the term \( x(n) \) is less than or equal to a constant \( b \), then \( a \leq b \). To do this, define a constant sequence \( y \) by \( y(n) = b \) for all natural numbers \( n \). By construction, for every \( n \), we have \( x(n) \leq y(n) \). Since \( y \) is constant, it obviously converges to \( b \). Now, applying the order limit theorem—which states that if one sequence is bounded above by another and both converge, then the limit of the first is less than or equal to the limit of the second—we conclude that \( a \leq b \). This completes the proof.
