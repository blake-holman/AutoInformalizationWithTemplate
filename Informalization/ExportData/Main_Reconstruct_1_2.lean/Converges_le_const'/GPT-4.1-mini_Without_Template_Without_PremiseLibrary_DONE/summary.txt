We will show that if for every natural number \( n \), the value \( x(n) \) is less than or equal to a constant \( b \), then the limit \( a \) of the sequence \( x \) also satisfies \( a \leq b \). To this end, define a constant sequence \( y \) by setting \( y(n) = b \) for all natural numbers \( n \). By the given assumption, for each natural number \( n \), we have \( x(n) \leq b \), which implies \( x(n) \leq y(n) \) since \( y \) is constant with value \( b \). Furthermore, since every term of \( y \) equals \( b \), the sequence \( y \) converges to \( b \) by the definition of convergence for constant sequences. Finally, as \( x \) converges to \( a \), and \( y \) converges to \( b \), with \( x(n) \leq y(n) \) for all \( n \), it follows from the order-preserving property of limits that \( a \leq b \). This completes the proof.
