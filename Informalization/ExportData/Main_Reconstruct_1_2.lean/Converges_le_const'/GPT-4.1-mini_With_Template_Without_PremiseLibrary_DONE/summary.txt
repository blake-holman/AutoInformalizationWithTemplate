We will show that if a sequence \( x \) converges to a limit \( a \) and is bounded above by a constant \( b \) for every natural number \( n \), then \( a \leq b \). To this end, define the constant sequence \( y \) by setting \( y(n) = b \) for all \( n \). By the assumption that \( x(n) \leq b \) for every \( n \), it follows that \( x(n) \leq y(n) \) holds termwise. Moreover, since \( y \) is constant, it trivially converges to \( b \). Finally, applying the comparison theorem for limits—which states that if \( x \to a \), \( y \to b \), and \( x(n) \leq y(n) \) for all \( n \), then \( a \leq b \)—we conclude that the limit \( a \) of the sequence \( x \) satisfies \( a \leq b \), as claimed.
