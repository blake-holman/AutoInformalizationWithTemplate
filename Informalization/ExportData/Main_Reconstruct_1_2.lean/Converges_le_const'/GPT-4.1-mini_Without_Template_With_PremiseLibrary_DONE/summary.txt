We will show that if a sequence \( x \) satisfies \( x(n) \leq b \) for every natural number \( n \), then its limit \( a \) is also less than or equal to \( b \). To this end, define the constant sequence \( y \) by setting \( y(n) = b \) for all natural numbers \( n \). Then, for an arbitrary natural number \( n \), we have \( x(n) \leq b = y(n) \), and since \( n \) was arbitrary, it follows that \( x(n) \leq y(n) \) for all \( n \). Next, observe that the sequence \( y \) converges to \( b \) because a constant sequence trivially converges to its constant value. Since the sequence \( x \) converges to \( a \) and satisfies \( x(n) \leq y(n) \) for every \( n \), by the comparison theorem for limits of sequences, we conclude that the limit \( a \) of \( x \) is less than or equal to the limit \( b \) of \( y \). This completes the proof.
