We will show that if a convergent sequence of real numbers \( (x_n) \) is bounded above by a constant \( b \), then its limit \( a \) satisfies \( a \leq b \). To this end, define the constant sequence \( (y_n) \) by \( y_n = b \) for all natural numbers \( n \). By the assumption that \( x_n \leq b \) for every \( n \), it follows immediately that \( x_n \leq y_n \) for all \( n \). Since \( y \) is a constant sequence, it converges to \( b \). Given that \( x \) converges to \( a \) and \( y \) converges to \( b \), and that \( x_n \leq y_n \) for all \( n \), we apply the standard comparison theorem for limits of sequences, which states that the limit of \( x \) is less than or equal to the limit of \( y \). Therefore, we conclude that \( a \leq b \), completing the proof.
