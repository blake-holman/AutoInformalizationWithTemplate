We will show that if a sequence \( x \) satisfies \( x(n) \leq a \) for every natural number \( n \), then the limit of \( x \) is less than or equal to \( a \). To this end, define the constant sequence \( y \) by \( y(n) = a \) for all \( n \). Clearly, for each \( n \), we have \( x(n) \leq y(n) \) by the initial assumption. Since \( y \) is constant, it converges to \( a \). Given that \( x \) is convergent and each term satisfies \( x(n) \leq y(n) \), it follows from the comparison of limits that \(\lim_{n \to \infty} x(n) \leq \lim_{n \to \infty} y(n) = a\). Thus, we conclude that the limit of \( x \) is less than or equal to \( a \), completing the proof.
