We will show that if for every natural number \( n \), the sequence \( x(n) \) satisfies \( x(n) \leq a \), then the limit of the sequence \( x \) is also less than or equal to \( a \). To this end, define the constant sequence \( y \) by setting \( y(n) = a \) for all \( n \in \mathbb{N} \). By assumption, for each natural number \( n \), we have \( x(n) \leq a \), which implies \( x(n) \leq y(n) \) since \( y(n) = a \). Hence, the inequality \( x(n) \leq y(n) \) holds for all \( n \). Next, observe that the constant sequence \( y \) trivially converges to \( a \), as its terms do not vary and remain equal to \( a \). Therefore, \( y \) is a convergent sequence with limit \( a \). Since \( x(n) \leq y(n) \) for all \( n \), and both sequences \( x \) and \( y \) converge, it follows by the order preservation property of limits that the limit of \( x \) is less than or equal to the limit of \( y \), that is, \(\lim x(n) \leq \lim y(n) = a\). Finally, interpreting \( a \) as the limit superior of the constant sequence \( y \) allows us to conclude that the limit superior of \( x \) is less than or equal to \( a \). This completes the proof.
