We will show that if a sequence \( x \) satisfies \( x(n) \leq a \) for every natural number \( n \), then the limit of \( x \) is less than or equal to \( a \). To this end, define the constant sequence \( y \) by \( y(n) = a \) for all \( n \). By construction, for every \( n \), we have \( x(n) \leq y(n) \). Since \( y \) is a constant sequence, it converges trivially to \( a \). Because each term of \( x \) is bounded above by the corresponding term of \( y \), it follows that the limit superior of \( x \) is less than or equal to the limit superior of \( y \). Given that \( y \) converges to \( a \), its limit superior equals \( a \). Therefore, the limit of \( x \), if it exists, must be less than or equal to \( a \). This completes the proof.
