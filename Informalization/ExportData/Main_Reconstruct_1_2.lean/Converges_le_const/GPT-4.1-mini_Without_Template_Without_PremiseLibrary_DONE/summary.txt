We will show that if a sequence \( x = (x_n) \) satisfies \( x_n \leq a \) for every natural number \( n \), then the limit of \( x \) is also less than or equal to \( a \). To this end, define the constant sequence \( y = (y_n) \) by setting \( y_n = a \) for all \( n \). By construction, for every \( n \), we have \( x_n \leq y_n \). Since \( y \) is constant, it trivially converges to \( a \). Therefore, \( y \) is a convergent sequence with limit \( a \). Given that \( x_n \leq y_n \) for all \( n \), it follows that the limit superior of \( x \) is less than or equal to the limit superior of \( y \), which equals \( a \). Using the fact that the limit of a convergent sequence coincides with its limit superior, we conclude that the limit of \( x \) is less than or equal to \( a \), completing the proof.
