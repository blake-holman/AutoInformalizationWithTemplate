We will show that if a convergent sequence \( x = (x(n)) \) of real numbers satisfies \( x(n) \leq a \) for every natural number \( n \), then the limit of \( x \) is less than or equal to \( a \). To this end, consider the constant sequence \( y = (y(n)) \) defined by \( y(n) = a \) for all \( n \). By the assumption, for every natural number \( n \), we have \( x(n) \leq y(n) \). Since \( y \) is constant, it trivially converges to \( a \). Moreover, \( x \) is convergent by hypothesis. Because each term of \( x \) is bounded above by the corresponding term of \( y \), a standard result on limits of sequences implies that the limit of \( x \) is less than or equal to the limit of \( y \). Hence, the limit of \( x \) is less than or equal to \( a \), completing the proof.
