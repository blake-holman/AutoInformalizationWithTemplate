We will show that if a sequence \( (x_n) \) converges to a limit \( a \), then the sequence obtained by multiplying each term of \( (x_n) \) by a scalar \( \alpha \) converges to \( \alpha a \). Assuming \( \varepsilon > 0 \), we aim to find a natural number \( n_0 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_0 \), the inequality \( |\alpha x_n - \alpha a| < \varepsilon \) holds. To do so, choose \( n_0 \) so that \( |x_n - a| < \frac{\varepsilon}{|\alpha| + 1} \) for all \( n \geq n_0 \); such an \( n_0 \) exists because \( (x_n) \) converges to \( a \). Then, for any \( n \geq n_0 \), we have 
\[
|\alpha x_n - \alpha a| = |\alpha| \cdot |x_n - a| \leq |\alpha| \cdot \frac{\varepsilon}{|\alpha| + 1} < \varepsilon,
\]
where we used the multiplicative properties of absolute values and inequalities preserved under multiplication by positive numbers. 

To justify this inequality, we consider two cases: if \( |\alpha| > 0 \), then dividing both sides of the inequality by \( |\alpha| \) (a positive number) preserves the inequality, allowing us to conclude \( |x_n - a| < \frac{\varepsilon}{|\alpha| + 1} \) from our choice of \( n_0 \). If \( |\alpha| = 0 \), then \( \alpha = 0 \), so \( |\alpha x_n - \alpha a| = 0 \) for all \( n \), trivially satisfying the inequality.

Finally, by the uniqueness of limits, since the scaled sequence converges and the limit must be unique, this sequence converges to \( \alpha a \). Thus, the sequence defined by \( \alpha x_n \) converges to \( \alpha a \), completing the proof.
