We will show that if a sequence \((x_n)\) of real numbers converges to a limit \(a\), then the sequence obtained by multiplying each term of \((x_n)\) by a scalar \(\alpha\) also converges, and its limit is \(\alpha a\). Let \(\varepsilon > 0\) be given. Since \((x_n)\) converges to \(a\), there exists a natural number \(n_0\) such that for all \(n \geq n_0\), the inequality 
\[
|x_n - a| < \frac{\varepsilon}{|\alpha| + 1}
\]
holds, where the denominator \( |\alpha| + 1 \) is strictly positive. For any \( n \geq n_0 \), we have
\[
|\alpha x_n - \alpha a| = |\alpha| \cdot |x_n - a| < |\alpha| \cdot \frac{\varepsilon}{|\alpha| + 1}.
\]
If \(|\alpha| > 0\), multiplying by \( |\alpha| \) preserves the inequality; if \(\alpha = 0\), the left-hand side equals zero and the inequality trivially holds. Furthermore, since \(|\alpha| < |\alpha| + 1\), we conclude
\[
|\alpha| \cdot \frac{\varepsilon}{|\alpha| + 1} < \varepsilon.
\]
Therefore, for all \(n \geq n_0\),
\[
|\alpha x_n - \alpha a| < \varepsilon,
\]
which shows that the sequence \((\alpha x_n)\) converges to \(\alpha a\). This completes the proof.
