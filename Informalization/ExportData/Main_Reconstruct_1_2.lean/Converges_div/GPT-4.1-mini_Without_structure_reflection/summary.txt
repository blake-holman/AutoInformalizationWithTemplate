We will show that if sequences \( x \) and \( y \) converge, \( y \) is never zero, and the limit of \( y \) is nonzero, then the sequence formed by dividing \( x \) by \( y \) converges to the quotient of their limits.

First, let \( b \) denote the limit of the sequence \( y \). Since \( y \) converges and never takes the value zero, and its limit \( b \) is not zero, it follows by the uniqueness of limits and the properties of convergent sequences that \( b \neq 0 \). From this, we establish that the absolute value of \( b \) is positive.

By applying the definition of convergence to \( y \) and \( b \), there exists a natural number \( n_1 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_1 \), the distance between \( y_n \) and \( b \) is less than half the absolute value of \( b \). It follows that for all such \( n \), the absolute value of \( y_n \) exceeds half the absolute value of \( b \).

Next, we demonstrate that the sequence formed by taking the reciprocal of each term in \( y \) converges to the reciprocal of \( b \). Given an arbitrary positive number \( \varepsilon \), there is a natural number \( n_2 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_2 \), the distance between \( y_n \) and \( b \) is less than \( |b|^2 \cdot \frac{\varepsilon}{2} \). Defining \( n_0 = \max(n_1, n_2) \), for each \( n \geq n_0 \), we can bound the absolute difference between the reciprocals \( \frac{1}{y_n} \) and \( \frac{1}{b} \) by \( \varepsilon \). This uses the inequality
\[
\left| \frac{1}{y_n} - \frac{1}{b} \right| = \frac{|y_n - b|}{|y_n b|} < \frac{2}{|b|^2} \cdot |y_n - b| < \varepsilon,
\]
noting that the denominator is controlled by the positivity of \( |y_n| \) and \( |b| \).

Thus, the reciprocal sequence converges to \( \frac{1}{b} \).

Finally, since the sequence of reciprocals of \( y \) converges to the reciprocal of the limit of \( y \), and \( x \) converges to its limit, it follows from the limit laws for products of sequences that the sequence \( \frac{x_n}{y_n} = x_n \cdot \frac{1}{y_n} \) converges to the product of their limits, namely \( \frac{\lim x_n}{\lim y_n} \).

This completes the proof.
