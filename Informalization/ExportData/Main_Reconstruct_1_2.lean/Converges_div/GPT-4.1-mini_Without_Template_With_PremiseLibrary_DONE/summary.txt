We will show that if a sequence \( y \) converges to a nonzero limit \( b \), then the sequence formed by dividing a convergent sequence \( x \) by \( y \) converges to the quotient of their limits. First, since \( y \to b \) and \( b \neq 0 \), the terms of \( y \) are eventually bounded away from zero. This ensures that the sequence of reciprocals \( (1/y_n) \) is well-defined and converges to \( 1/b \). To establish this, we use the definition of convergence and the triangle inequality to show that for any \(\varepsilon > 0\), there exists an index \( n_0 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_0 \), the difference \( |1/y_n - 1/b| \) is less than \(\varepsilon\). This continuity of the reciprocal function at the nonzero limit \( b \) confirms that \( 1/y_n \to 1/b \).

Next, noting that \( x \) converges to some limit \( a \), the sequence formed by term-wise multiplication \( x_n \cdot (1/y_n) \) converges to \( a \cdot (1/b) \) by the limit laws for products of convergent sequences. Since \( x/y \) can be expressed as \( x \cdot (1/y) \), it follows that \( x_n / y_n \to a / b \). Therefore, the limit of the quotient sequence equals the quotient of the limits, completing the proof.
