We will show that if \( y \) is a sequence converging to a nonzero limit \( b \), then the sequence formed by dividing \( x \) by \( y \) converges to the quotient of their respective limits. First, since \( y \to b \neq 0 \), we prove that the sequence of reciprocals \( \frac{1}{y_n} \) converges to \( \frac{1}{b} \). By the definition of convergence, for any \( \varepsilon > 0 \), there exists \( n_0 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_0 \), the terms \( y_n \) remain close to \( b \), and in particular, are bounded away from zero. This ensures that the reciprocal sequence terms \( \frac{1}{y_n} \) are well-defined and satisfy

\[
\left| \frac{1}{y_n} - \frac{1}{b} \right| = \frac{|y_n - b|}{|y_n||b|} < \varepsilon,
\]

establishing \( \frac{1}{y_n} \to \frac{1}{b} \). Since \( x \) is convergent, and now \( \frac{1}{y} \) is also convergent, their product sequence \( x \cdot \frac{1}{y} \) converges to the product of their limits. Rewriting, this implies

\[
\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{x_n}{y_n} = \frac{\lim_{n \to \infty} x_n}{\lim_{n \to \infty} y_n} = \frac{a}{b},
\]

where \( a = \lim x_n \). This completes the proof that the limit of the quotient sequence is the quotient of the limits.
