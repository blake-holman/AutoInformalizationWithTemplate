We will show that if the sequence \( y \) converges to a nonzero limit \( b \), then the sequence formed by the reciprocals \( \frac{1}{y_n} \) converges to \( \frac{1}{b} \), and consequently, the limit of the quotient sequence \( \frac{x_n}{y_n} \) equals the quotient of the limits of \( x \) and \( y \).

First, let \( b \) denote the limit of the sequence \( y \), and assume \( b \neq 0 \). Since \( y_n \to b \) and none of the terms \( y_n \) are zero, by the definition of convergence and the uniqueness of limits, it follows that \( y_n \) converges to the nonzero limit \( b \).

Next, because \( b \neq 0 \), we have \( |b| > 0 \). By the convergence of \( y_n \) to \( b \), there exists a natural number \( n_1 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_1 \), the difference \( |y_n - b| \) is less than \( \frac{|b|}{2} \). This ensures that \( |y_n| > \frac{|b|}{2} \) for all \( n \geq n_1 \), keeping the terms of the sequence away from zero.

Given any \( \varepsilon > 0 \), by the convergence of \( y_n \) to \( b \), there exists a natural number \( n_2 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_2 \), \( |y_n - b| < \frac{|b|^2 \varepsilon}{2} \). Setting \( n_0 = \max(n_1, n_2) \), for all \( n \geq n_0 \) we have

\[
\left| \frac{1}{y_n} - \frac{1}{b} \right| = \frac{|y_n - b|}{|y_n||b|} < \frac{\frac{|b|^2 \varepsilon}{2}}{\frac{|b|}{2} \cdot |b|} = \varepsilon.
\]

This inequality verifies that the sequence \( \left(\frac{1}{y_n}\right) \) converges to \( \frac{1}{b} \).

Finally, since \( x_n \) converges and \( \left(\frac{1}{y_n}\right) \) also converges to \( \frac{1}{b} \), the product rule for limits implies that the sequence \( \frac{x_n}{y_n} = x_n \cdot \frac{1}{y_n} \) converges to \( \lim x_n \cdot \lim \frac{1}{y_n} = \frac{\lim x_n}{\lim y_n} \).

This establishes that the limit of the quotient \( \frac{x_n}{y_n} \) is the quotient of the limits of \( x_n \) and \( y_n \), completing the proof under the assumption that \( y \) converges to a nonzero limit.
