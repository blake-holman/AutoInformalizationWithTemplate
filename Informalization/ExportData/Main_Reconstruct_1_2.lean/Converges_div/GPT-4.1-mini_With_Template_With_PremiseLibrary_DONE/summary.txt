We will show that if two real-valued sequences \( x = (x_n) \) and \( y = (y_n) \) converge, with the limit of \( y \) nonzero, then the sequence formed by their termwise quotient \( \frac{x_n}{y_n} \) converges to the quotient of their limits. Let \( b \) denote the limit of \( y \), which by assumption satisfies \( b \neq 0 \). Since \( y_n \to b \), it follows from the uniqueness of limits that the limit assigned to \( y \) equals \( b \), and thus \( b \) is nonzero.

To establish the convergence of the reciprocal sequence \( \left(\frac{1}{y_n}\right) \) and identify its limit, observe that because \( b \neq 0 \), there exists an index \( n_1 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_1 \), \( |y_n| > \frac{|b|}{2} \), ensuring the terms \( y_n \) stay away from zero. Furthermore, for an arbitrary \( \varepsilon > 0 \), convergence of \( y_n \to b \) guarantees a natural number \( n_2 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_2 \), \( |y_n - b| < \frac{|b|^2 \varepsilon}{2} \). Setting \( n_0 = \max(n_1, n_2) \), for all \( n \geq n_0 \) we have both these bounds simultaneously. Then, for these \( n \), the difference between the reciprocals satisfies
\[
\left| \frac{1}{y_n} - \frac{1}{b} \right| = \frac{|y_n - b|}{|y_n b|} < \frac{2}{|b|^2} \cdot \frac{|b|^2 \varepsilon}{2} = \varepsilon,
\]
showing that \(\frac{1}{y_n} \to \frac{1}{b}\).

Since \( x_n \to \lim x \) and \( \frac{1}{y_n} \to \frac{1}{b} \), the standard theorem on the product of convergent sequences implies \( x_n \cdot \frac{1}{y_n} \to \lim x \cdot \frac{1}{b} \). Recognizing that multiplication by the reciprocal corresponds exactly to division, we conclude that
\[
\frac{x_n}{y_n} = x_n \cdot \frac{1}{y_n} \to \frac{\lim x}{\lim y} = \frac{\lim x}{b}.
\]
Thus, under the assumptions that \( x \) and \( y \) converge, \( y_n \neq 0 \) for all \( n \), and \( \lim y = b \neq 0 \), the quotient sequence \( \frac{x_n}{y_n} \) converges to the quotient of the limits, as desired. This completes the proof.
