We will show that if the sequences \( x = (x_n) \) and \( y = (y_n) \) both converge to the same limit \( a \), and if for every natural number \( n \), the value \( z_n \) lies between \( x_n \) and \( y_n \), then the sequence \( z = (z_n) \) also converges to \( a \).

To this end, let \(\varepsilon > 0\) be arbitrary. Since \( x_n \to a \), there exists a natural number \( n_1 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_1 \), we have \( |x_n - a| < \varepsilon \). Similarly, since \( y_n \to a \), there exists \( n_2 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_2 \), \( |y_n - a| < \varepsilon \). Define \( n_0 = \max(n_1, n_2) \). Then for all \( n \geq n_0 \), both inequalities hold simultaneously.

By the definition of absolute value inequalities, the condition \( |x_n - a| < \varepsilon \) implies \( a - \varepsilon < x_n < a + \varepsilon \), and likewise \( |y_n - a| < \varepsilon \) implies \( a - \varepsilon < y_n < a + \varepsilon \). Since \( z_n \) lies between \( x_n \) and \( y_n \) for each \( n \), we have the chain of inequalities:
\[
a - \varepsilon < x_n \leq z_n \leq y_n < a + \varepsilon
\]
for all \( n \geq n_0 \).

This chain implies that \( z_n \) is sandwiched within the open interval \( (a - \varepsilon, a + \varepsilon) \), and therefore \( |z_n - a| < \varepsilon \) holds for all \( n \geq n_0 \).

Since \(\varepsilon > 0\) was arbitrary, it follows by the definition of convergence that \( z_n \to a \) as \( n \to \infty \), completing the proof.
