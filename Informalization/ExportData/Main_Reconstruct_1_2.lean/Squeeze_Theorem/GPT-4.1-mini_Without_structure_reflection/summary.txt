We will show that if for every natural number \( n \), the value \( z(n) \) lies between \( x(n) \) and \( y(n) \), and both sequences \( x \) and \( y \) converge to the limit \( a \), then the sequence \( z \) also converges to \( a \). Let \( \varepsilon > 0 \) be given. Since \( x \) converges to \( a \), there exists a natural number \( n_1 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_1 \), the distance \( |x_n - a| < \varepsilon \). Similarly, since \( y \) converges to \( a \), there exists \( n_2 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_2 \), \( |y_n - a| < \varepsilon \). Define \( n_0 = \max(n_1, n_2) \), so that for every \( n \geq n_0 \), both \( x_n \) and \( y_n \) lie within \( \varepsilon \) of \( a \).

Now, for any \( n \geq n_0 \), we have 
\[
a - \varepsilon < x_n \leq z_n \leq y_n < a + \varepsilon,
\]
where the inequalities \( x_n \leq z_n \leq y_n \) hold by assumption. From \( |x_n - a| < \varepsilon \), it follows that \( a - \varepsilon < x_n \), and from \( |y_n - a| < \varepsilon \), we deduce \( y_n < a + \varepsilon \). Thus, \( z_n \) lies strictly between \( a - \varepsilon \) and \( a + \varepsilon \), implying that 
\[
|z_n - a| < \varepsilon.
\]
Hence, for all \( n \geq n_0 \), the terms \( z_n \) are within \( \varepsilon \) of \( a \), which establishes the convergence of the sequence \( z \) to \( a \).
