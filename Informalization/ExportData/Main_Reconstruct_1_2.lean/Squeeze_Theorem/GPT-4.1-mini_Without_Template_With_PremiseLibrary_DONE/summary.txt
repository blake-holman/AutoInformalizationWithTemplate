We will show that if a sequence \( (z_n) \) lies between two sequences \( (x_n) \) and \( (y_n) \) that both converge to the same limit \( a \), then \( (z_n) \) also converges to \( a \). To this end, let \( \varepsilon > 0 \) be given. Since \( (x_n) \) converges to \( a \), there exists a natural number \( n_1 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_1 \), the distance between \( x_n \) and \( a \) is less than \( \varepsilon \), that is, \( |x_n - a| < \varepsilon \). Similarly, the convergence of \( (y_n) \) to \( a \) guarantees the existence of a natural number \( n_2 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_2 \), \( |y_n - a| < \varepsilon \). Define \( n_0 = \max\{ n_1, n_2 \} \); then for all \( n \geq n_0 \), both inequalities hold simultaneously.

Fix an arbitrary \( n \geq n_0 \). From the inequalities \( |x_n - a| < \varepsilon \) and \( |y_n - a| < \varepsilon \), we deduce the double inequalities \( a - \varepsilon < x_n < a + \varepsilon \) and \( a - \varepsilon < y_n < a + \varepsilon \). Given the assumption that \( z_n \) lies between \( x_n \) and \( y_n \), i.e., \( x_n \leq z_n \leq y_n \), it follows that \( z_n \) is also bounded by \( a - \varepsilon \) and \( a + \varepsilon \).

More precisely, combining these inequalities, we have 
\[
a - \varepsilon < x_n \leq z_n \leq y_n < a + \varepsilon,
\]
which implies the double inequality
\[
-\varepsilon < a - z_n < \varepsilon.
\]
Taking the absolute value, this shows that \( |z_n - a| < \varepsilon \) for all \( n \geq n_0 \).

Since \( \varepsilon > 0 \) was arbitrary, this establishes that the sequence \( (z_n) \) converges to \( a \).
