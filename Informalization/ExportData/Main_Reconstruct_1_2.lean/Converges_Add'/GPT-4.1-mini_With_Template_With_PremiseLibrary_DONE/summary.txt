We will show that the sum of two sequences \( (x_n) \) and \( (y_n) \), converging respectively to real numbers \( a \) and \( b \), converges to the sum \( a + b \). Let \( \varepsilon > 0 \). Since \( (x_n) \to a \), there exists an index \( n_1 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_1 \), \( |x_n - a| < \varepsilon/2 \). Similarly, since \( (y_n) \to b \), there exists an index \( n_2 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_2 \), \( |y_n - b| < \varepsilon/2 \). Note that \( \varepsilon/2 > 0 \) because \( \varepsilon \) is positive. Define \( n_0 = \max(n_1, n_2) \); then for all \( n \geq n_0 \), both inequalities hold simultaneously. Using the triangle inequality, we have

\[
|x_n + y_n - (a + b)| = |(x_n - a) + (y_n - b)| \leq |x_n - a| + |y_n - b| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2} + \frac{\varepsilon}{2} = \varepsilon.
\]

Thus, for every \( \varepsilon > 0 \), there exists \( n_0 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_0 \), \( |x_n + y_n - (a + b)| < \varepsilon \), proving that \( (x_n + y_n) \) converges to \( a + b \).
