We will show that the sequence \( (x_n + y_n) \) converges to \( a + b \), that is, for every \(\varepsilon > 0\), there exists an index \( n_0 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_0 \), the inequality \( |x_n + y_n - (a + b)| < \varepsilon \) holds. First, observe that by rewriting the difference of sums, we have

\[
|x_n + y_n - (a + b)| = |(x_n - a) + (y_n - b)|.
\]

Applying the triangle inequality, this is bounded above by

\[
|x_n - a| + |y_n - b|.
\]

To ensure this sum is less than \(\varepsilon\), it suffices to find \( n_0 \geq \max(n_1, n_2) \) such that for all \( n \geq n_0 \), both

\[
|x_n - a| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2} \quad \text{and} \quad |y_n - b| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2}
\]

hold. Since \( n_0 \) is defined as the maximum of \( n_1 \) and \( n_2 \), it follows that \( n_0 \geq n_1 \) and \( n_0 \geq n_2 \), ensuring both inequalities hold simultaneously for all \( n \geq n_0 \). Consequently,

\[
|x_n + y_n - (a + b)| \leq |x_n - a| + |y_n - b| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2} + \frac{\varepsilon}{2} = \varepsilon,
\]

which completes the proof.
