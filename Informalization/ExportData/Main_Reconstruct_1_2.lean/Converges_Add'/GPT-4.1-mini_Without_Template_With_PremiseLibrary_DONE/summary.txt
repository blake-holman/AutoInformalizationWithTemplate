We will show that for any given positive real number \(\varepsilon\), there exists a natural number \(n_0\) such that for all \(n \geq n_0\), the absolute difference between the sum \(x_n + y_n\) of two sequences and the sum \(a + b\) of their respective limits is less than \(\varepsilon\). Since \(x_n\) converges to \(a\), by the definition of convergence, there is a natural number \(n_1\) such that for all \(n \geq n_1\), we have \(|x_n - a| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2}\). Similarly, as \(y_n\) converges to \(b\), there exists a natural number \(n_2\) so that for all \(n \geq n_2\), \(|y_n - b| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2}\). Define \(n_0 = \max\{n_1, n_2\}\); then for every \(n \geq n_0\), both inequalities hold simultaneously. Considering the difference \(|(x_n + y_n) - (a + b)|\), we rewrite this as \(|(x_n - a) + (y_n - b)|\), and by the triangle inequality, this is bounded above by \(|x_n - a| + |y_n - b|\). Using the established bounds, this sum is less than \(\frac{\varepsilon}{2} + \frac{\varepsilon}{2} = \varepsilon\). Hence, the sum sequence converges to the sum of the limits, completing the proof.
