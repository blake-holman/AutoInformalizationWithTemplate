We will show that if the sequences \((x_n)\) and \((y_n)\) converge to limits \(a\) and \(b\) respectively, then the sequence \((x_n + y_n)\) converges to \(a + b\). Let \(\varepsilon > 0\) be given. By the definition of convergence, there exists a natural number \(n_1\) such that for all \(n \geq n_1\), \(|x_n - a| < \varepsilon/2\). Similarly, since \((y_n)\) converges to \(b\), there exists a natural number \(n_2\) such that for all \(n \geq n_2\), \(|y_n - b| < \varepsilon/2\). Define \(n_0 = \max(n_1, n_2)\). Then for all \(n \geq n_0\), both inequalities hold simultaneously, which implies
\[
|(x_n + y_n) - (a + b)| = |(x_n - a) + (y_n - b)| \leq |x_n - a| + |y_n - b| < \varepsilon/2 + \varepsilon/2 = \varepsilon.
\]
This shows that the sequence \((x_n + y_n)\) converges to \(a + b\), completing the proof.
