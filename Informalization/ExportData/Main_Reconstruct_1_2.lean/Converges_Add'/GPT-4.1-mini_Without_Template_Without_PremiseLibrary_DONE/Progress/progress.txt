We will show that for sequences \( (x_n) \) and \( (y_n) \) converging to limits \( a \) and \( b \) respectively, the sequence \( (x_n + y_n) \) converges to \( a + b \). To this end, consider the absolute difference \(|(x_n + y_n) - (a + b)|\). By rearranging, this expression can be written as \(|(x_n - a) + (y_n - b)|\). Applying the triangle inequality, we have that this is at most \(|x_n - a| + |y_n - b|\). Next, define \( n_0 = \max(n_1, n_2) \), where \( n_1 \) and \( n_2 \) are indices beyond which \( |x_n - a| < \varepsilon/2 \) and \( |y_n - b| < \varepsilon/2 \) hold, respectively. Since \( n \geq n_0 \), both inequalities hold simultaneously, and thus their sum is less than \( \varepsilon/2 + \varepsilon/2 = \varepsilon \). This establishes the desired bound on \(|(x_n + y_n) - (a + b)|\), completing the proof that \( x_n + y_n \to a + b \).
