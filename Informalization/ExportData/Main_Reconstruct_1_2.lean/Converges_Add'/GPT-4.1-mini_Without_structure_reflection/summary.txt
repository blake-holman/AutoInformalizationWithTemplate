We will show that if the sequences \( (x_n) \) and \( (y_n) \) converge to \( a \) and \( b \), respectively, then their sum \( (x_n + y_n) \) converges to \( a + b \). Let \( \varepsilon > 0 \) be given. Since \( (x_n) \) converges to \( a \), there exists \( n_1 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_1 \), the inequality \( |x_n - a| < \varepsilon/2 \) holds. Similarly, since \( (y_n) \) converges to \( b \), there exists \( n_2 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_2 \), we have \( |y_n - b| < \varepsilon/2 \). Define \( n_0 = \max(n_1, n_2) \), so that for all \( n \geq n_0 \), both inequalities hold simultaneously. For such \( n \), by the triangle inequality, we have
\[
|x_n + y_n - (a + b)| = |(x_n - a) + (y_n - b)| \leq |x_n - a| + |y_n - b| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2} + \frac{\varepsilon}{2} = \varepsilon.
\]
Therefore, for all \( n \geq n_0 \), \( |x_n + y_n - (a + b)| < \varepsilon \), which proves that the sequence \( (x_n + y_n) \) converges to \( a + b \).
