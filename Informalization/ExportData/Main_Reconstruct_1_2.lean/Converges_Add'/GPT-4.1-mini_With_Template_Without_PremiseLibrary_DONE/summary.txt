We will show that the sum of the sequences \( x_n \) and \( y_n \) converges to \( a + b \). Assume \( \varepsilon > 0 \) is given. Since \( x_n \to a \), there exists \( n_1 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_1 \), we have \( |x_n - a| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2} \). Similarly, since \( y_n \to b \), there exists \( n_2 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_2 \), \( |y_n - b| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2} \). Define \( n_0 = \max(n_1, n_2) \). Then, for all \( n \geq n_0 \), both inequalities above hold simultaneously. Using the triangle inequality, we get
\[
|x_n + y_n - (a + b)| = |(x_n - a) + (y_n - b)| \leq |x_n - a| + |y_n - b| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2} + \frac{\varepsilon}{2} = \varepsilon.
\]
Therefore, for every \( \varepsilon > 0 \), there exists \( n_0 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_0 \), \( |x_n + y_n - (a + b)| < \varepsilon \), proving that \( x_n + y_n \to a + b \).
