We will show that if a sequence \( (x_n) \) converges to a limit \( a \) and \(\alpha\) is any real number, then the sequence \( (\alpha x_n) \) converges to \(\alpha a\). Starting from the assumption that \( (x_n) \to a \), we fix an arbitrary \(\varepsilon > 0\). By the definition of convergence, there exists \( n_0 \in \mathbb{N} \) such that for all \( n \geq n_0 \), \(|x_n - a| < \frac{\varepsilon}{|\alpha| + 1}\), noting that \(|\alpha| + 1 > 0\). Considering the difference \(|\alpha x_n - \alpha a| = |\alpha||x_n - a|\), we use the bound on \(|x_n - a|\) to get \(|\alpha x_n - \alpha a| < |\alpha| \cdot \frac{\varepsilon}{|\alpha| + 1}\). If \(|\alpha| > 0\), it follows that this quantity is less than \(\varepsilon\), since \(\frac{|\alpha|}{|\alpha| + 1} < 1\). If \(|\alpha| = 0\), then \(\alpha = 0\) and the inequality trivially holds. Therefore, for all \( n \geq n_0 \), \(|\alpha x_n - \alpha a| < \varepsilon\), which confirms that the sequence \( (\alpha x_n) \) converges to \(\alpha a\). This completes the proof.
