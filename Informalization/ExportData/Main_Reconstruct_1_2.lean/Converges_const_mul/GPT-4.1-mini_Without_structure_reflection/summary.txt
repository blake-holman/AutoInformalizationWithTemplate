We will show that if a sequence \((x_n)\) converges to a limit \(a\), then the sequence obtained by multiplying each term by a constant \(\alpha\) converges to \(\alpha a\). Assume that \(\varepsilon > 0\) is given. Since \((x_n)\) converges to \(a\), there exists a natural number \(n_0\) such that for all \(n \geq n_0\), \(|x_n - a| < \frac{\varepsilon}{|\alpha| + 1}\). To prove the desired convergence, it suffices to show that for all such \(n\), \(|\alpha x_n - \alpha a| < \varepsilon\). By properties of absolute values and multiplication, we have \(|\alpha x_n - \alpha a| = |\alpha| \cdot |x_n - a|\). Using the choice of \(n_0\), it follows that \(|\alpha x_n - \alpha a| \leq |\alpha| \cdot \frac{\varepsilon}{|\alpha| + 1} < \varepsilon\), where the inequality holds because \(0 < 1\) implies \(|\alpha| < |\alpha| + 1\). In the case \(|\alpha| = 0\), i.e., \(\alpha=0\), the inequality holds trivially since both sides equal zero. Thus, by the definition of convergence and the fact that multiplication by a constant preserves limits, we conclude that the sequence \((\alpha x_n)\) converges to \(\alpha a\).
