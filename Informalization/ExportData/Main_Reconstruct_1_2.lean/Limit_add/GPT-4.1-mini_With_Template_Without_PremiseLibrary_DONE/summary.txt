We will show that the sum of two convergent sequences converges to the sum of their limits. Let \((x_n)\) and \((y_n)\) be sequences converging to limits \(a\) and \(b\), respectively. Given an arbitrary \(\varepsilon > 0\), by the definition of convergence, there exist natural numbers \(n_1\) and \(n_2\) such that for all \(n \geq n_1\), \(|x_n - a| < \varepsilon/2\), and for all \(n \geq n_2\), \(|y_n - b| < \varepsilon/2\). Setting \(n_0 = \max\{n_1, n_2\}\), it follows that for all \(n \geq n_0\), both inequalities hold simultaneously. Applying the triangle inequality, we have
\[
|(x_n + y_n) - (a + b)| = |(x_n - a) + (y_n - b)| \leq |x_n - a| + |y_n - b| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2} + \frac{\varepsilon}{2} = \varepsilon.
\]
Hence, for all \(n \geq n_0\), the terms of the sequence \(x_n + y_n\) lie within \(\varepsilon\) of \(a + b\), showing that \((x_n + y_n)\) converges to \(a + b\). This completes the proof.
