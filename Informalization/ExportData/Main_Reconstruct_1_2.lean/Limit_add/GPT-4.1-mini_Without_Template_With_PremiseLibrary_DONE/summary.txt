We will show that the sequence formed by adding two convergent sequences \( (x_n) \) and \( (y_n) \), which converge to limits \( a \) and \( b \) respectively, also converges to the sum \( a + b \). To prove this, let \(\varepsilon > 0\) be arbitrary. Since \( (x_n) \) converges to \( a \), there exists \( n_1 \in \mathbb{N} \) such that for all \( n \geq n_1 \), \(|x_n - a| < \varepsilon/2\). Similarly, because \( (y_n) \) converges to \( b \), there exists \( n_2 \in \mathbb{N} \) such that for all \( n \geq n_2 \), \(|y_n - b| < \varepsilon/2\). Defining \( n_0 = \max(n_1, n_2) \), it follows that for all \( n \geq n_0 \), both inequalities hold simultaneously. Then, by the triangle inequality, we have
\[
| (x_n + y_n) - (a + b) | = | (x_n - a) + (y_n - b) | \leq |x_n - a| + |y_n - b| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2} + \frac{\varepsilon}{2} = \varepsilon.
\]
Thus, the sequence \( (x_n + y_n) \) converges to \( a + b \). Since the limit of a convergent sequence is unique, this establishes that the limit of the sum of the sequences equals the sum of their limits, as required.
