We will show that the sequence formed by adding the corresponding terms of two convergent sequences \( x \) and \( y \) converges to the sum of their respective limits. Let \( a \) be the limit of the sequence \( x \) and \( b \) be the limit of the sequence \( y \). By the definition of convergence, to prove that the sequence \( x + y \) converges, it suffices to show that it converges to \( a + b \).

Assume an arbitrary \( \varepsilon > 0 \). Since \( x \) converges to \( a \), there exists a natural number \( n_1 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_1 \), the distance \( |x_n - a| \) is less than \( \varepsilon/2 \). Similarly, since \( y \) converges to \( b \), there exists \( n_2 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_2 \), \( |y_n - b| < \varepsilon/2 \). Define \( n_0 \) as the maximum of \( n_1 \) and \( n_2 \); thus, for all \( n \geq n_0 \), both inequalities hold.

For such \( n \), consider the \( n \)-th term of the sum sequence:
\[
|(x + y)_n - (a + b)| = |(x_n - a) + (y_n - b)|.
\]
By the triangle inequality, this is bounded above by:
\[
|x_n - a| + |y_n - b| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2} + \frac{\varepsilon}{2} = \varepsilon.
\]
Therefore, for all \( n \geq n_0 \), the distance between \( (x + y)_n \) and \( a + b \) is less than \( \varepsilon \), showing that \( x + y \) converges to \( a + b \).

Since \( a \) and \( b \) are limits of \( x \) and \( y \), respectively, and the sum sequence converges to \( a + b \), we conclude that the sum of two convergent sequences is convergent and its limit is the sum of their limits.
