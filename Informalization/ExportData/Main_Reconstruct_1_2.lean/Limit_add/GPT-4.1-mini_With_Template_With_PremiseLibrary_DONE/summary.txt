We will show that the sum of two convergent real-valued sequences converges to the sum of their individual limits. Let \((x_n)\) and \((y_n)\) be sequences converging to limits \(a\) and \(b\) respectively. To prove that the sequence \((x_n + y_n)\) converges to \(a + b\), we start by fixing an arbitrary \(\varepsilon > 0\). By the definition of convergence, there exist indices \(n_1\) and \(n_2\) such that for all \(n \geq n_1\), \(|x_n - a| < \varepsilon/2\), and for all \(n \geq n_2\), \(|y_n - b| < \varepsilon/2\). Setting \(n_0 = \max(n_1, n_2)\), it follows that for all \(n \geq n_0\), both inequalities hold simultaneously. Then, by the triangle inequality, we have
\[
| (x_n + y_n) - (a + b) | = |(x_n - a) + (y_n - b)| \leq |x_n - a| + |y_n - b| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2} + \frac{\varepsilon}{2} = \varepsilon.
\]
This shows that the sequence \((x_n + y_n)\) converges to \(a + b\). Hence, the limit of the sum sequence equals the sum of the limits of the individual sequences, completing the proof.
