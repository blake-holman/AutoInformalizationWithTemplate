We will show that if the sequences \( x = (x_n) \) and \( y = (y_n) \) converge to limits \( a \) and \( b \) respectively, then the sequence \( x + y = (x_n + y_n) \) converges to \( a + b \). Let \(\varepsilon > 0\) be arbitrary. Since \( x \) converges to \( a \), there exists \( n_1 \in \mathbb{N} \) such that for all \( n \geq n_1 \), \( |x_n - a| < \varepsilon/2 \). Similarly, because \( y \) converges to \( b \), there exists \( n_2 \in \mathbb{N} \) such that for all \( n \geq n_2 \), \( |y_n - b| < \varepsilon/2 \). Define \( n_0 = \max(n_1, n_2) \), so that for all \( n \geq n_0 \), both inequalities hold. Then, by the triangle inequality,
\[
|(x_n + y_n) - (a + b)| = |(x_n - a) + (y_n - b)| \leq |x_n - a| + |y_n - b| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2} + \frac{\varepsilon}{2} = \varepsilon.
\]
This shows that \( x + y \) converges to \( a + b \) by definition of convergence, completing the proof.
