We will show that if two sequences \((x_n)\) and \((y_n)\) of real numbers converge to limits \(a\) and \(b\), respectively, then their term-by-term product \((x_n y_n)\) converges to the product \(a b\).

First, since \((x_n)\) converges to \(a\), it is bounded; thus, there exists a positive real number \(m_1\) such that \(|x_n| \leq m_1\) for all \(n\). Similarly, because \((y_n)\) converges to \(b\), it is also bounded, so there exists a positive real number \(m_2\) such that \(|y_n| \leq m_2\) for all \(n\). Define \(M\) as the maximum of \(m_1\), \(m_2\), and \(|b|\); clearly, \(M > 0\).

Next, let \(\varepsilon > 0\) be given. Since \(M > 0\), the quantity \(\frac{\varepsilon}{2M}\) is positive. By the convergence of \((x_n)\) to \(a\), there exists an index \(n_1\) such that for all \(n \geq n_1\), \(|x_n - a| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2M}\). Similarly, by the convergence of \((y_n)\) to \(b\), there exists an index \(n_2\) such that for all \(n \geq n_2\), \(|y_n - b| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2M}\). Let \(n_0 = \max(n_1, n_2)\).

For all \(n \geq n_0\), we estimate the difference between the product terms and the product of limits using the triangle inequality:
\[
|x_n y_n - a b| = |x_n y_n - x_n b + x_n b - a b| \leq |x_n||y_n - b| + |x_n - a||b|.
\]
Using the bounds, we have \(|x_n| \leq M\) and \(|b| \leq M\), so
\[
|x_n y_n - a b| \leq M |y_n - b| + M |x_n - a| = M(|y_n - b| + |x_n - a|).
\]
Since for \(n \geq n_0\), both \(|x_n - a|\) and \(|y_n - b|\) are less than \(\frac{\varepsilon}{2M}\), it follows that
\[
|x_n y_n - a b| < M \cdot \frac{\varepsilon}{2M} + M \cdot \frac{\varepsilon}{2M} = \frac{\varepsilon}{2} + \frac{\varepsilon}{2} = \varepsilon.
\]

Thus, for every \(\varepsilon > 0\), there is an index \(n_0\) such that for all \(n \geq n_0\), the inequality \(|x_n y_n - a b| < \varepsilon\) holds, completing the proof that \((x_n y_n)\) converges to \(a b\).
