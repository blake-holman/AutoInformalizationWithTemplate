We will show that if two sequences \( (x_n) \) and \( (y_n) \) converge to limits \( a \) and \( b \) respectively, then the sequence formed by the term-wise product \( (x_n y_n) \) converges to the product \( a \cdot b \).

First, since \( (x_n) \) converges, it is bounded; thus, there exists a positive number \( m_1 \) such that for every natural number \( n \), \( |x_n| \leq m_1 \). Similarly, because \( (y_n) \) converges, it is also bounded; so there exists a positive number \( m_2 \) with \( |y_n| \leq m_2 \) for all \( n \). To unify these bounds and also include the absolute value of \( b \), we define 
\[
M = \max(m_1, m_2, |b|),
\]
which is strictly positive since it is the maximum of positive quantities.

Given an arbitrary \( \varepsilon > 0 \), we consider the positive number \( \frac{\varepsilon}{2M} \). Using the convergence of \( (x_n) \) to \( a \), there exists a natural number \( n_1 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_1 \), 
\[
|x_n - a| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2M}.
\]
Similarly, by the convergence of \( (y_n) \) to \( b \), there exists \( n_2 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_2 \), 
\[
|y_n - b| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2M}.
\]
Define \( n_0 = \max(n_1, n_2) \). Then for every \( n \geq n_0 \), both inequalities hold simultaneously.

We now examine the difference
\[
|x_n y_n - a b| = |x_n y_n - a y_n + a y_n - a b|.
\] 
Applying the triangle inequality yields
\[
|x_n y_n - a b| \leq |y_n||x_n - a| + |a||y_n - b|.
\]
Since \( |y_n| \leq M \) and \( |a| \leq M \) (because \( M \geq |b| \) and \( a \) is finite), it follows that
\[
|x_n y_n - a b| \leq M|x_n - a| + M|y_n - b| = M\big(|x_n - a| + |y_n - b|\big).
\]
Using the bounds established above, for all \( n \geq n_0 \),
\[
|x_n y_n - a b| < M \left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2M} + \frac{\varepsilon}{2M}\right) = \varepsilon.
\]

Hence, for every \( \varepsilon > 0 \), there exists \( n_0 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_0 \), the inequality \( |x_n y_n - a b| < \varepsilon \) holds, proving that the product sequence \( (x_n y_n) \) converges to \( a b \). This completes the proof.
