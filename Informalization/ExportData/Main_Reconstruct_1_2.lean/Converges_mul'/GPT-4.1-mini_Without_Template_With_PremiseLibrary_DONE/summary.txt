We will show that if the sequences \( (x_n) \) and \( (y_n) \) converge to limits \( a \) and \( b \) respectively, then the sequence defined by their pointwise product \( (x_n y_n) \) converges to the product \( a b \). First, since \( (x_n) \) converges to \( a \), it is bounded; thus, there exists a positive real number \( m_1 \) such that for all \( n \), \( |x_n| \leq m_1 \). Similarly, \( (y_n) \) converges to \( b \) and is bounded by some positive real \( m_2 \) so that \( |y_n| \leq m_2 \). Define \( M \) as the maximum of \( m_1, m_2, \) and \( |b| \), which is positive. Given any \( \varepsilon > 0 \), the positivity of \( M \) ensures that \(\frac{\varepsilon}{2M} > 0\). By the convergence of \( (x_n) \) and \( (y_n) \), there exist indices \( n_1 \) and \( n_2 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_1 \), \( |x_n - a| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2M} \), and for all \( n \geq n_2 \), \( |y_n - b| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2M} \). Let \( n_0 = \max(n_1, n_2) \). For any \( n \geq n_0 \), we analyze the difference \( |x_n y_n - a b| \), which can be rewritten as \( |x_n (y_n - b) + (x_n - a) b| \). By the triangle inequality, this is at most \( |x_n||y_n - b| + |x_n - a||b| \). Using the bounds, it follows that
\[
|x_n y_n - a b| \leq M \cdot |y_n - b| + M \cdot |x_n - a| < M \frac{\varepsilon}{2M} + M \frac{\varepsilon}{2M} = \varepsilon.
\]
Hence, \( (x_n y_n) \) converges to \( a b \), completing the proof.
