We will show that if the sequences \((x_n)\) and \((y_n)\) converge to \(a\) and \(b\) respectively, then the sequence defined by the product \(x_n y_n\) converges to the product \(a \cdot b\). First, since \((x_n)\) converges, there exists a real number \(m_1\) such that \(|x_n| \leq m_1\) for all \(n\), and similarly, since \((y_n)\) converges, there is \(m_2\) such that \(|y_n| \leq m_2\) for all \(n\). Defining \(M = \max(m_1, m_2, |b|)\), we have a positive constant \(M\) that bounds \(|x_n|\), \(|y_n|\), and \(|b|\) uniformly.

Next, for an arbitrary \(\varepsilon > 0\), consider \(\frac{\varepsilon}{2M} > 0\). By the convergence of \((x_n)\) and \((y_n)\), there exist indices \(n_1\) and \(n_2\) such that for all \(n \geq n_1\), \(|x_n - a| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2M}\) and for all \(n \geq n_2\), \(|y_n - b| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2M}\). Letting \(n_0 = \max(n_1, n_2)\), it follows that for all \(n \geq n_0\), both inequalities hold simultaneously.

Now, for \(n \geq n_0\), we analyze the difference:
\[
|x_n y_n - ab| = |x_n y_n - x_n b + x_n b - ab| \leq |x_n||y_n - b| + |x_n - a||b|.
\]
Since \(|x_n| \leq M\) and \(|b| \leq M\), this is bounded by
\[
M |y_n - b| + M |x_n - a| < M \cdot \frac{\varepsilon}{2M} + M \cdot \frac{\varepsilon}{2M} = \frac{\varepsilon}{2} + \frac{\varepsilon}{2} = \varepsilon.
\]
Hence, for all \(n \geq n_0\), \(|x_n y_n - ab| < \varepsilon\), demonstrating that the product sequence converges to \(ab\). This completes the proof.
