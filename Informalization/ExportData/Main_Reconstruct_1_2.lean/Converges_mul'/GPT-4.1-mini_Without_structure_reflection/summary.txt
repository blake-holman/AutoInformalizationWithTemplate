We will show that if the sequences \( (x_n) \) and \( (y_n) \) converge to \( a \) and \( b \) respectively, then the sequence defined by their pointwise product \( (x_n y_n) \) converges to the product \( a \cdot b \).

First, since \( (x_n) \) converges to \( a \), the set of absolute values \( \{ |x_n| : n \in \mathbb{N} \} \) is bounded above. Let \( m_1 \) denote an upper bound for this set. To ensure positivity for later use, consider the number \( M_x = \max(m_1, 1) \). Clearly, since \( 1 > 0 \), \( M_x > 0 \), and for all \( n \), \( |x_n| \leq M_x \).

Similarly, since \( (y_n) \) converges to \( b \), the sequence \( (y_n) \) is bounded. Let \( m_2 \) be an upper bound for the set \( \{ |y_n| : n \in \mathbb{N} \} \), and set \( M_y = \max(m_2, 1) \), which is positive for the same reasoning. Then for all \( n \), \( |y_n| \leq M_y \).

Define \( M = \max(M_x, M_y, |b|) \). By the properties of the maximum function, \( M \) is positive and bounds \( |x_n|, |y_n| \), and \( |b| \) from above.

Next, let \( \varepsilon > 0 \) be given. Since \( M > 0 \), the quantity \( \frac{\varepsilon}{2M} \) is positive. By the definition of convergence, there exist natural numbers \( n_1 \) and \( n_2 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_1 \), \( |x_n - a| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2M} \), and for all \( n \geq n_2 \), \( |y_n - b| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2M} \). Set \( n_0 = \max(n_1, n_2) \).

For any \( n \geq n_0 \), consider the difference:
\[
|x_n y_n - a b| = |x_n y_n - x_n b + x_n b - a b| \leq |x_n||y_n - b| + |x_n - a||b|.
\]
Since \( |x_n| \leq M \) and \( |b| \leq M \), it follows that
\[
|x_n y_n - a b| \leq M |y_n - b| + M |x_n - a| = M \left( |y_n - b| + |x_n - a| \right).
\]

Using the bounds on \( |x_n - a| \) and \( |y_n - b| \) for \( n \geq n_0 \), we have
\[
|x_n y_n - a b| < M \left( \frac{\varepsilon}{2M} + \frac{\varepsilon}{2M} \right) = \varepsilon.
\]

This shows that \( (x_n y_n) \) converges to \( a \cdot b \), completing the proof.
