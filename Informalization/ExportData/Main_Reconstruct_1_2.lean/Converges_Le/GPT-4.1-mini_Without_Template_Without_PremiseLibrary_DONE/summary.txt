We will show that if two sequences \((x_n)\) and \((y_n)\) of real numbers satisfy \(x_n \leq y_n\) for every natural number \(n\), and both sequences converge with limits \(a\) and \(b\) respectively, then \(a \leq b\).

Assuming \(x_n \to a\) and \(y_n \to b\), we fix an arbitrary positive \(\varepsilon\). Since \((x_n)\) converges to \(a\), there exists a natural number \(n_1\) such that for all \(n \geq n_1\), \(|x_n - a| < \varepsilon/2\), implying \(x_n > a - \varepsilon/2\). Similarly, since \((y_n)\) converges to \(b\), there exists \(n_2\) such that for all \(n \geq n_2\), \(|y_n - b| < \varepsilon/2\), yielding \(y_n < b + \varepsilon/2\). Defining \(n_0 = \max\{n_1, n_2\}\), for any \(n \geq n_0\), we have both \(x_n > a - \varepsilon/2\) and \(y_n < b + \varepsilon/2\). Given the assumption \(x_n \leq y_n\), it follows that
\[
a - \frac{\varepsilon}{2} < x_n \leq y_n < b + \frac{\varepsilon}{2}.
\]

Evaluating these inequalities at \(n = n_0\), we apply the transitive property of inequalities to conclude
\[
a - \frac{\varepsilon}{2} < b + \frac{\varepsilon}{2}.
\]

Rearranging the terms, this is equivalent to
\[
a < b + \varepsilon.
\]

Since \(\varepsilon > 0\) was arbitrary, we deduce that \(a \leq b\).

Finally, by identifying \(a\) and \(b\) as the limits of the sequences \((x_n)\) and \((y_n)\), respectively, we conclude that the limit of \(x_n\) is less than or equal to the limit of \(y_n\), as claimed.
