We will show that if two sequences \( x \) and \( y \) of real numbers satisfy \( x_n \leq y_n \) for every natural number \( n \), and if both sequences converge to limits \( a \) and \( b \) respectively, then \( a \leq b \). By the definition of convergence, for any positive \( \varepsilon \), there exist natural numbers \( n_1 \) and \( n_2 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_1 \), \( |x_n - a| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2} \), and for all \( n \geq n_2 \), \( |y_n - b| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2} \). Letting \( n_0 = \max(n_1, n_2) \), it follows that for all \( n \geq n_0 \),  
\[
a - \frac{\varepsilon}{2} < x_n < a + \frac{\varepsilon}{2} \quad \text{and} \quad b - \frac{\varepsilon}{2} < y_n < b + \frac{\varepsilon}{2}.
\]  
Since \( x_n \leq y_n \) by assumption, combining these inequalities yields  
\[
a - \frac{\varepsilon}{2} < x_n \leq y_n < b + \frac{\varepsilon}{2}.
\]  
Focusing on each part, we observe that \( x_n > a - \frac{\varepsilon}{2} \) because of the convergence of \( x \), \( x_n \leq y_n \) by assumption, and \( y_n < b + \frac{\varepsilon}{2} \) due to the convergence of \( y \). Evaluating these inequalities at \( n = n_0 \) and using transitivity, we conclude that  
\[
a - \frac{\varepsilon}{2} < b + \frac{\varepsilon}{2},
\]  
which implies  
\[
a < b + \varepsilon.
\]  
Since \( \varepsilon > 0 \) was arbitrary, this shows that \( a \leq b \). Hence, the limit of the sequence \( x \) is less than or equal to the limit of the sequence \( y \), completing the proof.
