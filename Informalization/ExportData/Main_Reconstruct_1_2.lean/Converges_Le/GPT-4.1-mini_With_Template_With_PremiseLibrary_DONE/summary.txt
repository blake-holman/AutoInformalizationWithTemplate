We will show that if one convergent real sequence \((x_n)\) is termwise less than or equal to another convergent sequence \((y_n)\), then the limit of the first, \(a = \lim x_n\), is less than or equal to the limit of the second, \(b = \lim y_n\). Assume that for every natural number \(n\), \(x_n \leq y_n\). Given \(\varepsilon > 0\), by the definition of convergence, there exist indices \(n_1\) and \(n_2\) such that for all \(n \geq n_1\), \(|x_n - a| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2}\), and for all \(n \geq n_2\), \(|y_n - b| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2}\). Letting \(n_0 = \max\{n_1, n_2\}\), it follows that for all \(n \geq n_0\), the inequalities 
\[
a - \frac{\varepsilon}{2} < x_n \leq y_n < b + \frac{\varepsilon}{2}
\]
hold. Each part of this conjunction is verified independently: the inequality \(a - \frac{\varepsilon}{2} < x_n\) follows from the definition of the absolute value, as \(|x_n - a| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2}\) implies \(x_n > a - \frac{\varepsilon}{2}\); similarly, the inequality \(y_n < b + \frac{\varepsilon}{2}\) is inferred from \(|y_n - b| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2}\). Since \(x_n \leq y_n\) holds by assumption, this establishes the combined inequalities. Applying the transitivity of inequalities at the index \(n_0\), we deduce 
\[
a - \frac{\varepsilon}{2} < x_{n_0} \leq y_{n_0} < b + \frac{\varepsilon}{2} \implies a - \frac{\varepsilon}{2} < b + \frac{\varepsilon}{2}.
\]
By rearranging terms via the commutativity and associativity of addition, this inequality translates to \(a < b + \varepsilon\). Since \(\varepsilon > 0\) was arbitrary, it follows that \(a \leq b\), completing the proof that the limit of \(x\) is less than or equal to the limit of \(y\).
