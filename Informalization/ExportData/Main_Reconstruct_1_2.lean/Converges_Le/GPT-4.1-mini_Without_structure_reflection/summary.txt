We will show that if for every natural number \( n \), the sequence values satisfy \( x(n) \leq y(n) \), then the limit of the sequence \( x \) is less than or equal to the limit of the sequence \( y \). Let \( a \) be the limit of \( x \) and \( b \) be the limit of \( y \). To establish that \( a \leq b \), it suffices to show that for every positive number \( \varepsilon \), \( a < b + \varepsilon \).

Assume \( \varepsilon > 0 \). Since \( x \to a \) and \( y \to b \), there exist indices \( n_1 \) and \( n_2 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_1 \), \( |x_n - a| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2} \), and for all \( n \geq n_2 \), \( |y_n - b| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2} \). Let \( n_0 = \max(n_1, n_2) \). For every \( n \geq n_0 \), it follows that \( a - \frac{\varepsilon}{2} < x_n \) and \( y_n < b + \frac{\varepsilon}{2} \), and by assumption, \( x_n \leq y_n \).

To see this, note that since \( n \geq n_1 \), \( |x_n - a| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2} \) implies \( a - \frac{\varepsilon}{2} < x_n \). Similarly, since \( n \geq n_2 \), \( |y_n - b| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2} \) implies \( y_n < b + \frac{\varepsilon}{2} \). The given assumption that \( x_n \leq y_n \) holds for all \( n \), so in particular for \( n \geq n_0 \).

Applying the transitivity of inequalities for \( n = n_0 \), we have
\[
a - \frac{\varepsilon}{2} < x_{n_0} \leq y_{n_0} < b + \frac{\varepsilon}{2}.
\]
This implies \( a - \frac{\varepsilon}{2} < b + \frac{\varepsilon}{2} \), and by adding \( \frac{\varepsilon}{2} \) to both sides, one obtains
\[
a < b + \varepsilon.
\]

Since this holds for every positive \( \varepsilon \), we conclude that \( a \leq b \). Finally, by the definitions of limits of sequences \( x \) and \( y \), this establishes that the limit of \( x \) is less than or equal to the limit of \( y \).
