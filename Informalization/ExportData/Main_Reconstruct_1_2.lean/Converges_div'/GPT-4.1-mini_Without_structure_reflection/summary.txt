We will show that if the sequence \( (x_n) \) converges to \( a \) and the sequence \( (y_n) \) converges to \( b \neq 0 \), then the sequence defined by term-wise division \( \left(\frac{x_n}{y_n}\right) \) converges to the quotient \( \frac{a}{b} \).

First, we establish that the absolute value of \( b \) is positive since \( b \neq 0 \). By the positivity of absolute value and convergence of \( (y_n) \) to \( b \), there exists a natural number \( n_1 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_1 \), the distance \( |y_n - b| \) is less than half of \( |b| \). From this, it follows using the reverse triangle inequality that for all \( n \geq n_1 \), \( |y_n| \) is greater than \( \frac{|b|}{2} \), ensuring that none of these \( y_n \) are zero and are bounded away from zero.

Next, for any \( \varepsilon > 0 \), since \( (y_n) \to b \), there exists \( n_2 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_2 \), \( |y_n - b| < \frac{|b|^2 \varepsilon}{2} \). Defining \( n_0 = \max(n_1, n_2) \), we consider any \( n \geq n_0 \).

To show that the sequence of reciprocals \( \left(\frac{1}{y_n}\right) \) converges to \( \frac{1}{b} \), note that

\[
\left| \frac{1}{y_n} - \frac{1}{b} \right| = \frac{|y_n - b|}{|y_n| \cdot |b|}.
\]

Since for \( n \geq n_1 \), we have \( |y_n| > \frac{|b|}{2} \), it follows that

\[
\frac{1}{|y_n| \cdot |b|} < \frac{2}{|b|^2}.
\]

Combining these inequalities yields

\[
\left| \frac{1}{y_n} - \frac{1}{b} \right| < \frac{2}{|b|^2} \cdot \frac{|b|^2 \varepsilon}{2} = \varepsilon,
\]

thus proving the reciprocal sequence converges.

Since \( (x_n) \to a \) and \( \left(\frac{1}{y_n}\right) \to \frac{1}{b} \), by the product limit theorem, the sequence \( \left(x_n \cdot \frac{1}{y_n}\right) = \left(\frac{x_n}{y_n}\right) \) converges to \( a \cdot \frac{1}{b} = \frac{a}{b} \).

This completes the proof that the term-wise division of convergent sequences converges to the quotient of their limits under the assumption that the limit of the denominator sequence is nonzero.
