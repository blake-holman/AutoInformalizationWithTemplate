Assume that the sequences \( (x_n) \) and \( (y_n) \) converge to real numbers \( a \) and \( b \), respectively, with \( b \neq 0 \). We aim to prove that the sequence \( \left( \frac{x_n}{y_n} \right) \) converges to \( \frac{a}{b} \). To do this, it suffices to show that the sequence of reciprocals \( \left( \frac{1}{y_n} \right) \) converges to \( \frac{1}{b} \), since then the product sequence \( x_n \cdot \frac{1}{y_n} \) converges to \( a \cdot \frac{1}{b} = \frac{a}{b} \) by the limit laws.

First, since \( b \neq 0 \), it follows that \( |b| > 0 \). Because \( y_n \to b \), for any positive radius less than \( \frac{|b|}{2} \), there exists a natural number \( n_1 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_1 \), the terms \( y_n \) lie within \( \frac{|b|}{2} \) of \( b \). From this, it follows that for all \( n \geq n_1 \), the absolute value \( |y_n| \) exceeds \( \frac{|b|}{2} \), ensuring that none of these terms are zero or arbitrarily close to zero.

Next, let \( \varepsilon > 0 \) be arbitrary, and set the positive number \( \delta = \frac{|b|^2 \varepsilon}{2} \). By the convergence of \( y_n \to b \), there exists \( n_2 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_2 \), the inequality \( |y_n - b| < \delta \) holds. Define \( n_0 = \max(n_1, n_2) \); thus, for all \( n \geq n_0 \), both the bounds on the closeness of \( y_n \) to \( b \) and the lower bound on \( |y_n| \) hold simultaneously.

For \( n \geq n_0 \), consider the absolute difference of reciprocals:
\[
\left| \frac{1}{y_n} - \frac{1}{b} \right| = \frac{|y_n - b|}{|y_n||b|} .
\]
Since \( |y_n| > \frac{|b|}{2} \), we have
\[
\left| \frac{1}{y_n} - \frac{1}{b} \right| < \frac{|y_n - b|}{\frac{|b|}{2} \cdot |b|} = \frac{2}{|b|^2} |y_n - b| .
\]
Using the bound \( |y_n - b| < \frac{|b|^2 \varepsilon}{2} \), this simplifies to
\[
\left| \frac{1}{y_n} - \frac{1}{b} \right| < \varepsilon .
\]

Since \( \varepsilon > 0 \) was arbitrary, it follows that \( \frac{1}{y_n} \to \frac{1}{b} \). Therefore, by the product rule for limits, the sequence \( \frac{x_n}{y_n} = x_n \cdot \frac{1}{y_n} \) converges to \( a \cdot \frac{1}{b} = \frac{a}{b} \), completing the proof.
