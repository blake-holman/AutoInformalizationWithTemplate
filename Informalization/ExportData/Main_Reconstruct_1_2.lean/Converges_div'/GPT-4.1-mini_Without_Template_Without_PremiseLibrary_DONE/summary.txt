We will show that if two sequences \( (x_n) \) and \( (y_n) \) converge to limits \( a \) and \( b \) respectively, with \( b \neq 0 \), then the sequence formed by dividing \( x_n \) by \( y_n \) converges to the quotient \( a/b \). To do this, it suffices to prove that the reciprocal sequence \( (1/y_n) \) converges to \( 1/b \), since the limit of the product \( x_n \cdot (1/y_n) \) would then be \( a \cdot (1/b) = a/b \).

First, note that the nonzero limit \( b \) implies \( |b| > 0 \), as the absolute value of a nonzero number is positive. Because \( y_n \to b \), there exists a natural number \( n_1 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_1 \), \( |y_n - b| < \frac{1}{2} |b| \). Applying the triangle inequality, it follows that for all such \( n \), the magnitude \( |y_n| \) is bounded below by \( \frac{1}{2}|b| \), ensuring that \( y_n \) stays away from zero and thus \( 1/y_n \) is well-defined.

Now, let \( \varepsilon > 0 \) be arbitrary. Since \( y_n \to b \), there exists another index \( n_2 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_2 \), \( |y_n - b| < \frac{|b|^{2}}{2} \varepsilon \). Define \( n_0 = \max(n_1, n_2) \); then for any \( n \geq n_0 \), these inequalities simultaneously hold.

For such \( n \), we analyze the difference between the reciprocals:
\[
\left| \frac{1}{y_n} - \frac{1}{b} \right| = \frac{|y_n - b|}{|y_n| \cdot |b|}.
\]
Using the lower bound on \( |y_n| \), we have
\[
|y_n| > \frac{1}{2} |b| \implies \frac{1}{|y_n| \cdot |b|} < \frac{2}{|b|^{2}}.
\]
Hence,
\[
\left| \frac{1}{y_n} - \frac{1}{b} \right| < \frac{2}{|b|^{2}} |y_n - b| < \frac{2}{|b|^{2}} \cdot \frac{|b|^{2}}{2} \varepsilon = \varepsilon.
\]
Since \( \varepsilon > 0 \) was arbitrary, this establishes that \( 1/y_n \to 1/b \).

Combining this with the convergence \( x_n \to a \), it follows from standard limit laws that
\[
\frac{x_n}{y_n} = x_n \cdot \frac{1}{y_n} \to a \cdot \frac{1}{b} = \frac{a}{b}.
\]
Therefore, the quotient sequence converges to the desired limit, completing the proof.
