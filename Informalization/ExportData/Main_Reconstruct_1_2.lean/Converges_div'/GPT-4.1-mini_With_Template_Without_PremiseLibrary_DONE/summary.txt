We will show that if the sequences \( (x_n) \) and \( (y_n) \) converge to limits \( a \) and \( b \), respectively, with \( b \neq 0 \), then the sequence defined by the term-wise division \( \left(\frac{x_n}{y_n}\right) \) converges to the quotient \( \frac{a}{b} \).

First, we establish that \(|b| > 0\) since it is given that \( b \neq 0 \), and by the properties of the absolute value, a nonzero number has positive absolute value.

Next, since \( y_n \to b \), for the positive number \(\frac{|b|}{2}\), there exists a natural number \( n_1 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_1 \), the distance \( |y_n - b| < \frac{|b|}{2} \). From this, we deduce that for all \( n \geq n_1 \), the absolute value \( |y_n| \) is greater than \( \frac{|b|}{2} \). This follows by applying the triangle inequality and manipulating inequalities to show \( |y_n| > \frac{|b|}{2} \).

To prove that the reciprocal sequence \( \left( \frac{1}{y_n} \right) \) converges to \( \frac{1}{b} \), let \(\varepsilon > 0\) be arbitrary. Since \( y_n \to b \), there exists a natural number \( n_2 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_2 \), \( |y_n - b| < \frac{|b|^2 \varepsilon}{2} \). Define \( n_0 = \max(n_1, n_2) \). Then for all \( n \geq n_0 \), the inequalities \( |y_n| > \frac{|b|}{2} \) and \( |y_n - b| < \frac{|b|^2 \varepsilon}{2} \) hold simultaneously.

Using these bounds, we analyze the difference of reciprocals:

\[
\left| \frac{1}{y_n} - \frac{1}{b} \right| = \frac{|y_n - b|}{|y_n| \cdot |b|} < \frac{|y_n - b|}{\frac{|b|}{2} \cdot |b|} = \frac{2}{|b|^2} |y_n - b|.
\]

Substituting the upper bound for \( |y_n - b| \) gives

\[
\left| \frac{1}{y_n} - \frac{1}{b} \right| < \frac{2}{|b|^2} \cdot \frac{|b|^2 \varepsilon}{2} = \varepsilon.
\]

Thus, for all \( n \geq n_0 \), the difference \( \left| \frac{1}{y_n} - \frac{1}{b} \right| \) is less than the arbitrary \(\varepsilon\), establishing the convergence of \( \left( \frac{1}{y_n} \right) \) to \( \frac{1}{b} \).

Finally, since the sequences \( (x_n) \to a \) and \( \left( \frac{1}{y_n} \right) \to \frac{1}{b} \), the product sequence \( \left( x_n \cdot \frac{1}{y_n} \right) \) converges to \( a \cdot \frac{1}{b} = \frac{a}{b} \). Recognizing that this product sequence equals the term-wise division sequence \( \left( \frac{x_n}{y_n} \right) \), we conclude that \( \frac{x_n}{y_n} \to \frac{a}{b} \), completing the proof.
