We will show that if two sequences \((x_n)\) and \((y_n)\) of real numbers converge to nonzero limits \(a\) and \(b\) respectively, then the sequence formed by their termwise quotients \(\left(\frac{x_n}{y_n}\right)\) converges to \(\frac{a}{b}\). 

Assuming \(b \neq 0\), it follows that \(|b| > 0\). By the definition of limit convergence, there exists a natural number \(n_1\) such that for all \(n \geq n_1\), the distance between \(y_n\) and \(b\) is less than \(\frac{|b|}{2}\). This implies, via the reverse triangle inequality, that \(|y_n| > \frac{|b|}{2}\) for all such \(n\), ensuring that none of the terms \(y_n\) are too close to zero and thus their reciprocals are well-defined and bounded away from zero.

Next, for an arbitrary \(\varepsilon > 0\), since \((y_n)\) converges to \(b\), there exists a natural number \(n_2\) such that for all \(n \geq n_2\), \(|y_n - b| < \frac{|b|^2 \varepsilon}{2}\). Setting \(n_0 = \max(n_1, n_2)\), we consider all \(n \geq n_0\). For these \(n\), we estimate the difference between the reciprocals:

\[
\left| \frac{1}{y_n} - \frac{1}{b} \right| = \frac{|y_n - b|}{|y_n| \cdot |b|}.
\]

Since \(|y_n| > \frac{|b|}{2}\), it follows that \(\frac{1}{|y_n|} < \frac{2}{|b|}\), and hence

\[
\left| \frac{1}{y_n} - \frac{1}{b} \right| < \frac{|y_n - b| \cdot 2}{|b|^2}.
\]

Applying the bound on \(|y_n - b|\), we get

\[
\left| \frac{1}{y_n} - \frac{1}{b} \right| < \frac{2}{|b|^2} \cdot \frac{|b|^2 \varepsilon}{2} = \varepsilon,
\]

which shows that \(\left(\frac{1}{y_n}\right)\) converges to \(\frac{1}{b}\).

Finally, since \((x_n)\) converges to \(a\) and \(\left(\frac{1}{y_n}\right)\) converges to \(\frac{1}{b}\), the sequence defined by the termwise product \(\left(x_n \cdot \frac{1}{y_n}\right) = \left(\frac{x_n}{y_n}\right)\) converges to \(a \cdot \frac{1}{b} = \frac{a}{b}\) by the standard limit product theorem. This concludes the proof.
