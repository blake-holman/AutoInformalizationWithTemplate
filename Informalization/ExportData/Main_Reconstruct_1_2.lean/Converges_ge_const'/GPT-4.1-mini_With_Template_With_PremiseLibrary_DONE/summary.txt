We will show that if a sequence \((x_n)\) of real numbers converges to a limit \(a\) and if each term \(x_n\) satisfies \(x_n \geq b\) for some fixed real number \(b\), then the limit \(a\) also satisfies \(a \geq b\). To establish this, consider the constant sequence \((y_n)\) defined by \(y_n = b\) for all natural numbers \(n\). By the assumption, for every \(n\), \(x_n \geq b = y_n\), so \(y_n \leq x_n\) holds for all \(n\). Since \((y_n)\) is constant, it converges to \(b\). Given that both \((x_n)\) and \((y_n)\) converge, and that \(y_n \leq x_n\) for all \(n\), it follows from the order preservation property of limits that the limit of \((y_n)\), which is \(b\), is less than or equal to the limit of \((x_n)\), which is \(a\). Therefore, we conclude \(a \geq b\), completing the proof.
