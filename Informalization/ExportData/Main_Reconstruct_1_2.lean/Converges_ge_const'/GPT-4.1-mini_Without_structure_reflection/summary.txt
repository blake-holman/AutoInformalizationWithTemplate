We will show that if a sequence \( x \) satisfies \( x(n) \geq b \) for every natural number \( n \) and converges to a limit \( a \), then \( a \geq b \). To this end, define the constant sequence \( y \) by \( y(n) = b \) for all \( n \). Clearly, for every \( n \), we have \( y(n) \leq x(n) \) since \( x(n) \geq b \) by assumption. Additionally, the sequence \( y \) converges to \( b \) because a constant sequence converges to its constant value. By applying the theorem that states if two sequences converge and one is always less than or equal to the other, then the limit of the first is less than or equal to the limit of the second, we conclude that \( b = \lim y(n) \leq \lim x(n) = a \). Hence, the limit \( a \) of the sequence \( x \) satisfies \( a \geq b \), completing the proof.
