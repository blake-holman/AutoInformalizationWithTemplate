We will show that if for every natural number \( n \), the sequence value \( x_n \) satisfies \( x_n \geq b \), then the limit \( a \) of the sequence \( x \) also satisfies \( a \geq b \). To this end, define a constant sequence \( y \) by setting \( y_n = b \) for all natural numbers \( n \). Clearly, for every \( n \), we have \( y_n = b \leq x_n \) by the given assumption. Moreover, since \( y \) is constant, it converges to \( b \). Given that \( x \) converges to \( a \), and that \( x_n \geq y_n \) for every \( n \), it follows by the order preservation property of limits that \( a \geq b \). This completes the proof.
