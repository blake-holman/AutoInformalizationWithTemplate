We will show that if for every natural number \( n \), the value \( x(n) \) is greater than or equal to a real number \( b \), then the limit \( a \) of the sequence \( x \) satisfies \( a \geq b \). To this end, define a constant sequence \( y \) by setting \( y(n) = b \) for all natural numbers \( n \). Clearly, for every \( n \), we have \( y(n) \leq x(n) \) since \( x(n) \geq b \) by assumption. Furthermore, the sequence \( y \), being constant, converges to \( b \). Since \( y(n) \leq x(n) \) for all \( n \), and the sequences \( y \) and \( x \) converge to \( b \) and \( a \) respectively, it follows by the order preservation of limits that \( a \geq b \), completing the proof.
