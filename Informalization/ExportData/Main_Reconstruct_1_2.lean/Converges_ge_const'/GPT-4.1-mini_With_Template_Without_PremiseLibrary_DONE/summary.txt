We will show that if a sequence \(x\) satisfies \(x(n) \geq b\) for every natural number \(n\) and converges to a limit \(a\), then \(a \geq b\). To this end, consider the constant sequence \(y\) defined by \(y(n) = b\) for all \(n\). Clearly, for every \(n\), we have \(y(n) = b \leq x(n)\) by the assumption on \(x\). Moreover, the sequence \(y\) trivially converges to \(b\) since all its terms are equal to \(b\). By applying the comparison theorem for limits—which states that if one sequence converges to a limit and is always less than or equal to another sequence converging to a limit, then the limit of the first is less than or equal to that of the second—we conclude that \(b \leq a\). This completes the proof.
