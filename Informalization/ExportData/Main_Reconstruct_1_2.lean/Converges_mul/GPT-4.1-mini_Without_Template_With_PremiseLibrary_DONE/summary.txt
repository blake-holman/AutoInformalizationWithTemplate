We will show that if two sequences \((x_n)\) and \((y_n)\) converge to limits \(a\) and \(b\), respectively, then the sequence \((x_n y_n)\) converges to the product \(a b\). Since \((x_n)\) converges, it is bounded; hence, there exists a positive real number \(m_1\) such that \(|x_n| \leq m_1\) for all \(n\). Similarly, the convergence of \((y_n)\) implies it is bounded by some positive \(m_2\) with \(|y_n| \leq m_2\). To ensure positivity, define \(M = \max(m_1, m_2, |b|)\), which is positive since it is the maximum of positive numbers and the absolute value of \(b\).

Given an arbitrary \(\varepsilon > 0\), consider \(\frac{\varepsilon}{2M} > 0\). By the convergence of \((x_n)\) to \(a\), there exists an index \(n_1\) such that for all \(n \geq n_1\), \(|x_n - a| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2M}\). Similarly, by convergence of \((y_n)\) to \(b\), there exists an index \(n_2\) such that for all \(n \geq n_2\), \(|y_n - b| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2M}\). Set \(n_0 = \max(n_1, n_2)\) so both inequalities hold simultaneously for all \(n \geq n_0\).

For \(n \geq n_0\), we have
\[
|x_n y_n - a b| = |x_n y_n - x_n b + x_n b - a b| \leq |x_n||y_n - b| + |x_n - a||b|.
\]
Using the bounds on \(|x_n|\) and \(|b|\), this is at most
\[
M |y_n - b| + M |x_n - a| = M(|y_n - b| + |x_n - a|).
\]
Applying the inequalities for \(|x_n - a|\) and \(|y_n - b|\), we obtain
\[
|x_n y_n - a b| < M \left( \frac{\varepsilon}{2M} + \frac{\varepsilon}{2M} \right) = \varepsilon,
\]
which implies that \((x_n y_n)\) converges to \(a b\).

Hence, the product of two convergent sequences converges to the product of their limits. This completes the proof.
