We will show that if the sequences \( x = (x_n) \) and \( y = (y_n) \) converge to limits \( a \) and \( b \) respectively, then the sequence formed by their termwise product converges to the product \( a \times b \).

First, we establish that both sequences are bounded. Since \( x \) converges to \( a \), there exists a positive real number \( m_1 \) that bounds the absolute values of \( x_n \) for all \( n \). Similarly, because \( y \) converges to \( b \), there exists a positive real number \( m_2 \) bounding the absolute values of \( y_n \). We then define \( M \) as the maximum of \( m_1 \), \( m_2 \), and the absolute value of \( b \). By properties of the maximum function, \( M \) is positive and greater than or equal to each of these values.

Given any \( \varepsilon > 0 \), we demonstrate the existence of an index \( n_0 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_0 \), the difference between \( x_n y_n \) and \( a b \) is less than \( \varepsilon \). To ensure this, observe that since \( M > 0 \), the quantity \( \frac{\varepsilon}{2M} \) is positive. By the convergence of \( x_n \to a \) and \( y_n \to b \), there exist indices \( n_1 \) and \( n_2 \) such that for all \( n \geq n_1 \), \( |x_n - a| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2M} \), and for all \( n \geq n_2 \), \( |y_n - b| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2M} \). Setting \( n_0 = \max(n_1, n_2) \), we ensure these inequalities hold simultaneously for all \( n \geq n_0 \).

For \( n \geq n_0 \), we compute
\[
|x_n y_n - a b| = |x_n y_n - x_n b + x_n b - a b| \leq |x_n| |y_n - b| + |x_n - a| |b|.
\]
Using the bounds, \( |x_n| \leq M \) and \( |b| \leq M \), we have
\[
|x_n y_n - a b| \leq M |y_n - b| + M |x_n - a| < M \cdot \frac{\varepsilon}{2M} + M \cdot \frac{\varepsilon}{2M} = \frac{\varepsilon}{2} + \frac{\varepsilon}{2} = \varepsilon.
\]

Thus, the product sequence \( (x_n y_n) \) converges to \( a b \). This demonstrates that the product of two convergent sequences converges to the product of their limits, completing the proof.
