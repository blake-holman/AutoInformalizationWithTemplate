We will show that if two sequences \((x_n)\) and \((y_n)\) converge to limits \(a\) and \(b\) respectively, then the sequence formed by their termwise products \((x_n y_n)\) converges to the product \(ab\). Since \(x_n \to a\) and \(y_n \to b\), both sequences are bounded, meaning there exist positive real numbers \(m_1\) and \(m_2\) such that \(|x_n| \leq m_1\) and \(|y_n| \leq m_2\) for all natural numbers \(n\). To ensure convenience in our estimates, define \(M = \max(m_1, m_2, |b|)\), which is a positive constant bounding \(|x_n|\), \(|y_n|\), and \(|b|\).

Given any \(\varepsilon > 0\), set \(\delta = \frac{\varepsilon}{2M}\), which is positive since \(M > 0\). Because \((x_n)\) and \((y_n)\) converge, there exist indices \(n_1\) and \(n_2\) such that for all \(n \geq n_1\), \(|x_n - a| < \delta\), and for all \(n \geq n_2\), \(|y_n - b| < \delta\). Letting \(n_0 = \max(n_1, n_2)\), we have for all \(n \geq n_0\) that both inequalities hold simultaneously.

Now, for \(n \geq n_0\), consider the absolute difference:
\[
|x_n y_n - ab| = |(x_n y_n - x_n b) + (x_n b - a b)| \leq |x_n|\cdot|y_n - b| + |x_n - a|\cdot|b|.
\]
Using the bounds \(|x_n| \leq M\) and \(|b| \leq M\), this is further bounded by
\[
M |y_n - b| + M |x_n - a| < M \delta + M \delta = 2 M \delta = \varepsilon.
\]
Since \(\varepsilon > 0\) was arbitrary, this shows that the sequence \((x_n y_n)\) converges to \(ab\), completing the proof.
