We will show that if two sequences \((x_n)\) and \((y_n)\) converge to limits \(a\) and \(b\) respectively, then the sequence of their termwise products \((x_n y_n)\) converges to the product \(ab\). First, since \((x_n)\) converges to \(a\), there exists a positive real number \(m_1\) such that \(|x_n| \leq m_1\) for all natural numbers \(n\). Similarly, since \((y_n)\) converges to \(b\), there exists a positive real number \(m_2\) such that \(|y_n| \leq m_2\) for all \(n\). Defining \(M\) as the maximum of \(m_1\), \(m_2\), and \(|b|\), we note that \(M\) is positive and bounds \(|x_n|\) and \(|b|\). Given any \(\varepsilon > 0\), the convergence of \((x_n)\) and \((y_n)\) ensures the existence of indices \(n_1\) and \(n_2\) such that for all \(n \geq \max(n_1, n_2) = n_0\), we have \(|x_n - a| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2M}\) and \(|y_n - b| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2M}\). Using the triangle inequality, we write 
\[
|x_n y_n - ab| = |x_n y_n - x_n b + x_n b - a b| \leq |x_n||y_n - b| + |x_n - a||b|.
\]
Each term on the right is bounded by \(M\) times the respective small difference, so the sum is less than \(\varepsilon\). Therefore, \(\lim_{n \to \infty} x_n y_n = ab\), completing the proof.
