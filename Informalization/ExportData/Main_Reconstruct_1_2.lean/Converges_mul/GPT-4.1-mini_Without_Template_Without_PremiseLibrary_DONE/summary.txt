We will show that the product of two convergent sequences \( (x_n) \) and \( (y_n) \) converges to the product of their respective limits. Let \( \lim_{n \to \infty} x_n = a \) and \( \lim_{n \to \infty} y_n = b \). Since both sequences converge, they are bounded; thus, there exist positive real numbers \( m_1 \) and \( m_2 \) such that for every \( n \), \( |x_n| \leq m_1 \) and \( |y_n| \leq m_2 \). Define \( M \) as the maximum among \( m_1 \), \( m_2 \), and \( |b| \), ensuring \( M > 0 \).

Given any \( \varepsilon > 0 \), by the convergence of \( (x_n) \) and \( (y_n) \), there exist natural numbers \( n_1 \) and \( n_2 \) so that for all \( n \geq n_1 \), \( |x_n - a| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2M} \), and for all \( n \geq n_2 \), \( |y_n - b| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2M} \). Setting \( n_0 = \max(n_1, n_2) \), for every \( n \geq n_0 \), we analyze the difference:

\[
|x_n y_n - ab| = |x_n y_n - x_n b + x_n b - a b| \leq |x_n||y_n - b| + |x_n - a||b|.
\]

Since \( |x_n| \leq M \) and \( |b| \leq M \), this is bounded above by

\[
M |y_n - b| + M |x_n - a| = M (|y_n - b| + |x_n - a|).
\]

Using the earlier inequalities,

\[
|x_n y_n - ab| < M \left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2M} + \frac{\varepsilon}{2M}\right) = \varepsilon.
\]

This shows that for all \( n \geq n_0 \), \( |x_n y_n - ab| < \varepsilon \), proving that \( (x_n y_n) \) converges to \( ab \). By the uniqueness of limits, the limit of the product sequence equals the product of the limits, completing the proof.
